US inflation (lead)
Fact

Hotter October prices lock in a December rise

What happened

The Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) said on Tuesday that the consumer price index (CPI) rose 0.4 percent in October after 0.6 percent in September, against a Reuters poll forecast of 0.3 percent. Core CPI, which excludes food and energy, rose 0.3 percent for a third month, against 0.2 percent expected. Energy rose only 0.7 percent after 5.2 percent, but food rose 0.5 percent, shelter 0.3 percent, car insurance 0.7 percent and public transport 2.0 percent. There is no official annual rate because October 2025 prices were not collected during last year’s shutdown.

Context (what it sits on)

Hours before the data, Fed Governor Christopher Waller said a core reading of 0.3 percent or more would justify considering a half-point rise at the 8–9 December meeting, and Dallas Fed President Lorie Logan said the Fed may need to move faster. Vice Chair for Supervision Michelle Bowman sounded less hurried. Reuters said futures priced about a 15 percent chance of a half-point rise by mid-morning, easing to about 10 percent by late afternoon, with traders now expecting three quarter-point rises by mid-2027. Investing.com, citing the CME FedWatch tool, put the odds of any December rise at 88 percent. The policy rate is 3.75–4.00 percent after September’s rise, so December would be a second.

Forward (months–years)

Fri 13 Nov 14:30 CET: producer prices. Late November: the personal consumption expenditures (PCE) index, the Fed’s preferred measure; one economist estimates core PCE at about 0.34 percent for October. Tue 8–Wed 9 Dec: Fed decision. Market odds are not a Fed decision.

AI companies
Fact

CoreWeave beats, Anthropic sets its price range

What happened

CoreWeave, a cloud company that rents Nvidia chips to AI developers, reported third-quarter revenue of $3.66 billion, up 168 percent from a year earlier and above the $3.55 billion analysts expected. Its order backlog rose to $132.5 billion from $104 billion in June. It raised its 2026 revenue forecast to $13.0–13.3 billion and capital spending to $37–39 billion, and said it expects about $9 billion of customer prepayments in the next three months. Shares rose about 5–8 percent after hours. Anthropic, maker of the Claude models, launched its roadshow on Tuesday for 825 million shares at $70–78 each, to list on Nasdaq as “ANT”.

Context (what it sits on)

CoreWeave has about $46.9 billion of debt and its shares fell more than 40 percent in the past month on fears about AI borrowing. It said it will not issue unsecured debt at current yields, expects to raise less than $5 billion more through 2027, and targets adjusted profitability in the first half of 2028 (Reuters). Quarterly capital spending of $14.5 billion was above its own guidance. TechCrunch calculates the top of Anthropic’s range implies about $1.404 trillion fully diluted, roughly $64 billion raised at the top; Reuters says the target is lower than some earlier estimates.

Forward (months–years)

Anthropic pricing date not announced (one report says as soon as Thu 19 Nov, claim). Watch CoreWeave bond and default-insurance prices, which were near distressed levels before the results.

Israel
Fact

New parliament, no government yet

What happened

Israel’s 26th Knesset was sworn in on Tuesday after the 27 October election left no majority (Reuters). Benjamin Netanyahu’s right-wing and religious bloc holds 53 of 120 seats and stays caretaker prime minister. Gadi Eisenkot, a former military chief whose centre-right Yashar party and partners hold 48 seats, was asked by President Isaac Herzog to form a government and has until 6 December, extendable to 20 December.

Context (what it sits on)

Arab-led parties, with 19 seats, oppose Netanyahu but are not formal partners, and ultra-Orthodox parties say they will not join Eisenkot. His bloc tabled bills for a state inquiry into the 7 October 2023 attacks, term limits for prime ministers and repeal of the judicial overhaul. Netanyahu called for a unity government and has alleged voter fraud without evidence; election officials see no fraud that could change the outcome.

Forward (months–years)

Sun 6 Dec: Eisenkot’s first deadline. Watch a vote to replace the Likud speaker and any deal with ultra-Orthodox parties. A repeat election is possible if all fail.

Mixed

What happened

Google DeepMind made Gemini 4 Carbon generally available on 10 November, two weeks after giving it to cybersecurity defenders. Google calls it its strongest model, built for long multi-step coding, open-ended research and finding software flaws. It costs $2 per million input tokens (units of text) and $10 per million output tokens until 30 November, then $4 and $20. Science and technology
Fact

A Webb “digital twin” reveals a hidden planet

What happened

Researchers at Caltech, Northwestern, MIT and the Space Telescope Science Institute built a detailed computer model of the James Webb Space Telescope’s infrared camera and used it to subtract a star’s glare from images. The method, called FLIP, made faint planets two to 15 times easier to detect in tests and uncovered a previously hidden planet in existing data. The paper appeared in Nature (online 4 November); Caltech publicised it on 10 November.

Context (what it sits on)

Rather than new hardware, the approach uses machine-learning optimisation tools to model tiny optical flaws. It could be applied to archived Webb images, NASA’s Roman Space Telescope and the planned Habitable Worlds Observatory, which aims to photograph Earth-like planets about 10 billion times fainter than their stars.

Forward (months–years)

Watch for searches of the Webb archive with the method.
