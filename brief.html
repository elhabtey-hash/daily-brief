Asia markets
Fact

Kospi falls about 8 percent and trading halts as chip stocks crash across Asia

What happened

On Thursday 15 October South Korea's Kospi fell 8.13 percent to 5,515.76 by 13:17 Seoul time after opening 5.59 percent lower, triggering a market-wide 20-minute circuit breaker at 12:38, and the tech-heavy Kosdaq also halted, the first day since 29 July both did so (Korea Herald). SK Hynix fell 11.35 percent and Samsung Electronics 10.13 percent at that point. In Tokyo the Nikkei 225 closed down about 4.4 percent at 64,778.92, with SoftBank down roughly 8 to 10 percent, Advantest nearly 14 percent and Tokyo Electron 6.5 percent (CNBC, Nikkei, Yahoo Finance data). TSMC in Taiwan fell 1.8 percent in early trade (CNBC). Drivers cited are Wednesday's hot US inflation, bets on further Fed rate rises, AI financing worries after CoreWeave's bond slide, and Chinese memory maker CXMT's claimed chip breakthrough.

Context (what it sits on)

Broadcom chief Hock Tan said on Monday that many AI investors behave like casino gamblers and that financing could become the biggest constraint on the AI buildout, which began this week's sell-off. Korean chipmakers have a heavy weight in the index, so their falls move the whole market. One analyst quoted by CNBC calls it a financing question, not a demand question.

Forward (months–years)

Watch the Kospi close, Friday's Asian open and whether TSMC's outlook today and Meta's results on Wednesday 21 October reassure investors on AI spending.

US inflation
Fact

September consumer prices rise 3.7 percent from a year earlier, slightly above forecasts, and Wall Street falls

What happened

The US Bureau of Labor Statistics reported on Wednesday 14 October that consumer prices rose 0.6 percent in September after 0.4 percent in August, leaving the annual rate at 3.7 percent against 3.4 percent in August and a 3.6 percent forecast. Core prices excluding food and energy rose 0.3 percent on the month and 2.6 percent on the year, against forecasts of 0.2 and 2.5 percent (BLS, CNBC, Reuters). Gasoline rose 9.1 percent and made up over half the monthly gain, and energy prices are up 20.4 percent on the year. The S&P 500 fell 1.11 percent to 7,718.94, the Nasdaq 1.88 percent to 26,692.87 and the Dow 0.09 percent to 51,621.48, its worst day since July (AP). The 10-year Treasury yield ended near 5.19 percent and the 30-year touched 5.63 percent before easing.

Context (what it sits on)

The Fed's target range is 3.75 to 4.00 percent. Officials have said another rise is likely by year-end, but recent comments suggested October was unlikely. Pricing for an October rise jumped on the data and then eased back, with estimates of roughly one in three in Reuters and CNBC reports (Claim on exact odds).

Forward (months–years)

The Fed meets on Tuesday 27 and Wednesday 28 October, with the decision on Wednesday 28 October at 14:00 New York time. US midterm elections are on Tuesday 3 November.

Energy
Fact

Wednesday settles: Brent December 106.06 dollars (+0.37), WTI November 95.09 dollars (+0.95)

What happened

Wednesday 14 October settlements. Brent for December delivery, the front month, settled at 106.06 dollars a barrel, up 37 cents or 0.35 percent from Tuesday's December settle of 105.69. West Texas Intermediate (WTI) for November delivery, the front month, settled at 95.09 dollars, up 95 cents or 1.01 percent from Tuesday's November 94.14, its highest close in three weeks (Xinhua, Dow Jones/Morningstar, Newsquawk, PFL Petroleum). Both changes tie exactly. Support came from the Houthi attack on an Aramco building and Iranian attacks on shipping. Thursday Asian quotes are intraday and not used. The US offshore regulator said at 11:00 Central time on Wednesday that 9.92 percent of Gulf of Mexico oil output (202,371 barrels a day) and 11.07 percent of gas was still shut in, down from 16.13 percent of oil on Tuesday, and that production had recovered to nearly 90 percent of normal, so it will likely issue no more daily storm updates.

Context (what it sits on)

Crude shipments through Hormuz averaged 8.5 million barrels a day in the week to 12 October, nearly 40 percent below normal (Kpler via CNBC). Gulf producers other than Iran exported over 81 percent of prewar volumes in September (PFL). A US-Russia diesel deal is a counterweight, but volumes and timing are unclear.

Forward (months–years)

WTI November expires on Tuesday 20 October and Brent December on Friday 30 October. OPEC Plus meets on Sunday 1 November.

Iran
Mixed

Container ship GFS Pride sinks in Hormuz. Iran says a mine did it, security firm doubts that

What happened

On Wednesday 14 October two Singapore-registered container ships were hit by unidentified projectiles in the Strait of Hormuz at about 16:40 to 17:00 GMT. The GFS Pride caught fire and sank, with all 21 crew evacuated and no injuries, and the GFS Giselle reached anchorage with hull damage (Singapore's maritime authority via CNBC and BBC). The Revolutionary Guard claimed via Tasnim that a ship hit a naval mine on an unauthorised route. The security firm Marisks says there is no independent evidence of a mine and calls the claim part of a psychological campaign. The International Maritime Organization counts it as the third commercial ship sunk in the war, and more than 20 vessels have been attacked this month, with two sailors missing and over a dozen injured.

Context (what it sits on)

Iranian officials told Reuters last week they changed tactics to hit more ships before the US midterms on Tuesday 3 November. Trump has said the US will not strike Iran before then and keeps a naval blockade of Iranian ports, and Iran says the US reply on reopening Hormuz is insufficient.

Forward (months–years)

Watch the daily UKMTO (UK Maritime Trade Operations) count, war-risk insurance, Hormuz flows and any change in US naval escorts.

Saudi Arabia
Fact

Houthis vow more attacks on Saudi airports and oil sites after Aramco building hit

What happened

The Saudi energy ministry said a Houthi ballistic missile hit an Aramco administrative building in Dhahran on Tuesday 13 October, causing a limited fire, and debris from intercepted missiles wounded four people in Riyadh (AP, AFP). On Wednesday 14 October the Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted a further missile launched towards Riyadh and struck Houthi-linked fuel and energy sites near Sanaa (Al Jazeera). Context (what it sits on)

Saturday's missile strike on Riyadh airport killed 12. Hitting Aramco property moves the Yemen war from airports towards the oil infrastructure that markets fear most. The US supplies intelligence and refuelling but has not struck Yemen.

Forward (months–years)

Watch for attacks on production or export sites, any US decision to join strikes, and Red Sea shipping.

Ukraine
Mixed

Russia hits 26 power facilities overnight despite Trump's energy ceasefire. Starshield limits row grows

What happened

Zelensky said Russia's overnight attack into Wednesday 14 October damaged 26 power-system facilities and four oil and gas sites, leaving at least 600,000 people without power in the Kyiv and Kharkiv regions, and called a ballistic strike on energy infrastructure 'entirely deliberate' (CNBC). A later overnight strike on Kyiv hit the Darnytska heating and power plant, killing one and injuring more than 20 (Euronews). Russia says it shot down 900 Ukrainian drones, 180 near Moscow, and accused Ukraine of hitting two refineries in breach of the deal, which Kyiv calls fake. Ukraine also says speed limits on Starshield military terminals disrupted its operations, and twice this week it received no warning of ballistic launches. Musk said Starshield limits are decided by the US military and 'ultimately' Trump, and Trump denied threatening to cut intelligence sharing (Euronews).

Context (what it sits on)

Tuesday's overnight lull in energy strikes did not hold. Moscow says the ceasefire lacks defined terms. The truce is tied to a US sanctions waiver for Russian diesel, and Italy's Meloni called that deal a legitimisation of Moscow.

Forward (months–years)

Watch whether further energy strikes follow, the US response on Starshield limits and EU leaders' meeting with Zelensky on Friday 16 October.

European Union
Mixed

EU leaders meet in Brussels: energy prices, budget, Ukraine, enlargement. No conclusions yet

What happened

The European Council meets on Thursday 15 and Friday 16 October. Thursday's session starts at 10:00 with a press conference around 19:30, and Friday includes a meeting with Zelensky and a press conference around 14:30 (Council media advisory). AI companies
Mixed

TSMC posts record third-quarter revenue and profit on AI chip demand

What happened

TSMC reported on Thursday 15 October third-quarter revenue of NT$1.49 trillion (about 46.4 to 46.7 billion US dollars), up 50 percent on a year earlier and above its guidance of 44.6 to 45.8 billion dollars and the NT$1.47 trillion analysts expected. Net profit was NT$763.1 billion, up 68.7 percent, a sixth straight record, against NT$746.3 billion expected. Operating margin was 58.7 percent, above its 56 to 58 percent guidance. High-performance computing was 68 percent of revenue and 3-nanometre chips the largest technology at 33 percent. Free cash flow was NT$476.6 billion and the board approved a NT$7.00 dividend (CNBC, Reuters, TSMC investor page).

Context (what it sits on)

TSMC makes most of the world's advanced AI chips for Nvidia and others. Its July plan was 60 to 64 billion dollars of 2026 capital spending and 'slightly above 40 percent' revenue growth. ASML said on Wednesday its 2027 to 2028 supply will not fully meet demand. Gross margin, fourth-quarter guidance and any change to spending were not yet in the reports found at filing time (to be confirmed on TSMC's 08:00 Paris call).

Forward (months–years)

Watch the fourth-quarter guidance, 2027 capital spending and advanced packaging comments, and how Asian chip shares react.

AI financing
Claim

CoreWeave bond yields hit about 15 percent. Anthropic tells investors revenue is 16.8 billion dollars

What happened

CoreWeave's 2032 unsecured bonds traded below 80 cents, a yield near 15 percent, on Wednesday 14 October and its shares fell about 15 to 16 percent as default-protection costs rose for it and peers (CNBC, Reuters, MarketWatch). Its chief executive sent staff a memo citing a strong backlog. Separately, Anthropic told prospective investors at a San Francisco briefing on Wednesday that third-quarter revenue rose 45 percent to 16.8 billion dollars, up from 11.6 billion in the second quarter and 4.7 billion in the first, and that it was profitable for a second straight quarter, with a listing expected before year-end (BBC and Bloomberg, unconfirmed by Anthropic). OpenAI's annualised revenue was recently put at about 50 billion dollars, 20 billion below earlier reports, because of cloud-partner accounting.

Context (what it sits on)

Rising interest rates raise the cost of debt-funded data centres, and CoreWeave is a bellwether for that model. Anthropic's figures come from the company's own investor briefing as relayed by the BBC, so treat them as a company claim.

Forward (months–years)

Watch credit spreads for AI borrowers, Anthropic's listing filing and OpenAI's funding.

Ebola
Fact

Congo's Bundibugyo Ebola outbreak passes 9,100 confirmed cases

What happened

As of 13 October the Democratic Republic of the Congo had 9,144 confirmed Bundibugyo virus disease cases and 4,410 deaths, with 79 new cases and 29 deaths reported for 12 October. Ituri province has 6,734 cases and 3,117 deaths, and the outbreak spans seven provinces (WHO and Congo health ministry reports via ECDC and trackers). Uganda has reported 20 cases and two deaths, Kenya one imported case and death, and France one case.

Context (what it sits on)

Bundibugyo is a rarer Ebola species with no approved vaccine. The Ervebo vaccine, made for the Zaire species, is used only in research and compassionate settings, with about 7,861 people vaccinated, and CEPI says a pivotal trial is in final preparation.

Forward (months–years)

Watch the start of the vaccine trial, spread to new countries and daily case counts.

Ethiopia
Mixed

Ethiopian troops reach Zalambessa as Eritrea appeals to the UN

What happened

By Wednesday 14 October Ethiopian federal forces had reached Zalambessa, a Tigray border town, after Eritrean troops reportedly withdrew through it on 12 October.
