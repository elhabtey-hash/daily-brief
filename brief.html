Lead
Mixed

Pipeline shut + Hormuz hits; Brent ~$108

What happened

Monday open, Brent crude (the global oil benchmark) traded about $107.5–$108.2 a barrel and West Texas Intermediate (the US gauge) about $102–$103 — both up roughly 3% after a weekend of fresh Gulf risk. On Friday a drone strike originating in Iraq knocked out Saudi Arabia’s ~1,200-km East-West crude pipeline (the main Hormuz bypass); Riyadh shut the line. Reuters, citing buyers, said stocks at Yanbu may cover only about 5–7 days of exports if the line stays shut, putting up to ~4% of global supply at risk on top of Hormuz disruption (Claim on exact duration). Over the weekend Iran-aligned Houthis reached Perim (Mayun) Island in the Bab el-Mandeb after taking Mocha; UKMTO reported a vessel struck in the Strait of Hormuz on Sunday; Iran said one killed and four wounded on an Iranian commercial vessel near Qeshm. Late Sunday Oman postponed Monday’s Iran–Gulf Hormuz meeting “in the interests of consensus.” US diesel’s AAA national average hit a record ~$6.204 a gallon as of 13 September.

Context (what it sits on)

US–Iran war since late February 2026. Quietish August had let some Mid-East flows trickle back; this weekend reversed that. Hormuz peacetime share is about one-fifth of seaborne oil. Iran says Hormuz stays closed until US demands are met.

Forward (months–years)

Dual choke (Hormuz + Bab el-Mandeb) plus a shut Saudi bypass raises lasting premium into winter and into the Federal Reserve’s 15–16 September meeting. Watch Yanbu stock draw, any partial restart, and whether Oman mediation gets a new date.

Separately, Supreme Court application 26A305 (USPS mail-ballot rule stay) is still pending Monday morning; the First Circuit denied the government’s stay; Judge Indira Talwani’s preliminary injunction blocking mandatory parts of the US Postal Service mail-ballot rule remains in force. States are already mailing ballots.

Context (what it sits on)

Forward (months–years)

Mixed

What happened

Overnight 12–13 September Ukrainian Air Force reported about 453 Russian drones and loitering munitions (~405 downed; 13 locations struck), with hundreds more through daytime 13 Sep. Fact

ECB 2.50% deposit still the living story; CRA reporting live

What happened

The European Central Bank’s 10 September hike remains the living euro-zone rates story: deposit facility 2.50%, main refinancing 2.65%, marginal lending 2.90%, all effective Wednesday 16 September. President Christine Lagarde called the move a unanimous “no-brainer,” said inflation will stay well above the 2% target for an extended period, and kept a meeting-by-meeting stance with no rate-path pre-commit. Staff see Harmonised Index of Consumer Prices averaging about 3.0% / 2.5% / 2.1% for 2026–28. Separately, the EU Cyber Resilience Act (CRA) Single Reporting Platform at ENISA went live Friday 11 September: manufacturers must file early warning within 24 hours and fuller notices on actively exploited vulnerabilities and severe incidents. Main product cybersecurity requirements still apply from 11 December 2027.

Context (what it sits on)

Second ECB hike of 2026 after Iran-war energy shocks. Next ECB decision 29 October. French OAT yields (see France) absorb the euro-area reset plus a France-specific premium.

Forward (months–years)
