Fact

Thu settle: Brent Dec $102.31 / WTI Nov $92.87. Fri Asia little changed. Diesel Nov $4.6420

What happened

Thursday 1 October settlements. Reuters, carried by The Star, names the month: the new front-month Brent contract for December delivery settled at 102.31 dollars a barrel, up 4.28 dollars, or 4.37 percent. Dow Jones Market Data, via Morningstar, names the month for the US benchmark: front-month West Texas Intermediate for November delivery gained 2.45 dollars, or 2.71 percent, to 92.87. That reconciles to Wednesday's November settlement of 90.42. Do not read 102.31 as a fall from Wednesday's 103.53. That 103.53 print was November Brent, the contract that expired on Wednesday. A market report (EnergyNow) put Wednesday's December Brent settlement at 98.03, and 98.03 plus 4.28 equals 102.31. That Wednesday December figure was not pulled from the exchange itself. The same Dow Jones note says front-month New York ultra-low-sulfur diesel for November delivery, which it calls the new front month, lost 4.61 cents, or 0.98 percent, to 4.6420 dollars a gallon. That daily change is inside the November contract. It is not a comparison with Wednesday's expired October diesel print of 4.9569. Early Friday, Reuters at 00:22 GMT (02:22 Paris) had Brent at 102.60, up 29 cents, and West Texas Intermediate at 93.14, up 27 cents, without naming the month. Those moves match Thursday's December Brent and November West Texas Intermediate settlements. A later report (Arabian Post, 03:50 GMT, 05:50 Paris) had Brent at 102.28, down 3 cents, and West Texas Intermediate at 92.68, down 19 cents, against those same Thursday settles. Newsquawk at 02:43 UTC labelled the contracts Brent December 2026, about flat, and West Texas Intermediate November 2026, down about 0.2 percent.

Context (what it sits on)

The United States–Iran war since 28 February still constrains the Strait of Hormuz, and refined fuel is tighter than crude. Thursday's jump came after a Reuters report that Chinese refiners had halted fuel exports, and after newspaper reports of more American forces heading to the Gulf. Households feel diesel. November is now the diesel contract to quote. A headline that sets Thursday's December Brent against Wednesday's expired November print will look like a drop of about a dollar. It is a different month, and December actually rose.

Forward (months–years)

OPEC+ meets on Sunday 4 October. People familiar with the talks told Reuters they expect November quotas to be left unchanged (Speculation). Quote Brent December that day. The September jobs report is today at 14:30 Paris and was not out at filing. A European discussion of diesel stockpiles is also today and had not produced a decision at filing. Goldman Sachs's estimate of Gulf flows, including ships with transponders off, at 23.3 million barrels a day was already in Thursday's brief and was not treated as a new tally.

China
Claim

Refiners reported to have halted October fuel exports beyond Hong Kong and Macau. No ministry text found

What happened

Reuters reported on Thursday that Chinese refiners had suspended exports of oil products beyond Hong Kong and Macau until further notice, citing four people familiar with the matter. The same account said PetroChina had cancelled some planned gasoline and jet-fuel cargoes. No Chinese government communiqué confirming a formal ban was found this morning, so the halt is a sourced news report, not a statute (Claim). The Star's Reuters copy said the report helped reverse an early 1 percent drop in oil. Mainland markets stay shut through Wednesday 7 October for the national holiday. Hong Kong, shut on Thursday, was trading again on Friday. A clean Friday closing level for Hong Kong was not taken.

Context (what it sits on)

China restricted fuel exports after the war began, eased that in July, and had been releasing diesel, gasoline and other products month by month. A holiday week with no fresh export licences would tighten an already short global fuel market even if crude loadings from the Gulf have recovered. It is a product story, not proof that crude supply has collapsed.

Forward (months–years)

Watch whether Beijing confirms the halt after the holiday, and whether November diesel, now the front month at 4.6420 dollars a gallon, keeps the gain. A restart of Chinese product exports would lean against Thursday's crude jump (Speculation until a licence round is published).

Ethiopia
Mixed

Embassy in Asmara to close. Ten Eritrean diplomats ordered out. Blasts reported in Addis Ababa

What happened

Al Jazeera, citing the Ethiopian foreign ministry, reported on Thursday that Ethiopia will close its embassy in Asmara and has ordered ten Eritrean diplomats in Addis Ababa to leave within 48 hours. The ministry accused Eritrea of hostile acts and of a threat to national security. Eritrea has denied backing the armed opposition. Three diplomatic sources told Reuters that explosions were heard overnight in Addis Ababa, hours after the government banned drone flights over the capital. A source linked to the Ethiopian Peoples' Forces Alliance for Survival told Reuters the alliance had carried out drone strikes in the capital, including against military headquarters. That claim is one source on the alliance side and is not independently confirmed (Claim inside this row). In the north, Al Jazeera's correspondent said on Wednesday that government forces had pushed to within about 40 kilometres of Mekelle and that thousands of civilians had left the city. Afar officials told Reuters they had retaken Erebti and Abaala. Al Jazeera said that claim was unconfirmed and that more than 80,000 residents were stuck in the area.

Context (what it sits on)

Thursday morning's brief had the Alamata casualty account: one medic told the BBC at least 52 civilians were killed, and Médecins Sans Frontières said more than 700 wounded had reached hospitals it supports. Those figures are not repeated here as a new count. What is new is the diplomatic break with Eritrea and the reported strikes on the capital. The Djibouti corridor, Ethiopia's sea access, runs through Afar. A confirmed closure of that road was not found.

Forward (months–years)

Watch whether the ten diplomats actually leave, whether a second source confirms strikes in Addis Ababa, and whether the road to Djibouti is interrupted. A wider war with Eritrea would sit next to the Hormuz shock as a second trade-route risk (Speculation until a corridor closure or a cross-border clash is confirmed).

France
Fact

2027 budget bills presented and filed. Deficit target 5%. Larcher re-elected with 203 votes

What happened

The Élysée record of Thursday's cabinet says the government presented the 2027 budget bill and the social-security financing bill. The National Assembly registered the budget bill the same day as number 3210. The public-deficit target is 5.0 percent of gross domestic product in 2027, against a revised 5.4 percent in 2026. New measures are 43 billion euros. Counting steps already in force that will ramp up, the effort is 54 billion euros. Growth is put at 0.5 percent in 2026 and 1.0 percent in 2027. Public debt is put at 119.3 percent of output in 2026. Interest is put at 79.2 billion euros in 2026 and 91.2 billion in 2027. The social-security deficit is aimed at 12.7 billion euros in 2027. Health-insurance spending (Ondam) is capped at a 2.0 percent rise. Local-government receipts may rise by 5 billion euros, described as the pace of inflation, with a progressive contribution that spares small and financially fragile communes and overseas communes. The income-tax scale is to be revalued. The corporate-tax surtax on large firms is to be cut by a third. Pensions are not cut, and small pensions up to 1,260 euros a month keep a full revaluation. The same afternoon the Senate re-elected Gérard Larcher on the first round with 203 votes, until 2029. BFM, with Agence France-Presse, said three candidates from the minority left stood against him, about forty senators cast blank ballots, and the National Rally and its allies abstained. Their group leader, Edwige Diaz, announced the name Union nationale pour les territoires. A total of ballots cast was not in the story that was opened, so 203 is the votes for Larcher, not a share of a stated total.

Context (what it sits on)

Lecornu leads a minority government seven months before the April 2027 presidential election. Thursday morning's brief had the 54 billion figure and the 5 percent target as the plan going into cabinet, and the Senate vote as still ahead. Both now have an outcome. The right and centre kept their Senate majority on Sunday. The National Rally group still has to file its official list.

Forward (months–years)

Group lists are due by Monday 5 October. National Assembly examination of the budget is still scheduled from Tuesday 13 October. The text says many measures are meant to be reversible by the next presidency. The government may again need special procedures after the Assembly debate. No confidence vote was held on Thursday.

Ukraine
Fact

Night of 1–2 Oct: 108 drones, 47 jet-powered. 88 destroyed or jammed by 08:00 Kyiv. One dead in a Kyiv tower

What happened

Ukraine's air force said Russia launched 108 drones on the night of 1 to 2 October, a mix of Shahed attack drones of which 47 were jet-powered, plus Gerbera drones and Parodiya decoys, from Bryansk, Primorsko-Akhtarsk, occupied Donetsk and occupied Crimea (RBC-Ukraine, citing the air force). By 08:00 Kyiv time, air defence had destroyed or jammed 88. Hits were recorded at 11 locations and debris at five. Several drones were still airborne, so the count is not final. In Kyiv, a 25-storey block in the Darnytskyi district was damaged, one person was killed and two were wounded. The South Bridge was struck again, after two hits on 1 October. Debris in Holosiivskyi damaged windows and cars, with no casualties reported there. This is a new night. It is not Thursday morning's raid of 107 drones, of which 63 were jet-powered.

Context (what it sits on)

Russia has been hitting bridges, housing and economic sites as the heating season starts. Jet-powered drones are harder to stop than propeller models. Thursday's raid is a separate set of figures and should not be added to this one. A separate daytime raid on 1 October was reported in headlines and was not opened, so it is not given a number here.

Forward (months–years)

Watch the final interception and casualty count later today, and whether the South Bridge stays closed. European launchers that can reach jet drones remain the equipment question. No new pledge was found this morning.

Fact

EU energy chief: a diesel-stock release is possible. No decision. Member states would each have to agree

What happened

Energy Commissioner Dan Jørgensen told Euronews on Thursday that releasing strategic stocks is one of the tools under discussion with members of the International Energy Agency, and that the choice belongs to each country. An EU source told Euronews the Commission held a call on Thursday with several countries, including the United Kingdom, to look at scenarios. Reuters, via The Star, said two EU diplomats expected the energy task force to meet on Friday, and that American officials had told Germany and France to draw down emergency diesel or face a possible United States ban on diesel exports. That American demand is a diplomatic account, not a published order (Claim inside this row). Euronews said a release could put about 120 million extra barrels on the market. That is a scale being discussed, not a decision. No release was announced at filing. The Friday meeting had not reported an outcome.

Context (what it sits on)

The International Energy Agency said in August that observed global stocks had fallen by about 410 million barrels since the war began on 28 February. A coordinated release in March covered 400 million barrels, with European Union countries about a fifth of that, and a large part had not yet reached the market. Diesel and jet fuel are the tight products. November diesel futures, not the expired October contract, settled on Thursday at 4.6420 dollars a gallon.

Forward (months–years)

Watch whether Friday's task force, or any capital, actually announces a draw. A release would lean against fuel prices and against December Brent (Speculation until the barrels are nominated). The European Central Bank's next decision is still later in October. No new rate decision was found this morning.
