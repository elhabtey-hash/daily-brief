Chips and Asian markets (lead)
Fact

Chip sell-off spreads across Asia for a second day

What happened

Official Tuesday closes: Taiwan's main index (Taiex) fell 947.97 points, or 2.31 percent, to 40,092.11 (Taiwan Stock Exchange via Economic Daily News), after swinging by more than 1,700 points during the day; chipmaker TSMC fell 2.03 percent and dozens of AI supplier stocks hit the daily limit down. South Korea's Kospi fell 132.02 points, or 2.59 percent, to 4,968.48, its fourth straight fall and lowest in nine months (Yonhap). Japan's Nikkei 225 fell 801.59 points, or 1.34 percent, to 58,860.43 (Nikkei), with SoftBank Group down about 6 percent; the broader Topix fell 1.57 percent.

Context (what it sits on)

These markets are dominated by companies that supply Nvidia and other AI chip buyers. Monday's US session set the tone: the Philadelphia semiconductor index (SOX) fell 5.28 percent after the Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) issued a rule requiring licences to export advanced AI chips to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil, and for overseas data centres that rent large amounts of computing to Chinese or Russian firms. Monday's closes in our last brief (Taiwan 41,040.08, Kospi 5,100.50, Nikkei 59,662.02) are confirmed by today's point changes.

Forward (months–years)

Nvidia's results tonight are the next test for the whole supply chain. Watch whether European chip stocks follow at the open and whether Asian authorities announce any market-calming steps (none at filing).

Chips and Asian markets (lead)
Fact

New US export rule for AI chips

What happened

The Bureau of Industry and Security issued an interim final rule on Monday 16 November, as reported by NBC, Politico and Fortune. It requires licences to sell advanced AI chips to five countries seen as diversion risks, with an exemption for buyers headquartered in the US, and covers data centres anywhere that remotely supply large amounts of computing to companies based in China, Russia and other restricted countries (Country Group D:5) or Macau. It formally cancels the Biden-era AI Diffusion Rule, which had not been enforced since early 2025.

Context (what it sits on)

The rule targets the route through Southeast Asia that US officials say was used to move Nvidia chips into China, the issue behind the Megaspeed inquiry and Jensen Huang's resignation. Existing data centres reportedly get 90 days to obtain licences. The Federal Register text was not read for this brief.

Forward (months–years)

The rule takes effect on publication, with comments due 60 days later. Watch the reaction from Singapore, Malaysia and Brazil, and what Nvidia says tonight about lost sales.

Nvidia
Mixed

Results tonight, the first under interim chief Kress

What happened

Nvidia reports results for its third fiscal quarter (ended 25 October) tonight at about 22:20 CET, with a call at 23:00 CET; the timing was reconfirmed in its 12 November release. These results are pending at filing. Analyst consensus is about $108.7 to $109.2 billion in revenue and $2.47 to $2.48 in earnings per share, against company guidance of $108 billion plus or minus 2 percent and a gross margin of about 74 percent. For next quarter analysts expect about $125 billion.

Context (what it sits on)

This is the first report since the board accepted Jensen Huang's resignation (Form 8-K filed 12 November, effective 11 November); finance chief Colette Kress is interim chief executive and still chief financial officer, and Harvey Jones is the first independent board chair. NVDA closed Monday at $156.14, down 4.12 percent and about 35 percent below its 6 October close. Options imply a move of roughly 7 to 10 percent either way (a priced range, not a forecast).

Forward (months–years)

Key questions: next-quarter guidance, margins, sales to Southeast Asia under the new rule, the Rubin chip ramp and anything Kress says on the chief executive search. Staff meeting Wed 18 Nov 18:00 CET (claim, from CNBC). No new 8-K or insider filing since 12 November.

Fact

What happened

Mixed

Oil falls more than 3 percent as Gulf exports recover

What happened

Monday 16 November settles (Reuters, Xinhua, exchange data): January Brent fell $3.40, or 3.55 percent, to $92.44 a barrel; December WTI (West Texas Intermediate, the US benchmark) fell $2.84, or 3.21 percent, to $85.72. Production at Iraq's Khor Mor gas field remains halted after a suspected drone attack last week.

Forward (months–years)

Europe
Fact

EU adds about 145 sanctions listings

What happened

At the Foreign Affairs Council in Brussels on Monday, ministers listed 113 people and entities to cut Russia's war financing, including refineries, banks, e-commerce and Gulf and East Asian firms that support Russia's shadow tanker fleet, plus 30 people and two entities for disinformation and cyber activity (Council of the EU). Context (what it sits on)

Several ministers said talks on Europe's future security with Russia would be premature before a ceasefire, a response to US envoy Kushner's push for Europe-Russia talks. These listings are separate from the planned 22nd sanctions package.

Forward (months–years)

The EU-UK summit in Brussels is on Fri 20 Nov. Watch for a Commission proposal on the 22nd package.

Palantir
Fact

What happened

Health minister James Frith told the House of Commons on Monday 16 November (Hansard) that the current NHS Federated Data Platform (FDP) contract, run by Palantir, will remain for two years and end in March 2029. Market engagement on a successor starts in January, formal procurement in the second half of 2027, and the process should take about 18 months. He called it a bridge, not the destination.

He did not say whether Palantir may bid again, and did not adopt a call to bar it. Palantir said exit arrangements are clear (per the minister); a company spokesperson called it good news for patients (claim, Politico). Palantir closed Monday at $160.39, down 4.52 percent, with no confirmed company-specific cause. A 13G/A filed under Palantir's name is its own 8.5 percent stake in Surf Air Mobility, not a new Palantir holder.

Forward (months–years)

Procurement terms are due in the coming weeks. Senate Homeland Security committee nominations session Thu 19 Nov. Possible Nasdaq-100 reshuffle announcement around Fri 11 Dec.

COP31
Mixed

Draft deal calls for leaving fossil fuels

What happened

The presidency of the UN climate summit (COP31) in Antalya, Türkiye, released its first draft cover decision on Monday. Per DW, S&P Global and Arab News, it calls for an immediate transition away from fossil fuels, a tripling of adaptation finance for developing countries to at least $120 billion, and puts electrification targets and carbon removal at the centre.

Context (what it sits on)

The summit president says the text aims to draw out where countries agree and disagree. The fossil fuel wording is stronger than at past summits and will face pushback from oil producers. Ministers arrive for the high-level segment starting today.

Forward (months–years)

Watch the next draft later this week and whether the 1.5 degree benchmark survives.

France
Mixed

Senate rejects pension freeze; Le Pen on Ukraine

What happened

The Senate unanimously rejected on Monday 16 November the government's plan to stop indexing about two-thirds of pensions to inflation, a measure worth about 5.5 billion euros in the 2027 social security budget (franceinfo). BFM reports a compromise in which the 10 percent tax allowance for pensioners would be removed instead (claim, not yet voted). Right and centre senators symbolically reinstated the 2023 rise in the pension age.

Context (what it sits on)

Marine Le Pen said on Monday that France would keep giving Ukraine material and humanitarian aid if she is elected next spring and would honour the 90 billion euro EU loan (Reuters). The main 2027 state budget, rejected by the National Assembly, is also in the Senate.

Forward (months–years)

Watch the Senate vote on the full social security budget and whether the government forces the budgets through or uses decrees after 9 December.
