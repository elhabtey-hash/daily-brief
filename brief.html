Oil closes above $100; Iran widens zone claim

What happened

Brent crude (the global oil benchmark) settled Wednesday at about $101.21 a barrel — the first close above $100 since late May — after the biggest recent wave of US–Iran shipping strikes. Early Thursday futures traded about $101.80. West Texas Intermediate (the US crude gauge) settled about $96. On Tuesday the US destroyed five Iranian tankers after missile attempts on a US warship; Iran fired ballistic missiles toward Jordan (Jordan said it intercepted 18 of 20, no casualties). On Wednesday Iran’s Revolutionary Guards said a new “prohibited zone” would stretch from near Chabahar across parts of the Gulf of Oman and Arabian Sea toward the Strait of Hormuz — the narrow lane that in peacetime carries about one-fifth of seaborne oil — but exact coordinates would come later. Ships entering without Iranian coordination face Iranian sanctions/service denial, per Guards’ claim. Oman corridor talks remain “final stage” rhetoric; published maps and completed International Maritime Organization registration are still not confirmed.

Context (what it sits on)

War since 28 February 2026. OPEC+ held October quotas flat Sunday. US diesel recently hit a nominal AAA record near $5.90 a gallon. Goldman Sachs researchers have raised odds of a path where Brent exceeds $120 if shipping attacks keep broadening — a bank scenario, not consensus.

Forward (months–years)

Watch whether coordinates are published and enforceable, whether $100+ sticks into US Consumer Price Index tomorrow and the Federal Reserve’s 15–16 September meeting, and European Central Bank messaging this afternoon. Cross-beat: French nuclear outages and euro-zone inflation.

US
Fact

Mail-ballot stay: responses in, no ruling yet

What happened

California and allied states filed their opposition Wednesday to the Justice Department’s Supreme Court stay application (26A305) against Judge Indira Talwani’s injunction blocking mandatory parts of the US Postal Service mail-ballot rule for the 3 November midterms. Civic groups and postal-worker amici also weighed in. The Court has not ruled overnight. North Carolina and Alabama are already mailing absentee ballots; more states follow this week. Producer Price Index (PPI — factory-gate prices) is due today 8:30 a.m. Eastern (14:30 Paris); Consumer Price Index (CPI — shoppers’ inflation) Friday — both gate the Federal Reserve meeting next week after August’s strong +162,000 jobs print.

Context (what it sits on)

About one-third of Americans typically vote by mail. Talwani found the rushed rule likely unlawful and a disenfranchisement risk. The administration argues ballots already mailed cannot be recalled.

Forward (months–years)

If the Court stays the injunction mid-mailing wave, administrators face chaos; if not, current processes hold toward Election Day. Oil above $100 and record diesel are the kitchen-table channel into midterms.

What happened
