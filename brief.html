Global markets
Fact

Wall Street steadies on Monday, Seoul falls back on Tuesday

What happened

On Monday 19 October the Nasdaq Composite rose 1.11 percent to 26,430.89, the S&P 500 rose 0.41 percent to 7,690.73 and the Dow Jones Industrial Average slipped 0.07 percent to 51,966.03 (WSJ, AP). The Cboe Volatility Index (VIX) fell 7.75 percent to 18.44. In Asia on Monday the Kospi closed up 1.76 percent at 5,768.01 and the Nikkei 225 down 0.62 percent at 64,916.70 (Yahoo Finance data). On Tuesday 20 October the Kospi was down about 4 percent in late trade, around 5,520 to 5,545, with Samsung Electronics, SK Hynix and battery makers lower (Ajunews, Yahoo Finance, intraday). The Nikkei was down roughly 0.6 to 1 percent at midday. Hong Kong reopened after a holiday.

Context (what it sits on)

Monday's rebound came as traders pared bets that AI companies had overstretched themselves with debt (WSJ). The rebound was narrow and led by tech. Korea has swung hardest in the AI sell-off because its market is dominated by memory-chip makers and retail investors who borrow to buy shares. Correction to yesterday's brief: its Monday Asia figures were intraday. Monday's official closes were Kospi 5,768.01 (up 1.76 percent, not 0.60 percent) and Nikkei 225 64,916.70 (down 0.62 percent, not 1.02 percent).

Forward (months–years)

Netflix reports on Tuesday evening, then Meta and Tesla on Wednesday 21 October. The Bank of Korea sets rates on Thursday 22 October. The Federal Reserve decision on Wednesday 28 October is the next macro test.

US rates and the Fed
Mixed

Yields edge up and markets see a small chance of a Fed rise

What happened

The 10-year Treasury yield rose to about 5.11 percent on Monday, the 2-year to about 4.75 percent and the 30-year to about 5.45 percent (Economic Times, CNBC). Futures priced about a 17 percent chance of a quarter-point Federal Reserve rate rise at the meeting ending on Wednesday 28 October, with about 84 percent on a hold (CME FedWatch via Economic Times).

Context (what it sits on)

Oil above 100 dollars is pushing up inflation expectations, which keeps a rise on the table even though Chair Kevin Warsh has not signalled one. Fed officials are in their pre-meeting blackout, so data and oil will drive pricing until the decision.

Forward (months–years)

Watch the 10-year yield around 5.1 percent and any surge in hike odds. The statement comes on Wednesday 28 October at 19:00 Paris time, which is winter time (CET) by then.

Chip stocks
Mixed

Semiconductor index ends an eight-day losing run

What happened

The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index rose 1.61 percent to 11,316.34 on Monday, its first gain in nine sessions (Korean market reports, tracker note). The eight losing sessions cut about 15.7 percent, the longest losing run since October 2008 (Claim, press). SK Hynix's US-listed shares rose about 4.7 percent. Nvidia rose 1.38 percent to 217.09 dollars, still about 9.3 percent below its 6 October high.

Context (what it sits on)

The sell-off was about who pays for the AI build-out and at what interest rate, not a fall in chip demand, since TSMC's record quarter showed demand is still strong. A Chinese memory maker, CXMT, reporting a return to profit added to worries about competition for Korean memory makers.

Forward (months–years)

Meta's spending guidance on Wednesday 21 October, then Microsoft and Alphabet on Wednesday 28 October and AMD on Tuesday 3 November, will show whether AI spending keeps rising.

Energy
Fact

Brent and December WTI ease on a Russia sanctions waiver, while expiring November WTI jumps

What happened

On Monday 19 October December Brent settled at 109.55 dollars a barrel, down 31 cents from Friday's 109.86 (Xinhua, WSJ, DTN). December West Texas Intermediate (WTI) settled at 98.54 dollars, down 48 cents from 99.02. November WTI, which expires on Tuesday 20 October, rose 2.25 dollars to 103.66 dollars from 101.41, its eighth straight gain, as buyers squeezed the barrel closest to delivery. Prices fell in the morning after Trump waived some new sanctions on Russian energy, then recovered as the Houthis claimed missile attacks on Saudi energy sites. In Asian trading on Tuesday December Brent was about 109.8 to 110.2 dollars (intraday, not a settle).

Context (what it sits on)

November WTI's premium over December widened to about 5.12 dollars, a sign of tight near-term US supply as buyers turn to American barrels with Hormuz largely shut to normal traffic. Iraq says its exports rose above 3.4 million barrels a day this month using Hormuz, Turkey's Ceyhan port and trucks through Syria.

Forward (months–years)

WTI November expires Tue 20 Oct, so from Wednesday 21 October the US headline price is the December contract, about 5 dollars lower on the roll alone. December Brent expires on Friday 30 October.

Iran and Hormuz
Mixed

Guards declare the strait closed and threaten undersea cables, while US says ships still pass

What happened

Iran's Revolutionary Guards said the Strait of Hormuz is closed, citing Israeli strikes on Hezbollah, and a Guards spokesman threatened seven undersea internet cables that run through the strait (Gulf News). US Central Command chief Admiral Brad Cooper said the waterway remains open for business. The German Navy's shipping centre counted 13 more attacks on merchant ships since 8 October, increasingly targeting container and cargo ships rather than only tankers. Pakistan's army chief Field Marshal Asim Munir is due in Tehran on Tuesday as a mediator (Reuters via Geo).

Context (what it sits on)

The war began with US and Israeli strikes in February and major combat has paused, but talks are stalled. Iran wants the US to lift its blockade of Iranian ports and ease oil sanctions, and the US wants the nuclear programme dealt with first. Russian Foreign Minister Lavrov visited Tehran and condemned the US-Israeli attacks.

Forward (months–years)

Watch the Munir visit for any revival of talks, and any attack on cables, which would hit Gulf internet and banking traffic.

Yemen, Saudi Arabia and Egypt
Fact

Houthi strike kills at least 53, and Egypt delays its vote again

What happened

A Houthi missile strike on a Yemeni government forces training camp in Shabwa province killed at least 53 people and injured about 200 on Monday (AP, Washington Post). The Houthis claimed six operations against Saudi targets including oil facilities and airports, which have not been independently confirmed. Egypt's parliament adjourned its second closed session on President Sisi's request for authority to support Saudi Arabia militarily and set the vote for Monday 26 October (Reuters, AFP).

Context (what it sits on)

Egypt's constitution needs a two-thirds majority, 398 of 596 members, to send troops on a combat mission abroad, and pro-government parties hold more than that. Approval would not send troops automatically. Egypt would act only if Saudi Arabia asks, and no request has been made. Riyadh holds 10.3 billion dollars of deposits at Egypt's central bank (The National).

Forward (months–years)

Watch the vote on Monday 26 October and whether Riyadh makes a formal request.

Ukraine
Mixed

Putin floats a Trump-Xi summit and Trump turns on Zelensky again

What happened

The Kremlin proposed that Putin, Trump and Xi Jinping meet at the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit in Shenzhen on 18 and 19 November, and Trump said on Monday he was open to it, framing it around nuclear arms and trade (Washington Post, CNBC, Politico). The Kremlin said no agenda had been agreed and China said it had no information. Trump again criticised Zelensky and said Russia has been fine on Ukraine (New York Post). Zelensky said US deliveries of Patriot interceptors are being held up (Euronews, AFP). Russian strikes on energy left more than 500,000 households without power.

Context (what it sits on)

Kyiv rejects any deal on its future made without it. The row with Trump follows weeks of pressure on Ukraine to accept terms, and European leaders have backed Kyiv at last week's EU summit.

Forward (months–years)

Watch whether the three-way meeting is confirmed, and any change in US weapons deliveries.

Israel and Lebanon
Fact

Military talks open in Tampa

What happened

Israeli and Lebanese military delegations began US-mediated talks at Central Command headquarters in Tampa, Florida, on Tuesday 20 October, expected to run into Wednesday and limited to working-level security talks (MTV Lebanon, US official). Lebanon says it will reject direct military coordination with Israel and will press for an end to Israeli attacks (The National, Al Arabiya).

Context (what it sits on)

The talks are meant to put into practice a framework agreed in June covering Hezbollah's disarmament, Lebanese army deployment, Israeli withdrawals and a verification mechanism. Israel holds a general election on Tuesday 27 October, and Lebanese commentators say Israel may be playing for time until then.

Forward (months–years)

A signal of progress would be an agreed pilot zone or a date for the next round.

Indonesia
Fact

Jakarta rebounds as the exchange changes the index

What happened

The Jakarta Composite Index rose 4.16 percent in Tuesday's morning session (CNBC Indonesia), after a record two-day fall triggered by MSCI's proposal to reclassify Indonesia from emerging market to a standalone market. The Indonesia Stock Exchange said it will exclude stocks with highly concentrated ownership, about 18 percent of index weight, from the main index (Nikkei Asia).

Context (what it sits on)

Standalone status would push big index-tracking funds to sell Indonesian shares. MSCI's consultation runs until 30 April 2027 with a decision by 30 June 2027, so Jakarta has time to address concerns about free float and ownership concentration.

Forward (months–years)

Watch whether the rebound holds and whether regulators announce further reforms.

France
Fact

Assembly postpones the vote on the revenue part of the budget

What happened

The formal vote on the first part of the 2027 budget, covering taxes and revenue, planned for Tuesday 20 October, has been postponed because the debate is far from finished (LCP, Capital). The debate resumes on Tuesday 27 October after the social security budget, whose vote is due that day. The final vote on the whole budget is still set for Tuesday 17 November.

Context (what it sits on)

Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu leads a minority government and needs opposition abstentions to pass the budget. Delays raise the chance the government runs short of time and must use special constitutional powers.

Forward (months–years)

Watch the social security budget vote on Tuesday 27 October.

Kenya and Congo (Ebola)
Fact

Kenya reaches eight cases, and Congo's outbreak keeps growing

What happened

Kenya confirmed its eighth Bundibugyo Ebola case, a 49-year-old hospital attendant exposed to the first imported case, with one death so far and no community spread reported (AP, Africanews). The European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control reports Congo's outbreak in eight provinces, including newly affected Nord Ubangi, with 9,455 confirmed cases and 4,561 deaths up to 17 October.

Context (what it sits on)

There is no approved vaccine for the Bundibugyo strain. Cuts to US health aid have weakened the response, according to the Guardian.

Forward (months–years)

Watch for any case in Kenya not linked to the first patient, which would signal community spread.

European Union
Fact

Brussels funds clean-tech projects and publishes the first AI Act standard

What happened

The European Commission announced 3.4 billion euros for 77 net-zero technology projects from the Innovation Fund and more than 390 million euros for 135 LIFE climate and environment projects on Monday (Commission press corner). It also published the first harmonised standard supporting the AI Act in the EU's Official Journal.

Context (what it sits on)

Harmonised standards give companies a practical route to show their AI systems meet the law, ahead of obligations for high-risk systems.

Forward (months–years)

The EU-Gulf Cooperation Council summit on Saturday 24 October is the next big EU diplomatic date.

AI (models and science)
Claim

New fast agent models from MiniMax and Meta

What happened

Chinese developer MiniMax released M3.1 Flash, an agent-focused model it says is nearly three times faster than its predecessor with 70 percent lower costs on agent tasks (company blog, Reuters). Meta published Muse Glimmer 30B, an open model designed to run agents on devices. Nvidia launched new open models for agents on company servers and PCs (TechCrunch). Inception launched Mercury 3, a fast diffusion-based language model.

Context (what it sits on)

Performance figures are company claims. The trend is toward smaller, cheaper models that run agents locally, which lowers the cost of AI use.

Forward (months–years)

Independent benchmarks over the coming weeks will test the claims.

Nvidia
Fact

Europe commitment put at nearly 10 billion dollars, German declaration signed

What happened

Nvidia said in a blog post on 19 October that its commitments in Europe total nearly 10 billion dollars. The German federal government signed a joint declaration of intent with Nvidia on physical-AI models, meaning AI for robots and machines, at the Invest in Germany Summit with Chancellor Merz, with no amount given. Nvidia disclosed new stakes in four German companies, Agile Robots, Avelios Medical, Mirelo and Reflex Aerospace, without amounts, plus projects with Siemens, the Julich research centre and the European Broadcasting Union. Shares rose 1.38 percent to 217.09 dollars (Claim, tracker market data).

Context (what it sits on)

Europe wants sovereign AI capacity, meaning computing that it controls, and Nvidia is positioning itself as the supplier. Most of the 10 billion is commitments and partnerships, not a single investment.

Forward (months–years)

GTC Berlin keynote Wed 21 Oct 11:00 Paris time. DGX Spark 64-gigabyte and Nemotron 3.5 Bolt on Fri 23 Oct. Nvidia results on Tue 17 Nov.

Tesla
Fact

Proxy sets a short 3 December ballot with no SpaceX vote

What happened

Tesla's 2026 proxy, filed with the Securities and Exchange Commission on 19 October, sets the annual meeting for Thursday 3 December at 3 pm Central time (22:00 Paris time) at Giga Texas and online. Only three items are on the ballot: re-electing Musk, Robyn Denholm and JB Straubel, an advisory vote on pay, and ratifying PwC as auditor. There are no shareholder proposals and no SpaceX merger vote. Musk's 2025 stock awards are valued at 158.36 billion dollars, none of the 12 tranches of the 2025 award has been earned, and he owns 19.8 percent. A new opt-in voluntary retail voting programme lets small investors automatically vote with the board from meetings after 3 December.

Context (what it sits on)

Critics read the retail voting programme as preparation for a future SpaceX merger vote (Speculation). Director Ira Ehrenpreis joined the SpaceX board in February. Musk's claim of Roadster stunts on X is his own claim.

Forward (months–years)

Third-quarter results on Wed 21 Oct, webcast 23:30 Paris time. The response to the US safety regulator's AQ26002 inquiry is due on Friday 30 October (Claim). DP26010 is a defect petition, not a formal investigation, with a grant-or-deny deadline of 21 January 2027 (Claim).

Palantir
Fact

Knox Systems partnership lifts shares, Swiss report questions sales

What happened

Palantir announced a partnership with Knox Systems on 19 October to speed deployment of mission software onto clouds approved under the US government cloud security programme (FedRAMP) and the defence department's Impact Level 5 standard, with terms undisclosed (Palantir investor relations). Shares rose 4.36 percent to 194.15 dollars. Swiss magazine Republik reported on 15 October that the Swiss military passed on Palantir five times between 2020 and 2026 over cost, requirements and server-sovereignty issues. Palantir says there was no formal evaluation.

Context (what it sits on)

Government cloud approval is a bottleneck for selling to US agencies, so the deal targets faster sales. Swiss lawmakers are weighing limits on foreign AI software, part of a wider European debate over US defence software.

Forward (months–years)

Sovereign AI event in Seoul on Thu 22 and Fri 23 Oct. Third-quarter results on Monday 2 November, against guidance of 2.160 to 2.164 billion dollars in revenue and a Street estimate of about 2.18 billion.
