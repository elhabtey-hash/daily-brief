Ukraine
Fact

At least 32 killed in Wednesday's strikes, 22 in Pryluky. EU pays out 1.24 billion euros for Ukrainian drones and missiles

What happened

Russia attacked Ukraine with waves of missiles and drones overnight into and during Wednesday 7 October, Vladimir Putin's birthday. Reuters, citing Ukraine's emergency service on Thursday morning, puts the toll at at least 32 dead: 22 in Pryluky in the northern Chernihiv region, where a missile destroyed a five-storey residential building, and four in Kyiv after two more bodies were found in a damaged apartment block (Reuters via The Japan Times). Earlier counts from AFP included five children. President Volodymyr Zelensky called it one of Russia's most vile strikes. Industrial facilities and energy infrastructure were also hit. Ukrainian outlets report the attack used about 70 missiles and 130 drones, including 46 jet-powered drones, of which 161 were shot down or jammed (h.ua and BattlePolicy, secondary, not the final air force tally). Overnight into Thursday, Russian jet-powered drones triggered alerts over Kyiv, Lutsk, Rivne and other areas (Kyiv Post). In Brussels, the European Commission paid out 1.24 billion euros from its Ukraine Support Loan for Ukrainian-made drones, drone interceptors, drone ammunition and missiles (Commission, Euronews).

Context (what it sits on)

Russia is hitting Ukraine's energy system and cities before winter, and Pryluky is the deadliest single strike reported in weeks. Kyiv has accepted American-proposed technical talks with Russia in the United Arab Emirates before the end of October, and Moscow still has not given a date.

Forward (months–years)

Nobel Prize
Fact

Chemistry to Henri Kagan (France) and Kenso Soai (Japan) for non-linear effects and autocatalysis in asymmetric synthesis. Literature today 13:00

What happened

The Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences awarded the 2026 Nobel Prize in Chemistry on Wednesday 7 October to Henri B. Kagan of Université Paris-Sud, France, and Kenso Soai of the Tokyo University of Science, Japan, "for the discovery of non-linear effects and autocatalysis in asymmetric organic synthesis", sharing 12 million Swedish kronor equally (nobelprize.org press release and summary). Many molecules, including amino acids, come in two mirror-image forms, like left and right hands, but living things use only one. Kagan showed that a slightly lopsided catalyst can produce a product much more lopsided than itself, a non-linear effect. Soai designed the first reaction in which a product speeds up its own formation and amplifies a tiny excess of one mirror form into an almost pure one, which is called autocatalysis. Committee chair Heiner Linke said they solved a chemical mystery more than a century old: how this one-handedness, called homochirality, can emerge spontaneously. Soai was born in 1950 in Hiroshima. Earlier this week Medicine went to Karl Deisseroth, Peter Hegemann and Georg Nagel for optogenetics, and Physics to Francis Halzen for the IceCube neutrino observatory.

Context (what it sits on)

Getting the right mirror form matters in medicine, because the wrong form of a drug can be useless or harmful. The work feeds into how chemists design reactions for pharmaceuticals and into theories of how life began. Kagan becomes the first French laureate of this year's prizes.

Forward (months–years)

US markets
Fact

Fed minutes: most see another hike likely by year end. 10-yr touched 5.365%, highest since 2002. S&P 500 -0.22% to 7,801.75

What happened

Minutes of the Federal Open Market Committee's 15-16 September meeting, released on Wednesday 7 October at 20:00 Paris, record a unanimous vote to raise the federal funds rate by a quarter point to 3.75 to 4.00 percent, the first rise in three years (Federal Reserve). Most participants judged that another rise would likely be appropriate by the end of the year, but said decisions depend on incoming data. Several said policy was not restrictive or only mildly restrictive. Many worried that the longer energy prices stay high, the greater the risk that cost increases in some sectors spread into broader inflation, and several noted that the AI investment boom is lifting core goods prices as the effect of tariffs fades (Axios, Kitco quoting the minutes). Chair Kevin Warsh did not submit a projection in September, when 16 of 18 officials pencilled in at least one more rise in 2026. Markets on Wednesday: the S&P 500 fell 0.22 percent to 7,801.75, the Nasdaq Composite 0.22 percent to 27,538.69 and the Dow 0.66 percent to 51,180.17, ending winning streaks, and the small-company Russell 2000 fell 1.3 percent (Reuters). The ten-year Treasury yield touched 5.365 percent, its highest since April 2002, and the 30-year 5.732 percent, before a strong 39 billion dollar ten-year auction at 5.300 percent pulled them back (CNBC via Swingfolio). Caterpillar fell 5.75 percent after the Federal Trade Commission and the Agriculture Department opened a joint inquiry into farm equipment markets.

Context (what it sits on)

The Fed is raising rates while the Iran war keeps energy expensive and AI spending runs hot, and long-term yields are being pushed up by worries over government debt. Analysts expect third-quarter S&P 500 earnings to rise about 30.6 percent (LSEG).

Forward (months–years)
