AI chip sell-off
Fact

Chip stocks suffer their worst day in months

What happened

On Thursday 22 October the Philadelphia semiconductor index (SOX) fell 6.72 percent to 10,731.98. Lam Research fell 11.8 percent despite beating forecasts, AMD fell 8.1 percent and Broadcom 4.3 percent (press, Bloomberg via secondary). The Nasdaq Composite fell 2.60 percent, its largest drop since 5 June. Triggers were Meta's 7 percent fall after it raised its spending floor, Tesla's results, rising oil and a comment by Nvidia chief Jensen Huang that demand for specialised AI cloud capacity is off the charts but that the working capital to fund it is missing (CNBC, claim).

Context (what it sits on)

The market is shifting from asking whether AI demand exists to asking who pays for it. Specialised AI cloud firms such as CoreWeave rely on borrowing, so funding doubts hit the whole supply chain. Company results were in fact solid (Lam beat and raised, Texas Instruments data-centre sales up 95 percent), which suggests sentiment rather than earnings drove the fall.

Forward (months–years)

Microsoft and Alphabet report Wednesday 28 October, Amazon about Thursday 29 October, AMD Tuesday 3 November.

Iran and the Gulf
Mixed

Strike plans reported as the war reaches Saudi Arabia

What happened

The New York Times reported that US and Israeli militaries have drawn up specific plans to resume strikes on Iran after the US midterm elections in early November (claim, anonymous officials). Trump said something would happen fairly soon. The US ordered non-emergency staff and families to leave its embassy in Riyadh and consulates in Saudi Arabia (State Department, AP, BBC). Houthi attacks hit King Fahd airport in Dammam, killing two people, and the Houthis claimed strikes on Riyadh and an Aramco site at Yanbu, which Saudi Arabia has not confirmed (Al Jazeera, Newsquawk). Iran's Revolutionary Guard navy said four oil-transfer vessels were struck in the Gulf.

Context (what it sits on)

Mediation continues: Pakistan's foreign ministry said US-Iran talks on the Strait of Hormuz are ongoing, and Secretary of State Rubio spoke with Qatar's prime minister. The core gap is unchanged, with Washington wanting nuclear steps first and Tehran wanting the blockade lifted and sanctions eased first. Reuters reports Iranian officials see dragging out talks as a way to influence the midterms (claim).

Forward (months–years)

Egypt's parliament votes on support for Saudi Arabia Monday 26 October. US midterms Tuesday 3 November.

Energy
Fact

December Brent jumps 4 percent to a five-month high

What happened

December Brent settled at 113.17 dollars on Thursday, up 4.35 from Wednesday's 108.82, its highest close since 4 May. December WTI settled at 99.47 dollars, up 2.32 from 97.15, after touching 100.17 (CNBC, Xinhua, DTN, Newsquawk). US diesel futures rose 3.5 percent.

Context (what it sits on)

December WTI is the front month. The spread between Brent and WTI widened to about 13.7 dollars, showing the risk premium is concentrated in seaborne Gulf oil. Attacks on Saudi export sites would threaten the main route around Hormuz, the Red Sea port of Yanbu. High oil also keeps inflation pressure on central banks.

Forward (months–years)

December Brent expires Friday 30 October, after which January becomes the Brent benchmark.

Global markets
Fact

Wall Street splits as tech falls and bonds rally

What happened

On Thursday 22 October the S&P 500 fell 1.21 percent to 7,663.43, its worst day since July, the Nasdaq Composite fell 2.60 percent to 26,008.86 and the Dow was flat, up 0.02 percent at 52,867.98 (AP, Yahoo Finance). The Russell 2000 of smaller companies rose 0.11 percent. In Europe the DAX fell 1.07 percent and the CAC 40 1.39 percent.

Context (what it sits on)

Money moved out of AI winners into bonds and less tech-heavy stocks rather than out of the market entirely. The Dow's resilience shows the sell-off was concentrated.

Forward (months–years)

US results season continues next week.

US rates and the Fed
Mixed

Bond rally pushes the 10-year yield below 5 percent

What happened

The 10-year Treasury yield fell to about 4.95 to 4.97 percent on Thursday, its lowest in nearly a month, from about 5.12 percent on Wednesday (CNBC, Mint). Market pricing for a rate rise at the Federal Reserve meeting ending Wednesday 28 October fell to between about 7 and 15 percent depending on the measure (CME FedWatch via press), with a hold around 85 percent or more.

Context (what it sits on)

Investors fled to safety and trimmed bets on further Fed tightening, even as oil rose. The Fed faces a tension between energy-driven inflation and a wobbling stock market.

Forward (months–years)

Statement Wednesday 28 October at 19:00 Paris, winter time (CET).

Nvidia
Fact

Shares slide, Kazakhstan licence and new desktop

What happened

Nvidia shares fell 4.40 percent to 212.21 dollars on Thursday, 11.3 percent below the 6 October close of 239.24 (press, claim on exact figures). Nvidia said Kazakh company Firebird has a US export licence for 45,000 Grace Blackwell chips, for a data centre with Kazakhtelecom targeted to open commercially in the third quarter of 2027 (Nvidia blog). It also launched Minerva, software to monitor and fix problems in large AI data centres, which Tesla, IBM and Lambda plan to use. The DGX Spark 64 gigabyte desktop AI computer goes on sale today from 4,999 dollars through partners such as Dell, HP and Asus, alongside a small Nemotron 3.5 Bolt model.

Context (what it sits on)

The licence shows Washington is approving large sales to friendly countries building national AI capacity, a growth market outside the big US cloud firms. Huang's working-capital remark, though aimed at customers, fed the sell-off. A possible Nvidia deal with Reflection AI reported by the Financial Times remains unconfirmed.

Forward (months–years)

Huang speaks in Astana today. Nvidia results about Tuesday 17 November (claim until formally announced).

SpaceX
Fact

Shares fall on lockup day, filing confirms Tesla study

What happened

SpaceX shares closed about 9.1 percent lower near 144.75 dollars on Thursday 22 October, when up to about 1.3 billion shares became eligible for sale (press). Its third-quarter 10-Q filing confirms a board special committee is evaluating a potential combination with Tesla, with no agreement. It also filed an S-8 registering plan shares linked to the Cursor and Screenplay acquisitions. No new insider sale filings have appeared since 30 September. Starlink 15-15 was scrubbed for a third night at 27 seconds before launch because of weather, so Falcon launches this year remain at 119.

Context (what it sits on)

Eligibility is not selling, but the price drop shows investors fear supply. A Tesla combination is being studied, not agreed. Another 328.4 million shares are freed on Saturday 24 October and become tradable on Monday 26 October.

Forward (months–years)

Starlink 15-15 target tonight, Friday 23 October, 23:30 Paris. US telecoms regulator (FCC) meeting on satellite-to-phone spectrum Thursday 29 October. Starship Flight 15 about Sunday 8 November (press date).

Tesla
Mixed

Shares slip on lower spending as Cybercab claims face regulators

What happened

Tesla filed its third-quarter 10-Q on 22 October. Shares fell about 2 percent on Thursday as attention turned to capital spending of about 20 billion dollars this year, down from more than 25 billion previously guided (Reuters). The stock is about 14 percent lower this year. Finance chief Vaibhav Taneja said Cybercab will launch in Dallas next month, with more cities and sales to fleet operators by year-end (claim). Musk said Optimus robot problems should be solved around the end of next year (claim).

Context (what it sits on)

Lower spending helps cash flow but sits awkwardly with ambitious robotaxi and robot plans. The US road-safety regulator (NHTSA) is probing whether Cybercab meets federal vehicle standards, and Tesla's answers are due Friday 30 October. In Europe the vehicle committee (TCMV) has never voted on supervised self-driving approval.

Forward (months–years)

China incentives end Saturday 31 October. Annual meeting Thursday 3 December, 22:00 Paris.

Brazil
Fact

Final Datafolha poll shows a dead heat

What happened

Datafolha's poll of 20 to 22 October, published Thursday, gives Flavio Bolsonaro 47 percent and President Lula 46 percent, with valid votes tied at 50 percent each (G1, Folha, Reuters). The margin of error is 2 points. 94 percent of voters say their choice is final. Rejection is 46 percent for Bolsonaro and 45 percent for Lula.

Context (what it sits on)

Lula leads among women, Catholics, poorer voters and the Northeast, Bolsonaro among men, evangelicals and the South and Southeast. A very narrow result raises the risk of a contested count and market volatility in the real.

Forward (months–years)

Runoff Sunday 25 October, results Sunday evening Brazil time.

Ukraine
Mixed

Kyiv hit hard hours after Russia backs new talks

What happened

Russia struck Kyiv with 21 ballistic missiles, three with cluster warheads, killing at least four people and injuring dozens including children (AP, CBS). Russia said it downed 808 Ukrainian drones, many aimed at Moscow. Putin agreed in principle on Wednesday to resume three-way talks with the US and Ukraine, expected after 10 November, but no date is set.

Context (what it sits on)

Zelensky said the strikes show no change in the Kremlin's position and asked for more air defence and sanctions. Diplomacy and escalation are running in parallel.

Forward (months–years)

Proposed three-way talks after Tuesday 10 November.
