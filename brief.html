Global markets
Fact

Wall Street ends a bad week for tech, and Asia is mixed on Monday

What happened

On Friday 16 October the Nasdaq Composite fell 2.14 percent to 26,140.03, the S&P 500 fell 1.09 percent to 7,659.44 and the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 0.34 percent to 52,002.41 (CNBC, WSJ). The Nasdaq lost about 4.5 percent over the week, its worst week since June, the S&P 500 about 2 percent, while the Dow rose about 0.7 percent. The VIX volatility index closed above 22 for the first time in four months. On Monday 19 October Japan's Nikkei 225 closed down 1.02 percent at 64,660.20, with Advantest, Tokyo Electron and SoftBank Group weak. South Korea's Kospi opened down about 3 percent but closed up 0.60 percent at 5,702.30. Shanghai was down about 0.4 percent in early trade and Hong Kong was closed for a holiday. US stock futures were slightly higher.

Context (what it sits on)

The sell-off is driven by doubts about whether AI spending will pay off, by the cost of borrowing for debt-funded data-centre builders, and by oil back above 100 dollars, which feeds inflation fears. It is a sector and rates story rather than a collapse in demand. Correction to Friday's brief: its Asia figures were intraday. Friday's official closes were Kospi 5,668.05 (up 2.05 percent, not about 3 percent) and Nikkei 225 65,324.29 (up 1.39 percent, not 1.75 percent).

Forward (months–years)

More than 70 S&P 500 companies report this week, including Netflix on Tuesday, Tesla and Meta on Wednesday. Their spending plans will decide whether the AI trade steadies. The Fed meeting ending Wednesday 28 October is the next macro test.

Korea
Fact

Kospi lost 14.4 percent last week and Seoul keeps emergency tools ready

What happened

The Kospi closed last week at 5,668.05, down about 14.4 percent from 6,625.93 on 8 October, its worst week of the year, after circuit breakers halted trading on Thursday 15 October when both the Kospi and the smaller Kosdaq fell more than 7 percent (Yahoo Finance data, Korean press). Yonhap says the Kospi is the worst-performing major market in the world this month. Over the weekend Finance Minister Lee Hyung-il said the government could activate a market stabilisation fund or temporarily ban short selling if volatility worsened, but would not announce steps in advance (Herald Business). The Financial Services Commission held an emergency check and urged brokers to limit lending for share buying.

Context (what it sits on)

Korea's market is dominated by Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix, which have been the biggest winners of the AI memory boom, so it swings hardest when AI sentiment turns. Heavy retail borrowing to buy shares amplifies falls. A Korean report says Samsung will use TSMC's 3-nanometre process for the base chips of Nvidia's custom high-bandwidth memory (Claim, Seoul Economic Daily via Reuters).

Forward (months–years)

Watch foreign selling and whether the government uses the stabilisation fund. A short-selling ban would be a strong signal of official alarm.

Japan
Fact

Nikkei slips as Takaichi's approval falls below 50 percent

What happened

The Nikkei 225 closed down 1.02 percent at 64,660.20 on Monday, after rising 1.39 percent on Friday. A Kyodo poll put Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi's approval at 49.1 percent, the lowest since she took office, after criticism over the delayed resignation of the farm minister (Japan Times). Watch whether the approval slide continues and whether China takes concrete steps beyond statements.

China
Fact

Growth picks up to 4.5 percent in the third quarter

What happened

The National Bureau of Statistics said on Monday 19 October that gross domestic product grew 4.5 percent in July to September from a year earlier, up from 4.3 percent in the second quarter and above the forecasts of about 4.4 percent from Bloomberg and Wind surveys. Growth over the first nine months was 4.6 percent and quarter-on-quarter growth was 1.2 percent. Industrial production rose 5.5 percent in September, in line with forecasts (Bloomberg via Moneycontrol). The 10-year government bond yield rose 1 basis point to 1.65 percent.

Context (what it sits on)

Beijing's target this year is 4.5 to 5 percent. Exports have been strong, with September exports up sharply, while consumer demand and the property market remain weak. Last week the authorities urged state funds and companies to buy shares after a tech-led slump took the CSI 300 index to its lowest since August 2025.

Forward (months–years)

A strong headline makes big new stimulus less likely this year. Watch Communist Party economic signals and October trade data in early November.

Mixed

Semiconductor index falls for eight straight sessions

What happened

The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index, which tracks the main US-listed chip companies, closed at 11,137.29 on Friday, down 3.83 percent (Nasdaq, WSJ data). US rates and the Fed
Mixed

Rate-rise odds fall even as inflation stays hot

What happened

The 10-year Treasury yield was about 5.10 to 5.12 percent on Friday, the 2-year about 4.72 to 4.77 percent and the 30-year about 5.42 to 5.44 percent (MarketWatch, WSJ). Fed funds futures gave about a 15 percent chance of a rate rise at the meeting on Tuesday 27 and Wednesday 28 October (CNBC citing futures), down from roughly one in three on Thursday. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh said at an International Monetary Fund event in Bangkok that he was not signalling a decision, and Vice Chair Philip Jefferson and New York Fed President John Williams have signalled no urgency to raise rates. Some news agencies still describe bets on a second straight hike, which overstates the futures pricing.

Context (what it sits on)

The Fed raised rates in September. US consumer prices rose 3.7 percent on the year in September and producer prices 6.3 percent, according to CNBC. Warsh has rejected giving advance guidance, so a surprise cannot be ruled out.

Forward (months–years)

The decision is due on Wednesday 28 October at 19:00 Paris time, after Paris moves to winter time. Officials are in their blackout period until then.

Iran
Mixed

Shipping attacks hit a weekly record as Tehran repeats its reopening plan

What happened

The UK Maritime Trade Operations agency said at least 10 vessels were attacked in or near the Strait of Hormuz in the week to Sunday 18 October, the most in any week since the war began in February (CBS). A tug was hit by two unknown projectiles off the United Arab Emirates on Sunday, with one minor injury. Iran's Revolutionary Guards said two supertankers hit mines on Saturday while trying to bypass Iran's shipping route (Claim). On Sunday Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Iran's seven-day plan is still on the table, under which the strait would reopen on the sixth day if the US lifts its blockade of Iranian ports and oil sanctions and ends hostilities on all fronts (Reuters). US Central Command said 97 percent of ships attempting to cross were not hit and that it has redirected 138 vessels under the blockade.

Context (what it sits on)

Before the war about a fifth of world oil and liquefied gas trade went through the strait. The US insists Iran's nuclear enrichment be addressed first. Indirect talks continue through Qatar and Oman, according to an Iranian lawmaker.

Forward (months–years)

Trump has said there will be no US strike on Iran before the 3 November midterms. Watch whether the US formally answers Iran's conditions.

Fact

Egypt edges toward military support for Riyadh

What happened

Egypt's National Defence Council pledged all necessary and possible support to Saudi Arabia, and President Abdel Fattah el-Sisi asked parliament to authorise defensive military help if Riyadh requests it. Forward (months–years)

Ukraine
Fact

Ukraine strikes St Petersburg oil sites as Russian grid attacks continue

What happened

Overnight into Sunday 18 October drones hit the St Petersburg Oil Terminal and the Ruchyi oil depot, killing at least one person and injuring five, and also hit an Ozon logistics centre and a VK data centre, the city's governor said (BBC). Russia's defence ministry said it shot down more than 1,000 Ukrainian drones overnight. Zelensky said on Saturday that Russia had attacked Ukraine's energy system 76 times since Trump's energy ceasefire call and had launched nearly 2,700 drones, 1,700 glide bombs and 66 missiles in a week. The United Nations said the first two weeks of October were the deadliest for Ukrainian civilians in years.

Context (what it sits on)

Both sides are hitting each other's energy systems ahead of winter. Ukraine paused refinery strikes for three days after Trump's call, then resumed. Tens of thousands of Ukrainians remain without power.

Forward (months–years)

Expect more Russian strikes on the grid and more Ukrainian attacks on refineries, which also push up diesel and oil prices.

European Union
Mixed

Summit ends with conclusions adopted on Ukraine, defence, Middle East and migration

What happened

The European Council adopted conclusions on 15 and 16 October on competitiveness, Ukraine, Moldova, the 2028 to 2034 budget, the Middle East, European defence and security, migration, and enlargement (Council, Press Club Brussels). According to summaries of the text, leaders backed further military and financial support for Ukraine and condemned attacks on its energy system, called for freedom of navigation and an end to attacks on shipping in the Middle East, and pressed for a military-mobility agreement by the end of 2026. Context (what it sits on)

The budget is the bloc's main spending fight for the next decade. The Middle East wording supports stronger EU naval operations as shipping attacks rise.

Forward (months–years)

Special budget summit on Thursday 26 November. An EU-Gulf Cooperation Council summit is planned for Saturday 24 October.

Kenya and Congo (Ebola)
Fact

Ebola reaches Kenya with seven cases

What happened

Kenya confirmed four more cases of the Bundibugyo strain of Ebola on Saturday 17 October, bringing its total to seven, including three health workers and a relative of the first patient, who died (Al Jazeera, AP). Officials say 128 of 130 contacts are in quarantine and there is no sign of community transmission. The World Health Organization's latest formal update counts 8,728 cases and 4,205 deaths in the Democratic Republic of Congo as of 6 October. The US Centers for Disease Control's later count, cited in Friday's brief, is higher. Sudan has stepped up border screening, with no cases reported.

Context (what it sits on)

There is no approved vaccine for the Bundibugyo strain, unlike the Zaire strain. Health-worker infections are an early warning sign of hospital spread.

Forward (months–years)

Watch for cases outside quarantine in Kenya and any WHO emergency committee meeting.
