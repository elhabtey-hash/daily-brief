Science
Fact

Nature: xenocortical mice (human cortical organoids)

What happened

Nature (16 September): a Stanford-led team (senior author Sergiu Pașca) transplanted lab-grown human cortical organoids into mice genetically blocked from developing most cortex and hippocampus (~98% absent). Human grafts grew extensively, generated diverse cortical cell types, and formed functional connections with the mouse nervous system. Authors reject “humanized mice / mini-brains” framing and call them xenocortical mice. Use case shown: oxygen deprivation injured human graft cells and produced motor deficits — supporting a model for perinatal injury and developmental disease research. Peer-reviewed research advance with stated ethics caveats; not a commercial catalyst.

Context (what it sits on)

Challenges and extends organoid-transplant methods for studying human cortical development and injury in vivo.

Forward (months–years)

Watch follow-on studies and any ethics/regulatory framing; no near-term market catalyst.
