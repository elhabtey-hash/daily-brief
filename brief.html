Palantir (lead)
Mixed

Poland is due to sign an AI decision-support contract with Palantir at midday, announced but not yet completed

What happened

Poland's Ministry of National Defence has announced that it will sign a contract today, Fri 27 Nov, at 12:00 CET, at the Polish Army Museum in the Warsaw Citadel, with Palantir Technologies Poland, for the supply of software for an artificial-intelligence decision-support system for the Polish armed forces. Deputy prime minister and defence minister Władysław Kosiniak-Kamysz is due to attend (official ministry notice, gov.pl). Reuters reported that the ministry gave no other details. At the time this brief was written, about four hours before the announced ceremony, the signing had not been confirmed as completed and should not be treated as done. Polish defence media, including the ministry-run Polska Zbrojna and TVN24, say the system is Palantir's Maven Smart System, under the codename Saturn. That identification has not been confirmed in the official notice. The value, term and number of units have not been disclosed. There were no new US share-sale filings overnight. Palantir's last official close remains Wednesday's $163.59, because US markets were shut on Thursday.

Context (what it sits on)

If signed, this would be Poland's first binding Palantir contract after a December 2024 cooperation agreement and an October 2025 letter of intent. It would extend Maven-style software to another NATO front-line state, against a backdrop of protests and an ombudsman inquiry in Poland and of French, German and Dutch efforts to build or switch to a European alternative. Until a signed text or a company statement appears, the financial size cannot be judged. Last week's tax-cover sales remain coded S, not F, totalling about $85.35 million, and director Peter Thiel bought 850,100 shares for about $122.25 million, coded P.

Forward (months–years)

Watch whether the 12:00 ceremony takes place, and whether value, term or a Palantir statement is disclosed. A possible Nasdaq-100 index decision is still listed for Fri 11 Dec. Links: Europe, NATO eastern flank, AI companies.

Mixed

Trump says a pardon for Huang is always an option but probably unnecessary, and floats a US stake

What happened

In an exclusive interview with the New York Post, published late on Tue 24 Nov and also reported by CNBC, President Trump said a pardon for Nvidia chief Jensen Huang was 'always an option, I guess, but I don't think it'll be necessary,' and that he wanted Huang to 'just tell the truth' and not invoke the Fifth Amendment at next week's congressional appearances. He also said he was open to the US government taking an equity stake in Nvidia, along the lines of the 2025 Intel stake, and that it was 'probably not too late.' No pardon request, charges, or stake negotiations have been reported. These remarks did not appear in the Daily Briefs of 24, 25 or 26 Nov (a 'pardon' line in the 25 Nov book referred only to the traditional turkey ceremony). They are claims from an interview, not an official pardon or a deal. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent's informal request for answers around 25 Nov has still produced no public Treasury or company statement. The overdue quarterly report, the 10-Q, is still unfiled, and there is still no public reply to Senator Cory Booker's 25 Nov letter, due on Wed 16 Dec, or from senators Michael Bennet and John Hickenlooper. The official Congress.gov calendar still lists a Senate Banking Committee open hearing on Thu 3 Dec at 16:00 CET in room 106 of the Dirksen Senate Office Building, titled 'Oversight hearings to examine United States export controls.' The date, time, venue and committee are established. Huang's appearance as a witness remains a claim. The official GTC Washington catalogue still names Ian Buck, not Huang, for the lead spotlight on Tue 1 Dec from 20:00 to 20:40 CET, and a former keynote page that had named Huang now returns an error. Nvidia's last official close remains Wednesday's $149.30, down 3.91 percent. Share volume for that session has been revised slightly in market-data sources to about 366.6 million, from the 363.4 million cited in yesterday's book. Huang resigned as chair effective 11 November and was reinstated effective 22 November. Colette Kress remains chief financial officer. Harvey Jones is chair.

Context (what it sits on)

Forward (months–years)

France
Fact

Joint committee fails to agree a social-security budget, which now returns to the Assembly

What happened

The joint committee of 14 deputies and senators that met on Thursday, Thu 26 Nov, failed to agree a common text of the social-security financing bill for 2027 (franceinfo, Public Sénat, Le Figaro). The bill therefore returns to the National Assembly for a new reading. The National Assembly had rejected the government's text on 23 October, and the Senate had adopted its own version on 18 November. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu had said on Wednesday that he had no wish to force the issue, while leaving open the use of article 49.3 of the Constitution, which allows the government to adopt a bill without a full vote. That option remains available.

Context (what it sits on)

The failure was widely expected and does not by itself decide the wider state budget, which faces the same choice between compromise, 49.3 and ordinances.

Forward (months–years)

Watch the new Assembly reading and whether the government then uses 49.3. Links: Europe.

Iran and the Gulf
Mixed

The Ras Tanura attack claim is still unconfirmed, as oil stays high and Hormuz incidents continue

What happened

Yemen's Houthi movement said on Wednesday that it had attacked the Ras Tanura oil refinery and Riyadh's financial district. Ras Tanura is Saudi Arabia's largest refinery and part of a major crude-export complex. The Saudi government and Aramco had still not confirmed the claim by Thursday's oil settle, and the effect on output and shipments remains unclear (CNBC, Reuters). Iran has again said mines struck ships in the Strait of Hormuz. Qatar continues to call for freedom of navigation through the strait and a return to talks between the United States and Iran. Talks remain stalled.

Context (what it sits on)

The unconfirmed refinery claim, and the wider run of incidents in the strait, were the main prompts for January Brent's further rise to 96.57 dollars on Thursday. Any confirmed damage at Ras Tanura would be a larger shock than another unconfirmed report.

Forward (months–years)

Spain
Fact

Last day of campaigning before Sunday's election, with a pre-election blackout still in force on new polls

What happened

Spain votes on Sun 29 Nov. Today, Fri 27 Nov, is the last day of the campaign. Saturday is a legal day of reflection, when campaigning is not allowed. Closing rallies are listed for Pedro Sánchez and Ada Colau in Madrid, Alberto Núñez Feijóo in Seville and Santiago Abascal in Valencia, after a four-candidate debate on Thursday. A legal pre-election blackout on the publication of new voting-intention polls remains in force, so this brief reports no new poll numbers and, of course, no results. The last legally published surveys, already reported, pointed in different directions, with several putting the conservative People's Party ahead, often short of a majority on its own, and the official CIS barometer putting the Socialists ahead.

Context (what it sits on)

The election will be decided at the ballot box on Sunday, not by the last published surveys. Any claim of a winner before official or recognised news-agency results would be speculation.

Forward (months–years)

Watch Sunday's official count, from AP, Reuters or the official Spanish results, and any comment on coalition arithmetic once those results exist. Links: Europe.

What happened

CoinNess, in a Thursday evening item, reported CME FedWatch at 99.4 percent for a quarter-point Federal Reserve rate rise on Wed 9 Dec and 0.6 percent for no change. That is a shade below the 99.8 percent CoinNess print used in yesterday's book after Wednesday's inflation and growth data, and still close to certain. Context (what it sits on)
