US markets (lead)
Fact

Best day since April as oil falls

What happened

Monday 9 November closes: S&P 500 7,549.94, up 173.08 points or 2.35 percent, its best day since 8 April. Nasdaq Composite 25,253.14, up 2.74 percent. Dow Jones Industrial Average 52,389.75, up 1,104.02 points or 2.15 percent (CNBC, AP). The Dow is back above its Election Day close, while the S&P 500 and Nasdaq remain slightly below. The Philadelphia semiconductor index (SOX) rose 6.85 percent.

Context (what it sits on)

The trigger was a sharp fall in oil after Trump said new strikes on Iran were unlikely, plus a rebound in chip stocks after last week’s sell-off, which cost the S&P 500 2.7 percent. Nvidia was the notable exception (see Nvidia row). A Reuters/Ipsos poll put Trump’s approval at 32 percent, near his career low, after the midterm losses.

Forward (months–years)

Tue 10 Nov 14:30 CET: October consumer prices. Tonight 23:00 CET: CoreWeave results. A one-day rally after a steep weekly fall is not a trend; watch whether it survives the inflation data.

Iran and the Gulf
Mixed

Trump holds off on strikes, keeps blockade

What happened

After meeting Qatar’s prime minister and foreign minister, Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al-Thani, at the White House on Monday, Trump told Reuters he will hold off on new strikes on Iran for now at the request of Qatar, Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates and others, but will keep up the naval blockade around the Strait of Hormuz. “We’re locked and loaded,” he said, adding that he did not think force would be needed. Iranian state media said the US struck Qeshm island overnight; the US has not commented.

Context (what it sits on)

The war began on 28 February. Iran’s proposal would end the US blockade of its ports, lift some sanctions and halt bombing in return for Iran ending its own blockade of the strait, with nuclear talks later; Washington has rejected it, and Secretary of State Marco Rubio called Iran’s conditions unrealistic. Al-Thani called the talks positive, while an Iranian security official dismissed reports of talks as fake. Iranian state television said 11 tankers were disabled in 10 days, and Yemen’s Houthi movement, allied to Iran, has hit Saudi targets. A reported 8 billion dollar asset-release plan is disputed by a Qatari official (claim).

Forward (months–years)

No deal, ceasefire or reopening of the strait is written as achieved. Watch Vice President JD Vance’s meeting with Al-Thani, any US reply to the Iranian plan, and tanker incident counts.

Energy
Fact

Oil falls again after a historic week

What happened

Monday 9 November settlements: January Brent, the headline contract, settled at $92.57, down $5.75 or 5.85 percent. December WTI (West Texas Intermediate, the US benchmark) settled at $86.85, down $3.98 or 4.38 percent (Reuters, Xinhua). December WTI is the front month until it expires around 19–20 November. Natural gas for December rose 7.34 percent to $3.218 per million British thermal units.

Context (what it sits on)

Reuters says January Brent kept falling after the settlement, at one point more than $7 down on the day and at its lowest since late August; those later prices are not settlements. Brent fell 16 percent last week, its steepest weekly drop since the start of the pandemic in 2020. Saudi Arabia’s East-West pipeline, its main route around Hormuz, was reported attacked in the Medina region.

Forward (months–years)

US inflation data today. Watch Tuesday settlements, any Hormuz shipping reopening and further attacks on Gulf infrastructure.

US economy
Fact

October inflation due today

What happened

The Bureau of Labor Statistics releases October consumer prices (CPI) at 14:30 CET today. This is pending. Consensus (Dow Jones and Reuters polls): headline CPI up 0.3 percent on the month after 0.6 percent in September, and core CPI (excluding food and energy) up 0.2 percent after 0.3 percent. There will be no official annual rate because October 2025 prices were not collected during last year’s government shutdown.

Context (what it sits on)

Unofficial estimates put annual inflation near 3.7–3.9 percent (Cleveland Fed nowcast 3.74 percent; Reuters poll 3.9 percent) against September’s official 3.7 percent, well above the Federal Reserve’s 2 percent goal. It is the last CPI before the Fed’s 8–9 December meeting. The CME FedWatch tool showed about 78 percent odds of a quarter-point rise in December on Monday, which would be a second rise after September’s. Chicago Fed President Austan Goolsbee said the Fed must face its “inflation-side failure”.

Forward (months–years)

Tue 10 Nov 14:30 CET: CPI. Wed 11 Nov: Veterans Day holiday (bond market closed). Fri 13 Nov 14:30 CET: producer prices. Market odds are not a Fed decision.

Second day of chip-led gains

China
Fact

Exports and imports surge, surplus passes one trillion

What happened

Customs data on Tuesday: October exports rose 35.2 percent from a year earlier to $412.3 billion, above a Reuters poll of 33.5 percent and slightly below September’s 36.3 percent. Imports rose 41.6 percent to $307.1 billion, the fastest since 2021, against a 32 percent forecast. The monthly surplus narrowed to $105.3 billion, the lowest since April, and the January–October surplus reached $1.04 trillion. Exports to the US rose 32.9 percent.

Context (what it sits on)

AI hardware such as chips and servers drives exports, while imports were lifted by tech goods, copper and energy; CNBC calculates high-tech imports up 72 percent by value. Yesterday’s data showed factory-gate prices up 5.7 percent with consumer inflation at 0.7 percent, so domestic demand is still weak. The EU’s surplus with China widened to $310.7 billion over ten months. Capital Economics estimates a seasonally adjusted 2 percent monthly fall in exports, suggesting momentum is slowing.

Forward (months–years)

Next week: Xi hosts Trump at the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in China. The US-China trade truce runs to January.

Ukraine
Mixed

Abu Dhabi talks open

What happened

Two days of three-way talks between the US, Russia and Ukraine begin in Abu Dhabi today, the first substantive round since February. US officials say they want a “final end” to the war and are proposing a new “long-term security architecture” between Russia and Europe; they also say they discussed with Russia security guarantees for Ukraine similar to NATO’s mutual-defence clause. Delegations from Germany, France, the UK, Italy and the EU will meet US negotiators separately.

Context (what it sits on)

Territory, especially Donetsk, and Ukraine’s NATO ambitions remain the main obstacles. Trump said both sides are “tired of fighting” and that Ukraine may end up a “little bit different shape”, implying territorial concessions. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky says battlefield gains strengthen Kyiv’s hand. European officials fear US concessions on NATO’s presence in eastern Europe, echoing Russia’s 2021 demands.

Forward (months–years)

Tue 10–Wed 11 Nov: talks. No agreement is assumed. Watch for any joint statement, prisoner exchanges or a leaders’ meeting announcement.

France
Fact

Paris blames Iran for supermarket hack

What happened

The foreign ministry formally attributed the 11–12 October cyberattack, which remotely raised refrigerator temperatures at more than 700 supermarket sites, to the Shahid Kaveh unit of Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps. France summoned Iran’s ambassador, has opened a judicial investigation and says it will seek EU sanctions and other proportionate responses. In Parliament, the Senate is examining the 2027 social security budget after the National Assembly rejected it.

Context (what it sits on)

An internal EU assessment seen by Reuters says a covert Russian network exploited last month’s riots in France on social media. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu lacks a majority and may have to choose between forcing the budget through with Article 49.3, which risks a no-confidence vote, or budget by decree (ordinances). Judo champion Teddy Riner has entered the 2027 presidential race, which President Emmanuel Macron welcomed.

Forward (months–years)

Fri 20 Nov: Senate starts the state budget. Watch for EU sanctions proposals on Iran and Iran’s response.

COP31 climate talks
Fact

UN climate summit opens in Türkiye

What happened

The 31st UN climate conference (COP31) opened in Antalya on Monday 9 November and is due to close on Friday 20 November. Türkiye’s environment minister Murat Kurum holds the presidency while Australia’s climate minister Chris Bowen leads negotiations under a co-presidency agreed last year. UN climate chief Simon Stiell said progress is “nowhere near far enough or fair enough”.

Context (what it sits on)

Organisers expect no major upgrade in emissions targets; the focus is on implementing existing pledges, climate finance for developing countries, adaptation and a just transition. Bowen wants to build a presidency decision from the start rather than in the final days. A strong El Niño is forecast to worsen extreme weather in coming months.

Forward (months–years)

Fri 20 Nov: scheduled close. Watch for finance pledges and whether a draft decision appears early.

Europe
Mixed

EU pushes back on a new security order

What happened

At the G7 foreign ministers’ meeting in Paris on Monday, EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas said Europe already has a security architecture and that the problem is Russia not respecting it, responding to US proposals ahead of Abu Dhabi. Forward (months–years)

Watch EU reaction to any Abu Dhabi outcome and whether ministers take up Iran cyber sanctions.

AI
Mixed

Google’s Gemini 4 Argon for robots

What happened

Google released Gemini 4 Argon on 9 November, describing it as a general physical-reasoning model for robot control, building simulations from video, labelling robot footage and improving robot control policies. It is available to Google AI Ultra subscribers and through the paid Gemini API. Google reports 87 percent on the ERQA (Embodied Reasoning Question Answering) benchmark without helper tools and says that leads other frontier models.

Context (what it sits on)

The benchmark lead is a company claim. InfoQ notes the demonstrations rely on long, task-specific prompts and helper code, and Google does not publish failure rates for most demos. The release adds to competition with specialised robot-control models from Nvidia and others.

Forward (months–years)

Watch independent evaluations and developer uptake.

Morocco
Fact

Health strike and a post-UN Western Sahara

What happened

The CDT health union announced a national strike for Tuesday 10 November over disputed government commitments and budget cuts (Morocco World News). The UN mission in Western Sahara (MINURSO) ended after the Security Council did not renew its mandate; the Royal Palace said on 4 November Morocco remains committed to the ceasefire (Reuters).

The head of government and eight ministers have resigned their parliamentary seats to comply with rules on holding both roles.

Forward (months–years)
