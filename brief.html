Asian markets (lead)
Fact

Taiwan plunges 4.4 percent as AI doubts and Fed signals collide

What happened

Official Thursday closes: Taiwan's main index (Taiex) fell 1,768.02 points, or 4.40 percent, to 38,426.89, its seventh-largest points drop on record, on turnover of about 895 billion Taiwan dollars (Taiwan Stock Exchange via Economic Daily News). It fell as low as 38,259.70 during the day and broke below its one-year average. Chip giant TSMC fell 3.45 percent to 2,100 Taiwan dollars, and the smaller-company index fell 7.22 percent. South Korea's Kospi rose 30.36 points, or 0.60 percent, to 5,097.80. Japan's Nikkei 225 rose 419.93 points, or 0.71 percent, to 59,444.21, while the broader Topix slipped 0.04 percent to 3,822.47.

Context (what it sits on)

Local analysts cited four pressures: Fed minutes pointing to more rate rises, Nvidia's slide despite strong results, continued foreign selling and heavy borrowed-money (margin) positions being unwound. Reuters said MSCI's emerging Asia index hit its lowest since early April and that money is being moved to make room for Anthropic shares. Wednesday's closes in our last brief (Nikkei 59,024.28, Kospi 5,067.44, Taiex 40,194.91) are confirmed by today's point changes; no correction needed.

Forward (months–years)

Taiwan is the most AI-heavy major market, so it moves first when investors question AI valuations. Watch whether European chip stocks follow at the open, Anthropic's first trade this afternoon and whether Nvidia steadies in New York.

AI companies
Fact

Anthropic prices record listing above range; first trade today

What happened

Anthropic said on Wednesday it priced its initial public offering (IPO) at $83 a share, above the $70 to $78 range. The offering is 980 million Class A shares: 956,351,354 sold by Anthropic and 23,648,646 by existing shareholders, with underwriters holding an option to buy up to 147 million more (company release). The total offering is about $81.3 billion. Anthropic itself receives about $79.4 billion before fees, and the rest goes to the selling shareholders. AP and Reuters put the valuation at about $1.5 trillion, the largest IPO on record, ahead of SpaceX's. A filing late on Wednesday registered about 178 million extra shares at $83.

Context (what it sits on)

The $81.3 billion is money raised, not the company's value. Shares are due to begin trading on Nasdaq under the symbol ANT at about 15:30 CET today, and the sale is due to close on Fri 20 Nov; neither has happened yet. The final prospectus is not yet filed. Anthropic is also a major customer of SpaceX's AI computing unit (see SpaceX).

Forward (months–years)

The first trade is the market's verdict on frontier AI developers that are still spending heavily. A strong debut could pull money from chip stocks in the short run, which may already be happening in Asia. Watch the opening price, how it closes against $83, and any underwriter option exercise within 30 days.

Nvidia
Mixed

Results bounce fades; analysts trim targets

What happened

Nvidia closed Wednesday's regular session at $150.96, down $4.50 or 2.90 percent, on about 345 million shares, roughly twice normal (company investor page). It opened at $159.19 and gave back the whole after-hours rise that followed Tuesday's results. Chip rivals rose: AMD 5.31 percent to $530.14 and Broadcom 3.64 percent to $354.05. Nvidia's after-hours and overnight prices (about $152) are not a close.

Context (what it sits on)

Several banks cut price targets while raising earnings forecasts. LSEG says the average target fell to about $252 from about $266 (claims of analysts). At a staff meeting on Wednesday, interim chief executive and finance chief Colette Kress reportedly said pay reviews would be moved up to December (claim, press reports; the company declined to comment). Jensen Huang resigned per the Form 8-K of 12 November, effective 11 November. Harvey Jones is board chair.

Forward (months–years)

Investors are pricing smaller rewards for strong growth, in part because of China export curbs and leadership uncertainty. The Commerce Department's rule on remote access to advanced chips is scheduled for the Federal Register today, taking effect on publication. Huang's House interviews are fixed for Tue 1 Dec. A Senate Banking appearance on Thu 3 Dec is a claim from the chair's spokesperson.

US markets
Fact

Fed minutes signal more rate rises; S&P down five days

What happened

Wednesday closes: Dow Jones Industrial Average 50,198.17, down 561.25 points or 1.11 percent; S&P 500 7,323.63, down 0.23 percent and a fifth straight fall, its longest losing run since June; Nasdaq Composite 24,429.88, up 0.42 percent (AP, CNBC, Yahoo). Minutes of the Federal Reserve's 27 to 28 October meeting, released Wednesday, said almost all officials judged that further rate rises would likely be appropriate, with a couple floating a half-point move in December (Federal Reserve, CNBC).

Context (what it sits on)

The Fed held its rate at 3.75 to 4 percent in October, with two dissents in favour of a rise. Officials called AI-related demand a material inflation risk and worried about opaque AI debt. Governor Christopher Waller repeated on CNBC that he favours a half-point rise. CME FedWatch put a quarter-point rise in December at 95.4 percent and a half-point at 4.6 percent in an 18 November snapshot taken before the minutes. The Fed raised rates in September, so a rise in December would be the second.

Forward (months–years)

Today: weekly jobless claims and the Philadelphia Fed factory survey at 14:30 CET, and Walmart results before the US open. Separately, the Associated Press called Alaska's Senate race for Democrat Mary Peltola over Republican Dan Sullivan on Tuesday. That gives Democrats 56 seats in the Senate from January (Roll Call count).

Energy
Fact

Big US stock build pushes oil lower

What happened

Wednesday settles: January Brent fell $1.19, or 1.28 percent, to $91.95 a barrel; December West Texas Intermediate (WTI), the US benchmark, fell $2.75, or 3.2 percent, to $83.26, its lowest since 26 August (Reuters, Xinhua, DTN). The Energy Information Administration (EIA) said US crude stocks rose 3.4 million barrels to 417.8 million in the week to 13 November, against a Reuters poll forecast of 58,000 barrels. Gasoline stocks rose 5.2 million barrels and distillates 1.6 million.

Context (what it sits on)

The gap between January Brent and December WTI widened to about $8.93, as high tanker costs make US exports less attractive. Both benchmarks are down about 20 percent this month as Gulf exports recover. December WTI is the front month until it expires on Fri 20 Nov; January WTI then becomes the reference.

Forward (months–years)

Iranian attacks on Gulf states (see Iran and the Gulf) are the main upside risk; inventories and the contract roll are the near-term drivers. Lower fuel prices ease inflation, but Fed officials say that alone will not stop them raising rates.

APEC summit in Shenzhen
Mixed

Trump meets Xi and Putin; three-way talks pending

What happened

Trump and Xi met for over an hour at a resort in Shenzhen on Thursday on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit, their third meeting this year (Reuters, CNA, Xinhua). Trump said a Board of Trade set up in May had brought balance to a lopsided relationship. Xi said ties were stable and improving. Trump also met Putin for about 35 to 40 minutes; Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov said Ukraine was the main topic and the meeting went very well (claim).

Context (what it sits on)

No trade, chip or Ukraine agreement was announced as of this morning. The US-China trade truce expires on 10 January. US limits on AI chip sales to China and Chinese controls on critical mineral exports are the main disputes. Xi pledged $20 million for APEC cooperation. Elon Musk was in the US delegation; no Tesla driver-assistance approval in China came out of it.

Forward (months–years)

The first three-way meeting of the US, Chinese and Russian leaders is due later today. AP expects nuclear arms control, Ukraine and Iran to top the agenda. Analysts expect no major deals (Reuters). Watch for any joint statement and any change to chip export policy.

Ukraine and Russia
Mixed

Kerch bridge hit again; Moscow targets the Netherlands

What happened

Crimea's Moscow-installed head Sergei Aksyonov said the Kerch bridge was attacked again on Wednesday and traffic was halted; the BBC reports it is the third attack this week. Destinus, a Dutch-headquartered start-up, confirmed its Ruta cruise missiles were the only foreign-made weapons used in Tuesday's strike, which brought down a road section. Russia's embassy in The Hague asked whether the Netherlands was 'openly joining the conflict' (Guardian, Reuters). Ukraine also hit two refineries in Russia's Krasnodar region, and a Russian strike on Odesa killed four.

Context (what it sits on)

US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner are expected in Berlin today for talks with Chancellor Friedrich Merz and the 'Coalition of the Willing' of about 36 countries backing Ukraine, on post-war security guarantees. President Volodymyr Zelensky says he wants more air defence and help for the power grid.

Forward (months–years)

Russia's warning raises the stakes for European arms makers whose weapons are used inside Russian-held territory. Watch the Berlin outcome against what Trump and Putin say after Shenzhen.

Europe
Fact

Commission proposes 45 billion euro loan for Ukraine

What happened

The European Commission proposed on Wednesday up to 45 billion euros in extra support for Ukraine in 2027, covering about two-thirds of an estimated 70 billion euro budget gap (Commission, Reuters). Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said Ukraine would repay only once Russia pays reparations. The Commission also welcomed a provisional deal between Parliament and member governments on faster permits for power grids and clean-energy projects.

Context (what it sits on)

The loan needs member governments' approval; leaders discuss it next week. It follows Tuesday's autumn forecast, which put EU inflation above 3 percent this year.

Forward (months–years)

Watch for opposition from Hungary or others and how the loan is funded, both of which decide whether it can be agreed before Ukraine's money runs short.

Iran and the Gulf
Mixed

Iran hits Kuwait and Bahrain after US strike

What happened

Iran's army said it targeted Ali Al Salem air base in Kuwait, which hosts US forces, and launched several attacks on Bahrain on Wednesday, after US jets struck a Revolutionary Guards missile site on Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz on Tuesday (AFP, BBC, LA Times). It was the first drone attack on Kuwait in about two months. Kuwait and Bahrain said they intercepted the drones. US Central Command said no American personnel were hurt this week.

Forward (months–years)

Further attacks would test the oil market's calm. Watch whether Gulf shipping or oil sites are hit and whether the three-way Shenzhen meeting addresses Iran.

Myanmar
Fact

World court rules today on Rohingya genocide case

What happened

The International Court of Justice (ICJ), the UN's top court, reads its judgment at 10:00 CET today in the case brought by The Gambia accusing Myanmar of genocide against the Rohingya, a mostly Muslim minority, with 11 states intervening (ICJ notice). The ruling has not been delivered yet.

Context (what it sits on)

The case followed the army's 2017 crackdown that drove more than 700,000 Rohingya into Bangladesh. To find genocide, the court must find intent to destroy the group in whole or in part. Its rulings are final and binding, but it cannot enforce them (AP).

Forward (months–years)

It is the first full genocide judgment in years and is being watched for its bearing on other cases, including South Africa's case against Israel over Gaza.

India
Fact

Cyclone Akvan heads for Odisha

What happened

The India Meteorological Department (IMD) says severe cyclonic storm Akvan is moving north over the Bay of Bengal at about 12 km/h and should cross the coast between Narsapur in Andhra Pradesh and Puri in Odisha, most likely near Gopalpur, around midday Friday. Odisha has evacuated about 50,000 people, West Bengal has shut schools, and a red alert covers north Andhra Pradesh, Yanam and south Odisha (The Hindu, India Today).

Context (what it sits on)

The track has shifted repeatedly; earlier forecasts put landfall further south in Andhra Pradesh. Forecasters do not expect it to strengthen further.

Forward (months–years)

Watch rainfall and storm surge at landfall and rail and port closures. Early evacuations have kept death tolls low in recent Odisha cyclones.

France
Fact

Lecornu to list budget compromise priorities

What happened

Public accounts minister David Amiel said Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu will list 'in the coming days' the priority topics for a 2027 budget compromise with the centre-right and centre-left (AFP). The Senate adopted the end-of-year 2026 spending adjustment bill by 228 votes to 109, after the National Assembly rejected it.

Science and technology
Fact

Gut viruses and early human finds

What happened

Researchers reported 'egoviruses' living in the guts of millions of people, which could be an evolutionary link between giant viruses that infect single-celled organisms and those that infect humans (Nature). Fossil teeth from south China suggest the early human Homo erectus lived in rainforest, not only woodland and savannah (Nature).

Context (what it sits on)

Both are early findings that need follow-up.

Forward (months–years)

The virus work could change how scientists trace the origins of human viruses.
