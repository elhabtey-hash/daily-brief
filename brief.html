AI
Mixed

Riemann hypothesis: Anthropic mathematician claims proof with Claude; Lean groups certify it

What happened

On Friday 20 Nov Levent Alpöge, a number theorist on leave from Harvard who works at Anthropic, posted a note saying that, working with Anthropic's AI model Claude over about six weeks, he had found a proof of the Riemann hypothesis. He published the proof in Lean, a programming language whose checker verifies each logical step (Nature, Scientific American, Guardian). On Saturday the Lean Focused Research Organization and the Mathlib Initiative said in a joint statement: 'We have full confidence that Alpöge's proof of the Riemann Hypothesis is correct.' MIT mathematician Andrew Sutherland said he compiled it independently and the chance anything is wrong is 'approximately zero' (Scientific American).

Context (what it sits on)

The Riemann hypothesis, posed in 1859, predicts a hidden regularity in how prime numbers are spread out, and it is one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems with a 1 million dollar award from the Clay Mathematics Institute. The proof used a non-public version of Claude and is extremely long; Nature quotes Fields medallist James Maynard as expecting it to be correct but saying mathematicians will take a long time to understand it. Andrew Granville of the University of Montreal called the 4-page human summary 'garbled' and questioned the timing, a day after Anthropic's stock market debut; Alpöge rejects that view. The same day, Stanford's Ravi Vakil posted a separate, entirely human-written draft proof by Alessandro Masullo, which still needs checking.

Forward (months–years)

Formal machine checking is a strong form of evidence, but the Clay prize also requires publication and two years of acceptance, so the 'solved' status will be settled by the mathematical community over months. The wider point: AI systems are moving from helping with routine maths to producing results at the frontier, which affects how research is credited and checked. Watch for a readable write-up, any Clay Mathematics Institute statement and expert review of the Masullo draft. Links: AI companies (Anthropic), Markets.

Fact

Cook makes the case for more rises; December move largely priced

What happened

Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook said on Friday, in a speech in Santiago, Chile, that the current policy rate is not high enough and that 'waiting longer to implement rate increases risks getting behind the curve' (Federal Reserve text; Reuters). She said there is 'little evidence inflation is declining'. CME FedWatch, which turns futures prices into odds, showed on Sun 22 Nov a 91.4 percent chance of a quarter-point rise at the Tue 8 to Wed 9 Dec meeting, 8.6 percent for no change and 0 percent for a half-point (CME FedWatch figures as relayed by CoinNess; the CME page itself was not read).

Context (what it sits on)

JPMorgan now expects further quarter-point rises in January and March (Reuters via Quartz).

Forward (months–years)

COP31
Fact

UN climate talks in Antalya collapse without a deal

What happened

The COP31 summit in Turkey ended on Sunday 22 Nov without a political agreement after the European Union and others rejected a final text that made no mention of fossil fuels. Turkey's conference president Murat Kurum said he would not table a new text. It ran two days over schedule, the longest COP on record (Reuters, UN News, BBC).

Context (what it sits on)

The last time UN climate talks failed to adopt a deal was Copenhagen in 2009. Saudi Arabia and other oil producers pushed to keep fossil fuels out; poorer countries objected to vague finance, a tripling of adaptation money with no baseline. The draft also backed carbon removal and raising electricity's share of energy use to 35 percent by 2035. Earlier agreements, including Paris, still stand.

Forward (months–years)

Without a new deal, pressure shifts to national climate plans and to coalitions outside the UN process. Watch the EU's next steps and the choice of COP32 arrangements. Links: Europe, Energy.

Science
Fact

BepiColombo enters orbit around Mercury

What happened

The European Space Agency (ESA) said on Saturday 21 Nov that BepiColombo's European-built transfer module has inserted the spacecraft into orbit around Mercury after an eight-year trip. It is only the second mission to orbit the innermost planet, after NASA's MESSENGER in 2011 to 2015 (Nature).

Context (what it sits on)

The craft carries two probes, ESA's Mercury Planetary Orbiter and Japan's (JAXA) Mio. They stay joined until Mio reaches its orbit on 9 December; the ESA probe then lowers itself until March 2027 to study the surface. Mio will study Mercury's magnetic field and the solar wind.

Forward (months–years)

Watch the separation around 9 December. Links: SpaceX (space), AI (another big science weekend).
