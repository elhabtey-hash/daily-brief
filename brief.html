Global markets
Fact

Asia rebounds after Thursday's chip crash, but mainland China and Indonesia fall hard

What happened

On Friday 16 October South Korea's Kospi closed at about 5,738, up roughly 3.3 percent from Thursday's close of 5,554.22, which was a fall of about 7.6 percent after circuit breakers halted trading (Yahoo Finance data). Japan's Nikkei 225 rose about 1.75 percent to roughly 65,550 from Thursday's close of 64,425.95, and Samsung Electronics, SK Hynix and Kioxia led the recovery (Investing.com). Taiwan's index rose about 1 percent and Hong Kong's Hang Seng about 1 percent, but mainland China fell, with the Shanghai Composite down more than 2 percent and the Shenzhen Component down roughly 3 to 4 percent depending on the source, led by AI-hardware names such as Eoptolink (Caixin, Business Upturn). Indonesia's Jakarta Composite dropped about 11 percent on its own news (see Indonesia). India's Nifty was volatile after Thursday's rally.

Context (what it sits on)

Thursday's crash came from hot US inflation, bets on further Federal Reserve rate rises, worries about debt-funded AI building and a claimed memory-chip advance by China's CXMT. Friday's rebound followed Wall Street's recovery on Thursday and falling bond yields, not a change in those worries. Correction to Thursday's brief: it gave the Nikkei close as 64,778.92, down about 4.4 percent, which was an intraday level. The official close was 64,425.95, down 4.90 percent, the third-largest single-day point drop on record (Nikkei Indexes, Kyodo).

Forward (months–years)

A second day of gains would suggest the fear is easing, while another hot inflation print or a new credit scare would revive it. TSMC's strong guidance did not stop its shares edging lower in Taipei, which shows how sensitive chip stocks are to financing worries.

Energy
Fact

Thursday settles: Brent December 105.92 dollars, WTI November 97.97 dollars

What happened

December Brent settled on Thursday 15 October at 105.92 dollars a barrel, down 14 cents (0.13 percent) from Wednesday's 106.06, and November West Texas Intermediate settled at 97.97 dollars, up 2.88 dollars (3.03 percent) from Wednesday's 95.09 (Reuters, ICE and NYMEX data). Context (what it sits on)

The gap between Brent December and WTI November has widened to about 8 dollars as cheaper US shipping lifts demand for American crude. Gulf of Mexico shut-ins after Hurricane Isaias were 9.92 percent of oil output on 14 October, and production has recovered to nearly 90 percent of normal (US regulator). The Trump administration is also weighing case-by-case licences for sanctioned tankers to move non-sanctioned oil, which analysts say would free few ships (Reuters).

Forward (months–years)

WTI November expires on Tue 20 October and Brent December on Fri 30 October, so quoted front-month prices will roll. A Hormuz escalation would be the main upside risk and a further stock release the main downside.

Iran
Mixed

Cape Dao and GFS Pride sink near Hormuz and another tanker burns as Trump rules out an attack before the midterms

What happened

The bulk carrier Cape Dao, flagged in Antigua and Barbuda, sank on 14 October weeks after a 23 September projectile strike that killed one crew member (TradeWinds, MarineRadar). The container ship GFS Pride sank on Wednesday with all 21 crew rescued, and the Revolutionary Guard says a mine did it while maritime sources point to a projectile (Euronews). On Thursday Iranian state media said an oil tanker with its tracking switched off caught fire in the Gulf of Oman after entering a Revolutionary Guard 'prohibited zone', the second such incident in 24 hours. The ship's name, flag and the cause were not given and the US Central Command had not commented. Maritime monitors say at least 10 ships have been struck since Sunday and further attacks are highly likely, while the US Central Command says more than 40 ships transited in 24 hours (CNN, CBS). Trump said the US would not attack Iran before the 3 November midterms, and indirect talks continue through Pakistan and Qatar (Al Jazeera).

Context (what it sits on)

Iran declared the strait closed at the start of the war in late February and the US answered with a naval blockade. Both sides now say they control the waterway. The tanker fire has not been independently confirmed, and the Iranian version, that ships break rules, is a claim.

Forward (months–years)

Watch for a US reply on the talks, any insurer or shipping-line suspension of Hormuz transits, and a US decision on shadow-fleet licences.

European Union
Mixed

EU summit fails to agree the 2028 to 2034 budget; Ukraine, defence and the Middle East on day two

What happened

At the European Council in Brussels on Thursday 15 October, leaders adopted conclusions on competitiveness, energy costs, the next multiannual financial framework (the EU's seven-year budget) and enlargement, published at 00:40 on Friday (Council). On energy they backed short-term measures including state aid, refinery optimisation, demand pooling and faster renewables, grids and storage. On the budget they did not agree. The Irish presidency's proposal to cut the total by 141 billion euros was largely rejected and Council President António Costa will take over the talks with a new proposal in a few weeks (Euronews). A special summit is set for 26 November. Today's second day, from 10:00, covers Ukraine, defence and security, the Middle East and migration, and conclusions on those topics were not yet published at filing. Draft texts reaffirm support for Ukraine, call for de-escalation and freedom of navigation in the Middle East, and ask Iran and the Houthis to stop attacks on shipping (draft, Politico).

Context (what it sits on)

The conclusions say an agreement before the end of 2026 would allow laws to pass in 2027 so funds reach recipients in January 2028, but the wording was softened from an explicit aim of agreement by year-end. Several leaders, including Sweden's and Italy's, said they do not feel bound by December. Elections in France, Italy, Poland and other countries in 2027 add pressure.

Forward (months–years)

Special EU summit on Thu 26 November. EU-Gulf Cooperation Council summit on Sat 24 October. Friday's conclusions should be on the Council website later today.

AI models
Fact

Meta releases open-weight Muse Spark Open and Apple says it runs on Mac Studios

What happened

Meta released Muse Spark Open on 15 October, an open-weight multimodal model with 816 billion total parameters, 42 billion active per token, a one-million-token context window and weights on Hugging Face (Meta). It takes text, image, video and audio and outputs text, and is aimed at coding and long-running agent work. Apple and Meta said developers can run it locally by linking two to four Mac Studio computers with the M5 Ultra chip, and a 256 gigabyte machine costs at least 10,799 dollars (CNBC). Meta's AI chief Alexandr Wang said it is not the company's most capable model, which is the proprietary Muse Wonder released earlier this week.

Context (what it sits on)

Open-weight means anyone can download the model, though Meta's licence allows only limited commercial use. The Apple tie-up is unusual after years of rivalry and shows interest in running powerful AI locally instead of in data centres. Google's Gemini 4 Argon was made available to everyone on 13 October.

Forward (months–years)

Independent benchmark results should appear in the coming days. They will test Meta's claims.
