AI sell-off
Fact

Tech rout deepens into month end

What happened

On Friday 30 October the S&P 500 fell 1.82 percent to 7,580.07, the Nasdaq 2.86 percent to 25,437.00 and the Dow 1.09 percent to 51,770.31 (AP, closing data). Nvidia closed at 204.64 dollars, the chip index (SOX) at 10,688.80, Palantir fell 6.27 percent to 184.11 dollars and SpaceX (SPCX) ended at 134.72 dollars (tracker notes). Reuters reported that the cost of insuring company debt against default widened sharply last week on worries about negative free cash flow at the big spenders. This morning US stock futures are up about 0.4 percent for the S&P and 0.6 to 0.9 percent for the Nasdaq (indicative, not a close).

Context (what it sits on)

After results from Alphabet, Microsoft and Amazon, investors no longer reward every dollar of AI spending. Goldman estimates the largest cloud companies will spend about 800 billion dollars in 2026 and 1.2 trillion in 2027, which is why cash flow and borrowing now drive the shares.

Forward (months–years)

Apple and Palantir tonight, AMD Tuesday 3 November, Arm and Qualcomm Wednesday 4 November, CoreWeave Tuesday 10 November, Nvidia Tuesday 17 November.

US midterms
Speculation

Control of Congress at stake on Tuesday

What happened

All 435 House seats and 35 Senate seats are up on Tuesday 3 November. Independent forecast models published over the weekend give Democrats roughly a 91 to 95 percent chance of winning the House and about 76 to 86 percent for the Senate, with Alaska, Iowa, Kansas, Maine, Ohio and Texas the closest Senate races (Silver Bulletin and other models). Results arrive overnight Paris time, and close races can take days.

Context (what it sits on)

A Democratic House would end the President's single-party control and could slow tax, tariff and spending plans, and open investigations. Reuters notes Democratic gains could add hurdles for data-centre projects, and US officials have told Gulf allies to be ready for possible strikes on Iran after the vote.

Forward (months–years)

Polls close from about 00:00 Paris on Wednesday 4 November. Forecasts are probabilities, not results.

Iran and the Gulf
Mixed

Tanker attacks and Qatari talks

What happened

A tanker was struck by unidentified projectiles near Saudi Arabia's Ras al-Khafji on Sunday 1 November with no injuries (Iran International), and more tankers were reported hit in the Strait of Hormuz (Al Jazeera). Saudi air defences intercepted a Houthi drone and missile aimed at the south of the kingdom. Iran and the US are not in direct talks, but Qatari mediators are discussing a plan to reopen Hormuz and end the war, and Iran said on Monday the talks are making progress, with no deal reached (Newsquawk, CNBC). President Trump again said the conflict would end soon.

Context (what it sits on)

The Strait of Hormuz carries about a fifth of the world's oil, so each attack or each hint of a deal moves prices and inflation everywhere. A deal would ease pressure on central banks, while a renewed US campaign after the midterms would do the opposite.

Forward (months–years)

Any Qatari announcement, Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia, US moves after Tuesday's vote.

Energy
Fact

December Brent expires at a six-month high

What happened

On Friday 30 October the expiring December Brent contract settled up 2.76 dollars at 117.64 dollars a barrel, its highest settle since late April, after a tanker attack in Hormuz (Reuters, ICE). January Brent, now the headline contract, settled at 113.43 dollars, up 2.63. December WTI, the US front month, settled at 104.12 dollars, up 2.12. In October, Brent gained 13.6 percent and WTI 15.2 percent. This morning January Brent is trading around 110 dollars, down about 3 percent, on the Qatari talks (intraday, not a settle).

Context (what it sits on)

Oil has been above 100 dollars since the Iran war began, which keeps inflation high and the US, eurozone and Japanese central banks leaning towards rate rises. The gap between December and January Brent shows how tight supply is right now.

Forward (months–years)

Monday 2 November settles tonight. December WTI stays front month until it expires on Thursday 19 November.

US economy
Fact

Fed odds ease slightly before the December meeting

What happened

After Thursday's data, September inflation on the Fed's preferred PCE measure at 3.6 percent and growth at a 3.4 percent annual rate, futures put the chance of a quarter-point Federal Reserve rise at its Wednesday 9 December decision at roughly 71 to 72 percent, down from about 85 percent after the Fed held at 3.75 to 4.00 percent (CME FedWatch, Reuters). Forward (months–years)

Apple and Palantir
Fact

Two results tonight, both pending

What happened

Apple reports its fiscal fourth quarter (July to September) after the US close today, with analysts expecting revenue of about 113.3 billion dollars, up about 11 percent, and earnings of about 1.98 dollars a share (consensus via Yahoo and AlphaStreet). The focus is iPhone sales of about 57 billion dollars, services growth near 10 percent, and the gross margin as memory chips get dearer, in the first results under new chief executive John Ternus (Reuters preview). Palantir also reports after the close with its shareholder letter, headline figures expected around 22:00 Paris and the webcast at 23:00. It guided third-quarter revenue of 2.160 to 2.164 billion dollars against consensus of about 2.17 to 2.18 billion, with earnings of about 0.42 dollars a share expected and a full-year guide of about 8.15 billion dollars (Palantir, tracker).

Context (what it sits on)

Apple is the biggest test yet of how rising memory prices, a side-effect of the AI boom, hit consumer devices. Palantir is the cleanest read on whether companies are paying for AI software, after its shares fell 6.27 percent on Friday.

Forward (months–years)

Watch Palantir's US commercial growth and any rise to its full-year guide, and Apple's December-quarter outlook.

Israel
Fact

Herzog opens coalition talks, pushes unity

What happened

The final count left Netanyahu's bloc with 53 of 120 seats, the Jewish-led opposition with 48 and the Arab-led Joint List and Ra'am with 19 (Times of Israel). President Isaac Herzog invited every party to consultations from Wednesday 4 November and urged leaders to compromise and avoid another election, signalling support for a unity government (Times of Israel, NBC). The Joint List and Ra'am said they will recommend Gadi Eisenkot for prime minister (Jerusalem Post).

Context (what it sits on)

No side can reach 61 seats without partners it has ruled out, so a unity deal or a fresh election are the main paths. The outcome shapes Israel's stance on Iran, Gaza and Lebanon.

Forward (months–years)

Consultations Wednesday 4 November. Herzog then chooses who gets the first try at forming a government.

Western Sahara
Fact

UN mission ends after Security Council deadlock

What happened

On Friday 30 October the Security Council rejected a US text to renew the UN mission in Western Sahara (MINURSO) by 11 votes for, 1 against (Russia's veto) and 3 abstentions (UN). A rival Russian text for a one-year renewal got only 3 votes in favour. The mandate expired at midnight on Saturday 31 October (UN News). The Polisario Front welcomed the outcome, while the US and Morocco argued the mission's referendum purpose no longer fits reality.

Context (what it sits on)

Morocco's autonomy plan has strong US and European backing, and the end of the mission removes a UN presence that dated from 1991. The risk is more friction between Morocco and Algeria and a gap in ceasefire monitoring.

Forward (months–years)

UN statements on how peace efforts continue, any new draft in the Council.

Tesla
Mixed

Cybercab answers unseen, China incentives end

What happened

Tesla's sworn answers to the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) on whether the Cybercab meets federal vehicle safety standards were due on Friday 30 October, and nothing has been published (NHTSA file AQ26002, tracker). China's car purchase incentives ended on Saturday 31 October. Texas now lists 1,244 authorised Tesla robotaxis, 573 Cybercabs and 671 Model Ys, which are permits, not rides (TxDMV). A regulator's finding against the Cybercab, which has no steering wheel, would slow Tesla's robotaxi plans. The proposed Tesla-SpaceX combination remains under study only. No terms exist.

SpaceX
Fact

Starlink 15-18 flies, USSF-432 slips a day

What happened

Starlink 15-18 lifted off from Vandenberg, California, at 05:04 Paris on Saturday 31 October with 27 satellites, and its booster landed on the droneship (SpaceX, Spaceflight Now). It was the 700th Falcon 9 launch ever. The military USSF-432 mission moved to Wednesday 4 November, 02:28 to 06:28 Paris. Reports say SpaceX told small Moon-lander companies it will no longer sell rides to the Moon, and a SpaceX letter to the aviation regulator (FAA) foresees 10,000 launches a year (press, unconfirmed by SpaceX).

Context (what it sits on)

SpaceX is winding down Falcon in favour of Starship, whose Flight 15 is pencilled for about Thursday 12 November without a launch licence yet. The ~40 billion dollar Apollo GPU lease is a commitment, expected to close by year end.

Forward (months–years)

USSF-432 Wednesday 4 November, Grain 800 MHz notice to the FCC Thursday 5 November, Starlink 15-21 Friday 6 November, FAA Starbase meetings 17 to 19 November.

AI
Fact

OpenAI and Google ship new models

What happened

OpenAI released GPT-6.1 Terra, a fast model for operating computers and visual reasoning, priced at 1 dollar per million input tokens (chunks of text) and 5 dollars output, half the earlier price, with a context window of over a million tokens (OpenAI via press). Google DeepMind's Gemini 4 Carbon, aimed at cybersecurity and long coding tasks, remains limited to selected defenders, and Google showed Genie 4, a real-time interactive world model, to trusted testers.

Context (what it sits on)

Prices for capable models keep falling fast, which spreads AI use but squeezes the companies selling it.

Forward (months–years)

Anthropic's listing: roadshow possibly from Tuesday 10 November, pricing perhaps the week after (IFR, Claim).

Ukraine
Fact

Oleshky truce ends, strikes on power

What happened

The six-day local ceasefire around Russian-occupied Oleshky in the Kherson region, the longest of the war, ended on Sunday 1 November after Russia declined an extension (BBC, AP). About 800 residents were evacuated during it, with no violations reported. Russian strikes cut power to tens of thousands of homes across Ukraine (DW).

Context (what it sits on)

The truce had been seen as a possible model for wider pauses, so its end is a step back as winter begins.

Forward (months–years)

Russian winter strikes on energy, EU funding for Ukraine.

Europe
Fact

Second round of EU defence loans opens

What happened

The European Commission opened a second call under its SAFE defence loan scheme on Thursday 29 October, offering 21.3 billion euros of long-term loans to all member states, with applications due Monday 30 November (Commission). It encourages joint buying with Ukraine of air defence, drones and ammunition, and Brussels hopes unspent loans may help fill Ukraine's budget gap (Euronews).

Context (what it sits on)

The EU is borrowing jointly to rearm, a shift from a decade ago, and Ireland is among countries considering a bid (Irish Times).

Forward (months–years)

Applications due Monday 30 November.

The National Assembly's formal vote on the first part of the 2027 finance bill, the revenue side, is set for Tuesday 3 November at 15:00 after questions to the government (Assemblée nationale). If the amendments are not finished on Monday, the vote could be pushed back.

Brazil
Fact

President Lula said on Sunday 1 November that no social programme will be cut in his new term and that new ones may be created, calling reports of cuts to Bolsa Família fake news (Valor, Folha). Markets are still waiting for his finance minister pick, expected after a trip abroad.

Investors worry about Brazil's public debt, so spending promises without a credible finance minister weigh on the real and on bonds.

Forward (months–years)

Cabinet announcements, likely in December.
