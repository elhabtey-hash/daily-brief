Brazil
Fact

Lula wins a fourth term in a historic comeback

What happened

In Sunday's runoff President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva won 51.79 percent of valid votes (64,256,232) against 48.21 percent (59,826,041) for Flavio Bolsonaro, with 100 percent of sections counted (Superior Electoral Court via Guardian, G1, BBC). In the first round on 4 October Lula trailed with 45.16 percent to 47.03 percent. Lula won all nine north-eastern states and Minas Gerais, while Bolsonaro took the South, the Centre-West and Sao Paulo. Bolsonaro has not conceded and called a two-hour extension of voting inexplicable, although both campaigns requested it (BBC).

Context (what it sits on)

Lula gained about 10.4 million votes between rounds, the first turnaround in Brazilian presidential history. The result keeps a left-of-centre government in Latin America's largest economy, with tensions likely with Washington on trade and tariffs and with a right-leaning Congress on spending. Markets will watch the real and Brazilian bonds today for the reaction to fiscal policy.

Forward (months–years)

Bolsonaro's response and any legal challenge, Lula's economic team in the coming weeks, inauguration 1 January 2027.

Iran and the Gulf
Mixed

Talks stall as Houthi strikes on Saudi Arabia intensify

What happened

Iran's foreign ministry spokesman said the window for diplomacy is open and messages pass to the US via intermediaries, but rejected what he called demands for complete surrender (AP, 25 Oct). Iran's ambassador in Islamabad said Tehran would reopen the Strait of Hormuz if Washington ended its naval blockade of Iranian ports, while the Revolutionary Guard-linked Tasnim agency called reports of a new offer lies (AP, Iran International). Trump raised the possibility of strikes as soon as tomorrow, and the US is sending more forces. The Houthis fired at least 12 missiles and two drones at Saudi Arabia since 24 October (Critical Threats), and on Monday claimed hits on the East-West oil pipeline pumping stations and Riyadh airport, unconfirmed by Riyadh (Reuters).

Context (what it sits on)

The basic swap on offer, Hormuz reopened for the blockade lifted, matches the June interim framework brokered by Pakistan and Qatar, but Washington wants nuclear steps first. The East-West pipeline carries Saudi crude to the Red Sea, the main way around Hormuz, so attacks on it hit the world's backup route. Saudi, Pakistani and Turkish officials were due in Tehran on Monday.

Forward (months–years)

Egypt's vote today, Israel's election Tuesday 27 October, US midterms Tuesday 3 November.

Energy
Fact

December Brent closes at a six-month high

What happened

December Brent settled at 116.72 dollars on Friday 23 October, up 3.55 from Thursday's 113.17 and its highest close since 29 April. December WTI settled at 103.27 dollars, up 3.80 from 99.47 (Reuters, CNBC). For the week December Brent rose about 6 percent and December WTI about 2.3 percent. On Monday morning December Brent traded near 114.5 and December WTI near 101 dollars, intraday, after reports that Qatari mediators were in Tehran (Reuters).

Context (what it sits on)

December WTI is the front month. December Brent expires on Friday 30 October, after which January becomes the Brent benchmark, so headline prices may jump on the roll. The gap between Brent and WTI is about 13.5 dollars, a sign the risk is in seaborne Gulf oil. High oil keeps pressure on central banks meeting this week.

Forward (months–years)

December Brent expiry Friday 30 October.

Global markets
Fact

Stocks slip on oil while chips steady

What happened

On Friday 23 October the S&P 500 fell 0.52 percent to 7,623.68, the Nasdaq Composite 0.21 percent to 25,955.34 and the Dow 1.23 percent to 52,215.16 (Reuters, CNBC). The chip index rose 0.4 percent after Thursday's rout, and the energy sector hit a record high. For the week the S&P 500 fell 0.5 percent and the Nasdaq 0.7 percent, while the Dow rose 0.4 percent. US stock futures were slightly higher on Monday morning.

Context (what it sits on)

The sell-off moved from AI stocks into consumer and financial shares as oil rose, which shows the market now fears energy-driven inflation as much as AI funding. About a third of S&P 500 companies report this week.

Forward (months–years)

Microsoft and Alphabet Wednesday 28 October, Amazon and Intel Thursday 29 October.

Central banks
Mixed

Fed, ECB and Bank of Japan all meet this week

What happened

The US Federal Reserve meets Tuesday and Wednesday, with a decision Wednesday 28 October at 19:00 Paris. Markets price about a 90 percent chance of holding rates at 3.75 to 4.00 percent and about 6 to 10 percent for a quarter-point rise (CME FedWatch and Kalshi via trackers, claim). The European Central Bank decides Thursday 29 October and the Bank of Japan Friday 30 October, where Nikkei reports a hold at 1.25 percent, with a December rise expected by many analysts (Reuters).

Context (what it sits on)

The Fed raised rates at its last meeting because US inflation is well above 3 percent. Oil at six-month highs pushes all three banks towards a firmer tone even if they hold. The US 10-year yield is near 5 percent, which raises the cost of financing AI data centres.

Forward (months–years)

Fed Wednesday 28 October, ECB Thursday 29 October, Bank of Japan Friday 30 October.

SpaceX
Claim

Musk says 40 billion dollars of chip debt is closed

What happened

Musk said on X on Sunday that SpaceX has closed 40 billion dollars of mainly callable debt to buy AI chips (GPUs) arriving in the first half of 2027, not a major factor this quarter (Reuters, CNBC, claim). Reuters said it is unclear whether pricing was agreed, and Bloomberg reports a separate vehicle to keep it off the balance sheet. There is no company filing yet. SpaceX shares closed Friday at 147.49 dollars, up 1.9 percent, after Phillip Securities upgraded them to strong buy with a 250 dollar target. About 328 million shares freed on Saturday 24 October can trade from today. Starlink 15-15 launched at 00:48 Paris on Saturday after three scrubs, the 120th Falcon launch of the year.

Context (what it sits on)

The debt would make SpaceX one of the largest single buyers of AI chips, linked to its xAI business, and it eases one of the financing worries that hit chip stocks last week. A board committee is still studying a possible combination with Tesla, and nothing is agreed. New share supply today may weigh on the price.

Forward (months–years)

Starlink 15-16 Tuesday 27 October 05:45 to 09:45 Paris (target), US Federal Communications Commission (FCC) meeting on satellite-to-phone spectrum Thursday 29 October.

Nvidia
Claim

Huang prices agent software, chip results loom

What happened

Nvidia closed Friday at 210.96 dollars, down about 0.6 percent. In a video from its Berlin conference on 22 October, Jensen Huang said running Nvidia's agent software costs about 2,000 dollars per chip per year and that software will become an increasingly major part of the business (Tom's Hardware, claim). No new filing or results-date announcement has appeared, and the 17 November results date rests on the last earnings call.

Context (what it sits on)

Software would give Nvidia recurring revenue beyond hardware sales. SpaceX's reported debt is supportive of 2027 demand, though Nvidia's role is unknown. Memory makers SK hynix and Samsung report this week, a read on supply of the high-bandwidth memory used in Nvidia chips.

Forward (months–years)

SK hynix Wednesday 28 October, Samsung Thursday 29 October, AMD Tuesday 3 November, Nvidia results about Tuesday 17 November (claim).

Tesla
Mixed

First robotaxi safety report, Cybercab answers due Friday

What happened

Tesla published its first robotaxi safety report on 21 October, claiming 3.1 million driverless miles this year without a safety monitor, mostly in Austin and Dallas, and no injuries in collisions where its car made first contact, by its own narrow definition (Not a Tesla App, claim). It named Las Vegas, Jacksonville, Phoenix and San Antonio as next cities without dates. Shares closed Friday at 372.22 dollars.

Context (what it sits on)

Answers to the US road-safety regulator on the Cybercab, which has no steering wheel or pedals, are due Friday 30 October and decide whether Tesla can launch unsupervised rides in Dallas in November as it claims. China's purchase incentives end Saturday 31 October, which may pull sales forward.

Forward (months–years)

NHTSA answers Friday 30 October, China October wholesale about 2 to 4 November, annual meeting Thursday 3 December 22:00 Paris.

Egypt
Fact

Parliament votes on military support for Saudi Arabia

What happened

Egypt's House of Representatives was due to vote on Monday 26 October, in closed session, on President Sisi's request to authorise military support for Saudi Arabia against Houthi attacks. It needs two thirds, 398 of 596 members (Al Jazeera, Masrawy). The vote was postponed from 19 October over concerns about the mission's scope, cost and length.

Context (what it sits on)

A yes would bring Egypt into the Yemen war, partly to protect Red Sea shipping through the Suez Canal, a key source of foreign currency for Egypt. The outcome was pending at the time of writing.

Forward (months–years)

Result expected today.

Israel
Mixed

Election Tuesday with no clear majority in polls

What happened

Israelis vote on Tuesday 27 October. Final polls give Netanyahu's bloc about 50 to 59 seats and the opposition bloc led by former army chief Gadi Eisenkot about 47 to 52, both short of the 61 needed (i24, Times of Israel). Likud and Eisenkot's Yashar party poll about 21 to 23 seats each.

Context (what it sits on)

Arab-backed parties may hold the balance, and a hung result could mean months of coalition talks. The winner shapes Israel's role in any renewed strikes on Iran.

Forward (months–years)

Polls close Tuesday 27 October 21:00 Paris, exit polls then.

Ukraine
Mixed

Local ceasefire in Oleshky as strikes continue

What happened

Ukraine and Russia agreed a local humanitarian ceasefire in occupied Oleshky from Tuesday 27 October for aid and phased evacuation of civilians (Kyiv Post). Overnight strikes killed at least four people in Ukraine and three in Russia (Anadolu), and Zelensky urged faster delivery of Patriot air-defence missiles. Ukraine disputes Musk's claims about Starlink restrictions (AP).

Context (what it sits on)

The Oleshky deal is small but a rare practical agreement before the proposed three-way talks around 10 November. Starlink restrictions also feed European worries about depending on US-owned systems.

Forward (months–years)

Oleshky ceasefire from Tuesday 27 October, talks around 10 November (claim).

France
Mixed

Budget tax debate resumes Tuesday

What happened

The National Assembly resumes debate on the revenue half of the 2027 budget on Tuesday 27 October after the vote was postponed because of the volume of amendments (LCP, Capital). Deputies have already undone many of the main measures. Prime Minister Lecornu says a compromise is still possible (BFM). A formal vote has been reported for Tuesday 3 November (claim).

Context (what it sits on)

Without a majority the government may again need the special constitutional procedure known as 49.3, which risks a no-confidence vote. The deficit path matters for France's borrowing costs.

Forward (months–years)

Debate resumes Tuesday 27 October.
