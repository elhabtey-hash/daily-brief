Fed (lead)
Fact

October inflation edged up and third-quarter growth was revised sharply higher, leaving markets almost sure of a December rate rise

What happened

The Bureau of Economic Analysis released the October personal consumption figures and the second estimate of third-quarter growth on Wed 25 Nov at 14:30 CET, the last major official data before the Federal Reserve meets on Tue 8 Dec and Wed 9 Dec. The personal consumption expenditures price index, the Fed's preferred inflation gauge, rose 0.3 percent in October and 3.8 percent on the year, after 0.5 percent and 3.7 percent in September (BEA). Core prices, which exclude food and energy, also rose 0.3 percent on the month, a third straight such increase, and 3.3 percent on the year, the largest annual rise since November 2023 (Reuters). Personal income rose 0.7 percent, disposable income 0.8 percent and spending 0.3 percent. Real spending was essentially flat and the saving rate was 4.1 percent. The headline inflation figure was in line with the Dow Jones consensus. Core inflation was 0.1 percentage point above the forecast (CNBC). In a separate release, real gross domestic product in the third quarter was revised up to an annual rate of 4.3 percent, 0.9 percentage points above the first estimate of 3.4 percent, led by investment and consumer spending (BEA). Real final sales to private domestic purchasers were revised up to 5.8 percent. After the data, CoinNess reported CME FedWatch at 99.8 percent for a quarter-point rise on 9 Dec and 0.2 percent for no change, up from 97.4 percent on Tuesday evening. Odds of a further 25 basis points of tightening by January 2027 stood at 40.3 percent, and of 50 basis points at 59.6 percent.

Context (what it sits on)

Inflation remains well above the Fed's 2 percent target and has edged further away from it, while the economy was growing much faster than first thought. That combination leaves little in the official data to argue for holding rates in December. A quarter-point rise then would be the second rise since September, after the increase already delivered that month and the pause in October. The previous Daily Brief had marked both releases as not yet published.

Forward (months–years)

Watch the Fed meeting on Tue 8 Dec and Wed 9 Dec. The next official inflation and growth releases are due on Wed 23 Dec. Links: Markets, Energy.

SpaceX
Mixed

Regulator authorises up to one million orbital data-centre satellites; company pledges a share gift to Trump Accounts

What happened

The Federal Communications Commission on Wed 25 Nov adopted and released Order DA 26-1273, authorising SpaceX to deploy a constellation, branded Starmind, of up to 1,000,000 satellites for orbital data centres. The order was signed by the chief of the Space Bureau under delegated authority, not by a full vote of the Commission. It was granted in part and deferred in part: a request to dispose of some satellites without bringing them back into the atmosphere was put off. The licence sets milestones of one satellite by 25 Nov 2033, 10 percent of the system by 2035, 50 percent by 2038 and the full system by 2040, and gives SpaceX 30 days to decline the conditions. Amazon's Leo unit, Viasat and the American Astronomical Society had opposed the application. Earthjustice criticised the decision being issued in a holiday week without a full environmental review. In a separate investor-relations note on 25 Nov, SpaceX said it would gift one newly issued Class A share to every currently active Trump Account, that Elon Musk would match the gift, and that president Gwynne Shotwell would gift over 2 million of her own shares. The company gave no total share count or dollar value, and said the plan depends on approvals. No current report, or 8-K, had been filed on the gift when this brief was written. SpaceX shares closed at $127.44, up 1.30 percent, still 5.6 percent under the $135 offer price, after an intraday low of $112.93. The year-to-date Falcon launch count stays at 126 and excludes Starship. Two missions are listed for Sun 29 Nov: USSF-380 at 05:39 CET and Northrop Grumman's NG-25 cargo flight to the space station at 16:47 CET.

Context (what it sits on)

The FCC action is a licensing decision, not a finding that orbital computing already works. SpaceX's own listing documents have said that business is not proven. Secondary estimates of the Trump Account gift, including a figure of about 8.9 billion dollars cited by one research firm, are speculation and are not used here. The post-listing lock-up is still inferred from the prospectus as ending on Tue 8 Dec, with an upper branch of 797.6 million shares. The company has not issued a confirming notice. No transaction has been agreed on any Tesla–SpaceX combination. A special committee is still evaluating the idea, and remarks about timing remain a claim.

Forward (months–years)

Watch the Sun 29 Nov launch window, written comments in the Cochise County Starship case due Fri 27 Nov at 20:00 CET, the hearing on Wed 9 Dec at 22:00 CET, and the lock-up date of Tue 8 Dec. Links: Tesla, AI companies.

Russia-Ukraine war
Fact

Russian strikes hit Ukraine's power system and later Kyiv, as leaders in Brussels discuss 2027 financing

What happened

A Russian attack overnight into Wednesday damaged energy and gas infrastructure and caused electricity outages in five Ukrainian regions, including Dnipropetrovsk, Donetsk and Zaporizhzhia, officials said (Reuters). Power generation was halted at a DTEK thermal plant, and a Naftogaz gas facility was hit. Later on Wednesday, mayor Vitali Klitschko said ballistic missiles struck a shopping centre and two other buildings in Kyiv, and Reuters journalists heard explosions in the capital. Local authorities said at least five people were killed in strikes across the country, with some local counts higher (Al Jazeera, Euronews). Russia and Ukraine reported further drone and missile exchanges overnight into Thursday. The strikes come as European leaders in Brussels discuss a 45 billion euro loan to fund Ukraine in 2027.

Context (what it sits on)

The energy attacks follow a pattern of strikes on power and gas as winter deepens. Casualty figures still vary by local count, so the lower confirmed threshold is used here.

Morocco
Mixed

After the sports court restored Senegal as Africa Cup winner, Morocco prepares a challenge in the Swiss courts

What happened

The Court of Arbitration for Sport in Lausanne ruled on Tue 24 Nov that it upheld the Senegalese Football Federation's appeal, set aside a Confederation of African Football appeals-board decision, and reinstated the disciplinary board's result: Senegal won the 2025 Africa Cup of Nations final 1-0 (CAS media release). The Royal Moroccan Football Federation said it would pursue all remaining legal avenues. Reporting overnight says the federation is preparing a challenge at the Swiss Federal Tribunal, the only court that can review a CAS award, and that the deadline is 30 days from notification of the reasoned award, a period that cannot simply be extended (Morocco World News, Médias24). Success rates in such challenges are very low. No formal filing at the Swiss court had been confirmed when this brief was written.

Context (what it sits on)

The CAS panel, sitting unanimously, treated the referee's decision to restart the match after Senegal had briefly left the pitch as a 'field of play' matter that sports courts do not second-guess except in cases of bad faith or arbitrariness, which it did not find. An earlier Spanish report that the title had been left vacant was wrong. An appeal to the Swiss Federal Tribunal would not automatically change the on-field result while it is pending.

Forward (months–years)

Watch whether a filing is lodged once the reasoned award is notified, and the 30-day clock. Links: Europe.

Context (what it sits on)

A deal in the joint committee would still need to pass both houses, and the government could still use 49.3. The wider state budget remains stuck on the same choice between compromise, 49.3 and ordinances.
