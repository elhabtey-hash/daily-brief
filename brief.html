Fact

Anthropic up 17 percent on day one after opening 34 percent higher

What happened

Anthropic's shares (symbol ANT, Nasdaq) opened shortly before noon New York time on Thursday at $111.50, 34 percent above the $83 offer price. Forward (months–years)

Nvidia
Mixed

New 52-week low as US chip-licence rule takes effect

What happened

Nvidia closed Thursday at $145.98, down $4.98 or 3.30 percent, on about 323 million shares, after touching a new 52-week low of $145.71 (company investor page). Overnight indications near $143.5 are not a close. AMD fell 7.72 percent and Broadcom 2.09 percent. The Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) rule on remote access to advanced chips was published in the Federal Register and took effect on 19 November (91 FR 74970). It requires licences to ship advanced chips to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil; requires a licence for overseas data centres that supply significant computing remotely to companies based in China and other countries in group D:5 or Macau; and formally scraps the earlier 'AI Diffusion' rule.

Context (what it sits on)

Data centres already serving such customers have 90 days, to about 17 February, to obtain a licence. Comments are due by 19 January 2027. Nvidia's general counsel Tim Teter filed notice of a planned sale of 30,000 shares under a trading plan set up in May (Form 144). Governance: Jensen Huang resigned per the Form 8-K of 12 November, effective 11 November; Colette Kress is interim chief executive and also finance chief; Harvey Jones is board chair.

Forward (months–years)

Next dates: Nvidia presents at a UBS conference on Tue 1 Dec at 15:35 CET; its GTC conference in Washington runs 30 Nov to 3 Dec. Huang's closed-door House interview is set for Tue 1 Dec and Teter's for Wed 2 Dec. Senate Banking chair Tim Scott says Huang has agreed to testify, but the 3 Dec date has no official committee notice, so it remains a claim. Links: Markets, AI companies.

Iran and the Gulf
Claim

Iran says two supertankers hit mines in Hormuz; Houthi strike on Riyadh

What happened

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) navy said on Thursday that two supertankers loaded with more than 4 million barrels of crude hit naval mines and caught fire after using what it called an unauthorised route through the Strait of Hormuz (Iranian state media via Anadolu, Iran International). Reuters could not independently verify the report, and the ships have not been publicly identified. Saudi authorities said a tower in Riyadh's King Abdullah Financial District was hit in a Houthi attack; the Houthis said Saudi-led air strikes in Yemen killed at least 15 people (AP, Al Jazeera).

Context (what it sits on)

This is the ninth month of the US-Israeli war with Iran. A day earlier, Iran fired drones at a US base in Kuwait and at Bahrain after a US strike on Qeshm Island. Hormuz carries about a fifth of the world's oil.

Forward (months–years)

Watch for confirmation from shipowners, insurers or the US Navy, any US response, and whether Gulf shipping rates jump. Links: Energy.

Shenzhen summit
Fact

Trump, Xi and Putin sign a short peace declaration

What happened

After a 90-minute meeting on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit, the three leaders issued a 'Declaration toward Peace' (White House, Xinhua and Kremlin texts). It says a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought, promises a three-way dialogue on nuclear risk and proliferation, pledges to reform the UN and to increase supplies of refined fuel, and says the International Criminal Court must not abuse its jurisdiction.

Context (what it sits on)

Reuters says the declaration is symbolic and sets out no arms-control talks, which Trump had sought, and that the trip produced few concrete results. A day earlier Trump and Xi agreed to draft an action plan on strategic stability; China's readout mentions farm goods, energy and AI safety. The US-China trade truce runs to 10 January. European allies voiced unease at Trump's closeness to Putin.

Forward (months–years)

Watch for any follow-up on the nuclear dialogue and on chip export controls, which China wants loosened. Links: Ukraine and Russia, Nvidia, Energy.

Myanmar
Fact

World court finds Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya

What happened

The International Court of Justice (ICJ), the UN's top court, ruled on Thursday in The Gambia v. Myanmar that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya, a Muslim minority, during the 2017 'clearance operations' in northern Rakhine State. The vote was nine to six. The court found Myanmar also failed to prevent and punish genocide and breached a 2020 order to preserve evidence, but, by 14 votes to one, did not directly incite genocide (ICJ press release).

Context (what it sits on)

It ordered Myanmar to prosecute suspects, allow the safe return of displaced Rohingya and compensate them, and to report within two years on restitution and compensation. The court cited an estimated 10,000 Rohingya killed (UN News). Its rulings are final and binding but it can only refer non-compliance to the UN Security Council (AP).

Forward (months–years)

Watch the reaction of Myanmar's military government and of Bangladesh, which hosts about a million Rohingya refugees. The ruling may also affect the South Africa v. Israel case at the same court.

India
Fact

Cyclone Akvan crosses the Odisha coast after weakening

What happened

Cyclone Akvan began crossing the coast of Odisha, in eastern India, around 08:00 local time on Friday (03:30 CET), with winds of 70 to 80 kilometres per hour, after weakening from a severe cyclonic storm (India Meteorological Department via The Hindu, Business Standard). Forward (months–years)

COP31
Fact

Last scheduled day: draft deal drops fossil fuels and the adaptation finance target

France
Fact

Senate takes up the 2027 budget; forcing it through remains possible

The Senate begins debating the 2027 budget bill today, after the National Assembly rejected it unanimously on 4 November (Senate, CNews, Public Sénat). Forward (months–years)

AI
Mixed

Study: many chatbot bad habits fade within about 16 months

What happened

Nature reports an analysis finding that many unwanted behaviours of large language models (LLMs), including invented academic references, largely disappear about 16 months after they first appear across a model generation (Nature news, 19 Nov). Google's Gemini 4 Neon, released on 18 November, remains the newest major model.

Context (what it sits on)

The study is a useful counterweight to fixed ideas about AI's limits, but it measures averages; individual models can still invent sources. The Decoder reports that OpenAI is giving outside safety testers (Apollo Research, METR, Transluce, Goodfire) deeper access to unreleased models (claim, press).

Forward (months–years)

Watch independent tests of Gemini 4 Neon and reaction to the EU cyberattack, which shows misuse risks. Links: Europe, AI companies.
