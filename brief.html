Energy
Fact

Oil's biggest drop since the pandemic week

What happened

On Wednesday 4 November January Brent, the headline contract, settled at 94.62 dollars a barrel, down about 8.7 percent and 8.98 dollars from Tuesday's 103.60 close, its lowest settle since late August (CNBC, Edge consultancy). December WTI, the US front month, settled at 88.12 dollars, down 8.3 percent or 7.97 dollars (MarketScreener, CNBC). Oil is down more than 14 percent this week on hopes for US-Iran diplomacy. Distillate stocks fell to 99.3 million barrels, the lowest since 2003 (EIA via secondary), even as prices tumbled. Gold for November delivery on Comex settled at 4,061.10 dollars an ounce, up 3.39 percent (Morningstar Data Talk).

Context (what it sits on)

Vice President JD Vance wrote that 'the reason we lost is the war', and analysts say markets now price a higher chance that President Trump will cut a deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, which moves about a fifth of the world's oil. A Democratic Congress can also try to restrict war funding from January. Escalation risk remains, and some traders call the move overdone.

Forward (months–years)

Oil still trading lower this morning around 94 dollars for Brent. December WTI expires Thursday 19 November.

Markets
Mixed

Relief rally, AI chips left behind

What happened

On Wednesday 4 November the Dow Jones Industrial Average jumped 1,569.40 points, or 3.03 percent, to 53,356.12, its best day since April 2025 and back above 53,000 for the first time since 8 September. The S&P 500 rose 1.58 percent to 7,688.62 and the Nasdaq Composite 1.44 percent to 25,736.51 (CNBC closing data). Nine of eleven S&P sectors rose, led by consumer discretionary, while energy fell about 4.8 percent. Nasdaq-100 futures had been down as much as about 2.5 percent overnight before the open. CME FedWatch puts the odds of a 25-basis-point Federal Reserve rate rise at the December meeting at about 81.5 percent, with an 18.5 percent chance of a hold (CME FedWatch via secondary, 5 November). That would be a first hike in this cycle, not a second.

Context (what it sits on)

Markets usually rise after midterms. This time the relief came from gridlock and from cheaper oil, while investors sold the AI suppliers that had led the year. History says cheaper oil cools inflation and helps bonds and stocks, which fits the Fed-odds move.

Forward (months–years)

US October jobs report Friday 6 November 14:30 Paris. Bank of England decision today 13:00 Paris.

US midterms
Fact

Majorities firm, two Senate races open

What happened

Reuters reports Democrats hold a Senate majority of at least 54 to 44 after flipping seats in Kansas, Iowa, Texas, Ohio, Maine, North Carolina and South Carolina, with Alaska and Mississippi still uncalled as of Wednesday, which could take the majority to 56 to 44. Democrats also gained at least 30 House seats. The Associated Press had already called both chambers for the Democrats. Named governors include Democrats Gina Hinojosa in Texas, Keisha Lance Bottoms in Georgia and Amy Acton in Ohio. President Trump accepted the results and toured White House ballroom construction. House Speaker Mike Johnson said he would not lead the Republican caucus next year. Final House seat totals are not written here because Reuters has not published a complete chamber total in the pieces checked.

Context (what it sits on)

From January Democrats can investigate the administration, block nominations and reshape spending on the Iran war, Ukraine and tariffs. New governors in Texas and Ohio campaigned on slowing data-centre build-outs. A funding bill for the government past December is the main lame-duck fight still under Republican control.

Forward (months–years)

Alaska and Mississippi counts through the week. New Congress sits 3 January 2027.

AI companies
Mixed

Arm beats, Qualcomm soft, SoftBank blocked in China

What happened

Arm reported fiscal second-quarter revenue of 1.44 billion dollars, up 27 percent, and adjusted earnings of 0.52 dollars a share, above estimates, with data-centre royalties doubling, and guided third-quarter revenue of 1.54 billion plus or minus 50 million (Arm newsroom, 4 November). Shares slipped after hours on the in-line guide. Qualcomm reported adjusted revenue of 10.44 billion dollars and adjusted earnings of 2.17 dollars a share, but handset-chip sales fell 26 percent to 5.14 billion and the current-quarter earnings midpoint of about 2.25 dollars trailed estimates. It guided about 650 million dollars of data-centre sales for the current quarter and still targets more than 5 billion by the end of fiscal 2027 (CNBC, Reuters). Shares fell about 6 to 7 percent after hours. The Financial Times reported that China has blocked SoftBank's 5.4 billion dollar purchase of ABB's robotics division (Claim, FT). Anthropic's stock-market listing is still reported for after the midterms, with an earliest possible trade date around mid-November (Claim).

Context (what it sits on)

Arm shows AI compute demand is still growing through CPU designs used by the big clouds. Qualcomm shows the phone market is hurting from memory costs even as it builds a small data-centre business. SoftBank's blocked deal is a fresh China-tech friction point.

Forward (months–years)

CoreWeave results Tuesday 10 November. Nvidia results Tuesday 17 November. Anthropic listing watch mid-November.

Nvidia
Mixed

Chips decouple from the relief rally

What happened

Nvidia closed at 200.13 dollars on Wednesday 4 November, down 3.17 percent, its lowest close in the recent slide and about 16.3 percent below the 6 October close of 239.24 dollars (tracker, secondary price data). The PHLX Semiconductor Index fell about 3.1 percent to a six-month low (Claim, Reuters via secondary). The Financial Times and The Information report that Nvidia has told cloud customers, verbally since the summer, to sell at least 51 percent of capacity under new contracts to non-Chinese end users, with end users disclosed. Nvidia neither confirmed nor denied, saying that if the world's clouds rely on foreign-made chips, the model and application layers will be foreign-built as well (Claim plus company statement). Wells Fargo says Texas holds about 27 percent of planned US data-centre capacity (Claim). No new Nvidia filing.

Context (what it sits on)

State pauses on data centres and a Democratic Congress raise permit and export-policy risk around Nvidia's sales timing. The 51 percent guidance is a modest headwind for Southeast Asian cloud buyers. CME FedWatch prices a December rate rise, not a second hike. The Nvidia tracker's line about markets betting on a second Fed hike conflicts with futures and is not used.

Forward (months–years)

US jobs Friday 6 November. CoreWeave Tuesday 10 November. Nvidia third-quarter results Tuesday 17 November about 22:20 Paris, call 23:00. GTC Washington 30 November to 3 December. Google Cloud A5X (Vera Rubin) general availability in November.

France
Fact

Budget rejected unanimously at first reading

What happened

On Wednesday night 4 November the National Assembly rejected the revenue half of the 2027 budget by 458 votes to none, with two abstentions, which under the rules rejects the whole bill at first reading (Assemblee nationale, France 24, Les Echos). Deputies had already stripped a surtax on large companies and other savings measures. The government's original text, aiming for a public deficit of 5 percent of GDP, now goes to the Senate. Matignon pointed to a possible compromise in December. Options later include article 49.3 or an unused budget ordinance.

Context (what it sits on)

A unanimous rejection leaves the minority government without a parliamentary majority for its own text and raises the chance of a forced passage that could trigger a no-confidence fight. Markets will watch French borrowing costs.

Forward (months–years)

Senate examination from about 20 November. Full budget path into December.

Bank of England
Claim

First hike in three years widely expected today

What happened

The Bank of England's Monetary Policy Committee publishes its decision and the November Monetary Policy Report at 12:00 GMT, 13:00 Paris, today Thursday 5 November (Bank of England schedule). Most analysts expect Bank Rate to rise from 3.75 percent to 4 percent, the first increase since 2023, after six holds (AP, BBC). Inflation has been pushed up by fuel and energy costs tied to the Iran war. The decision is not yet out and is not written as done.

Context (what it sits on)

A hike would confirm that higher oil has forced at least one major central bank to tighten again. The statement's hints about further rises will matter more for sterling and gilt yields than the 25-basis-point step itself.

Forward (months–years)

Decision and report at 13:00 Paris today.

Iran and the Gulf
Mixed

Deal hopes drive oil, no agreement yet

What happened

Oil's collapse prices a higher chance of diplomacy after the midterm rebuke of the Iran war. Qatar continues to mediate an interim arrangement, and both sides have signalled openness to talks, but Iran still ties any Hormuz reopening to sanctions relief, an end to the US naval blockade and release of frozen funds (prior brief, Al-Monitor, secondary). President Trump told the Financial Times he has no immediate plans for further strikes. Attacks on tankers continue. No deal has been reached.

Context (what it sits on)

Cheaper oil eases inflation and the case for rate rises, which is why stocks and gold rallied with the drop. Democrats campaigned on war costs and can press war funding from January.

Forward (months–years)

Qatari mediation continues. Watch for any public interim text.

Morocco
Fact

Post-MINURSO security orders from the King

What happened

After the UN MINURSO mission's mandate for Western Sahara ended on 31 October, King Mohammed VI chaired a working session and ordered firm security and development measures for the southern provinces, directing the Interior Ministry's coordination office to manage an orderly withdrawal of the mission (Cabinet Royal communique, Morocco World News, le360). Morocco says it will continue to observe the ceasefire.

Context (what it sits on)

The end of the 35-year UN mission shifts responsibility for the territory's security posture more squarely onto Rabat and removes a long-standing international buffer, which neighbours and the UN Security Council will watch.

Forward (months–years)

Implementation of the withdrawal and any Security Council follow-up.
