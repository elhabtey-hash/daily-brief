Big tech earnings
Fact

Microsoft and Alphabet grow fast but split on spending

What happened

Microsoft reported quarterly revenue of 92.3 billion dollars, up 19 percent, with its Azure cloud up 48 percent and contracted future revenue of 677 billion dollars (Microsoft release, Wednesday 28 October). Its capital spending, including leases, was 45.2 billion dollars, below the more than 50 billion it planned, because some third-party capacity deliveries were delayed (company call). It now sees about 170 billion dollars for calendar 2026, down from about 175 billion in July (Reuters). Alphabet reported revenue of 127.6 billion dollars, up 25 percent, with Google Cloud up 103 percent to 30.7 billion dollars and a 561 billion dollar backlog (Alphabet 8-K). Capital spending was 51.4 billion dollars, the 2026 guide of 195 to 205 billion was kept, and 2027 spending will 'increase significantly'. Free cash flow was minus 9.1 billion dollars and ad revenue missed forecasts (Reuters). Microsoft rose more than 4 percent after hours and Alphabet fell more than 7 percent (Reuters).

Context (what it sits on)

Demand for AI computing still exceeds supply at both companies, so the spending boom is not ending. But Alphabet is now spending more cash than it brings in, and Microsoft says it will seek extra funding, which keeps the question of how AI is paid for alive. Microsoft's delay points at the smaller 'neocloud' providers that rent out Nvidia chips, which is a timing issue for now.

Forward (months–years)

Amazon and Intel results tonight after the US close (Amazon call 22:00 Paris), Meta follow-through.

US Federal Reserve
Mixed

Fed holds, two members want a rise

What happened

The Federal Reserve kept its target range at 3.75 to 4.00 percent on Wednesday 28 October by 10 votes to 2, with Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack voting for a quarter-point rise (Fed statement). The statement says the economy is 'expanding at a strong pace' and 'inflation remains elevated', adding that 'the Committee will deliver price stability'. Chair Kevin Warsh said the Fed is ready to act. Futures put the chance of a December rise at 85 percent, up from 78 percent on Tuesday (CME FedWatch via Reuters). The 10-year Treasury yield rose to 5.044 percent and the 2-year was little changed at 4.731 percent (MarketWatch).

Context (what it sits on)

The Fed raised rates in September for the first time since 2023 because of oil-driven inflation. Two dissents for a rise signal that December is likely to bring another increase, which makes borrowing costlier for households and for companies financing AI data centres.

Forward (months–years)

US third-quarter growth (GDP) and September PCE inflation today, Fed meeting 9 December.

Iran and the Gulf
Claim

US tells allies to brace for strikes after the midterms

What happened

US officials told several Middle East countries to prepare for possible renewed military action against Iran after the 3 November midterms, and for Iranian retaliation against energy sites, two diplomats told AP on Wednesday 28 October. Trump has not made a final decision. Iran's foreign minister Abbas Araghchi said Tehran 'continues to give diplomacy a chance' and is working with mediators. Tehran rejected reported Qatari proposals but says talks continue over reopening the Strait of Hormuz (Gulf News). The Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted a Houthi ballistic missile aimed at the Eastern Province, and Houthi missiles hit the perimeter of Aden airport while the Houthis said Saudi strikes killed 14 people in Jawf (AFP).

Context (what it sits on)

The war that began in late February has been in a strike pause since early September, but the US build-up of about 9,000 extra troops and the warnings to allies make a new round after next Tuesday a real possibility. That would raise the risk to Gulf oil exports and push prices higher.

Forward (months–years)

US midterms Tuesday 3 November, Iran reply to mediators.

Energy
Fact

Oil climbs back on strike reports

What happened

December Brent settled at 115.32 dollars on Wednesday 28 October, up 1.44 from 113.88, and December WTI at 102.78 dollars, up 2.88 from 99.90 (ICE and NYMEX via Xinhua, Newsquawk). January Brent, which follows December Brent as the headline contract, settled at 111.64 dollars, up 2.23 from 109.41 (market data). Context (what it sits on)

December WTI is the front month and is back above 100 dollars. December Brent expires on Friday 30 October, and January Brent is about 3.7 dollars cheaper, so the headline Brent price will seem to drop on Monday even if the market does not move.

Forward (months–years)

December Brent expiry Friday 30 October.

Israel
Fact

Count confirms deadlock, no bloc near a majority

What happened

With about 91 percent of regular votes counted, Netanyahu's bloc has 53 seats, Gadi Eisenkot's bloc 48 and Arab-led parties a record 19 (Central Elections Committee via Haaretz and the Guardian). Likud has 26 seats and Eisenkot's Yashar 21. Counting of about 600,000 absentee 'double envelope' ballots, mostly soldiers', began at 01:00 Israel time on Thursday and is expected to finish today (Central Elections Committee). The Guardian reports the absentee count is not expected to shift the result much.

Context (what it sits on)

A government needs 61 of 120 seats. Netanyahu is 8 short, and the opposition can only reach a majority with Arab parties, which most Jewish-led parties have refused to do. Weeks of coalition talks or even another election are possible, while the country is at war with Iran. Wednesday's partial figure of 56 seats for Netanyahu's bloc has fallen as more votes were counted.

Forward (months–years)

Final count today, president's consultations next week.

Tesla
Mixed

Cybercab answers due, Semi fire under investigation

What happened

Tesla's sworn answers to the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) on whether the Cybercab meets federal motor vehicle safety standards (FMVSS), audit AQ26002, are due Friday 30 October. A Tesla Semi caught fire on Interstate 880 near Hayward, California, on Tuesday evening without a crash, the driver was unhurt and the road was closed for about 10 hours, with the cause under investigation (California Highway Patrol via Electrek). The Cybercab has no steering wheel or pedals, so the regulator's view decides how fast it can scale beyond Austin. A Dallas launch in November is a company claim. Any Tesla and SpaceX combination is only under study by a SpaceX board committee, and nothing is agreed.

SpaceX
Fact

Spectrum vote this afternoon

What happened

The Federal Communications Commission (FCC) votes at its open meeting today, Thursday 29 October, 15:30 to 17:30 Paris time, on two proposals: auctioning 25 MHz for land, satellite-to-phone or hybrid use, and making another 482 MHz available for satellite coverage of phones (FCC agenda). Both are proposals for comment, not final rules. No Falcon launch since Tuesday, so the year count stays at 121. The next Starlink launch is set for Saturday 31 October, 04:48 Paris (SpaceX).

Context (what it sits on)

More spectrum is what Starlink needs to compete with mobile networks for phone service, but rules take months to finalise.

Forward (months–years)

FCC today, Starlink Saturday 31 October, USSF-432 Tuesday 3 November.
