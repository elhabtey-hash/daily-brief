Nvidia (lead)
Claim

Board reported weighing Huang’s removal

What happened

The Wall Street Journal reported on Wednesday evening (US time) that Nvidia’s board is preparing to remove chief executive and co-founder Jensen Huang, and Reuters, citing a source, said the board was meeting late Wednesday to decide. Semafor reported that some directors favour chief financial officer Colette Kress as interim chief executive and that the full board had not decided; Bloomberg reported the board has been working with an outside firm on possible successors. All of these are claims from unnamed sources. The reports followed a New York Times report that Huang also had a relationship with an Nvidia executive who reported to him. As of about 08:25 CET, Nvidia had issued no statement and no decision had been announced; its last statements (8 and 10 November) announced an independent investigation, said the board had no reason to believe national security or company secrets were compromised and expressed confidence in Huang.

Context (what it sits on)

Shares closed up 2.87 percent at $173.73 on Wednesday, then fell about 7.7 to 8 percent in after-hours trading to around $160, an after-hours price, not a close. Under Nvidia’s bylaws a majority of directors can remove the chief executive; Huang owns about 3.6 percent of the shares and has no publicly named successor. Separately, the deadline for Huang to respond to a House request for a closed-door briefing passed at 23:00 CET on Wednesday with no public reply; committee chairs said subpoenas were likely, but none has been reported issued. Reuters and AFP report that a Chinese university on the US export blacklist bought two H200 chips through a supplier (claim; Nvidia declined to comment).

Forward (months–years)

Any board statement or filing (an 8-K) today. Tue 17 Nov ~22:20 CET: third-quarter results, call 23:00 CET; Nvidia guided to $108 billion revenue, plus or minus 2 percent. Wed 18 Nov: requested House briefing, not confirmed.

Ukraine
Mixed

Prisoner swap agreed, envoys head to Moscow

What happened

The US, Russian and Ukrainian delegations ended two days of talks in Abu Dhabi on Wednesday, the first in nine months. Russia’s chief negotiator Vladimir Medinsky said the sides agreed in principle on a large-scale prisoner exchange; Russian state agency TASS says it could happen in two to three weeks, and US envoy Jared Kushner said numbers would be finalised on Thursday (Al Jazeera, BBC, RFE/RL). Kushner and Steve Witkoff are due to meet President Vladimir Putin in Moscow today, then brief President Volodymyr Zelensky in Dublin.

Context (what it sits on)

Kushner called the talks “very tough” but “productive” and said there are “some openings”, while noting territory remains the hardest issue and that a security framework depends on ending the war. Ukraine’s Rustem Umerov said significant differences remain. Medinsky said Moscow did not see what there was to discuss with the EU, while Ukraine wants Europe at the table in a wider format. Both sides exchanged large drone attacks overnight before the second day.

Forward (months–years)

Thu 12 Nov: Witkoff and Kushner meet Putin. Fri 13 Nov: EU leaders in Dublin with Zelensky. The prisoner exchange is agreed in principle, not done.

Iran and the Gulf
Mixed

Vance weighs direct talks, Tehran says no

What happened

The Wall Street Journal reported on Wednesday that Vice President JD Vance has taken the lead on Iran diplomacy and is considering face-to-face talks with Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran’s Supreme National Security Council (claim, unnamed officials). Rezaei told Al Mayadeen television there are no talks with the US, that Iran will keep its enriched uranium at home, and that the Strait of Hormuz stays shut until the US accepts seven conditions, including lifting sanctions and the naval blockade. He threatened distant US bases if Iran is struck again.

Context (what it sits on)

At a Veterans Day speech, President Trump said the US has already won but wants “total victory”. Reuters sources confirmed Iranian attacks on ship-navigation equipment on Oman’s Didamar island in the strait; shipping sources expect limited impact. Gulf oil exports recovered to over 80 percent of pre-war levels in September and October. Iran’s parliament speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf told Al Jazeera the “ball is in the US court”.

Forward (months–years)

No deal, talks or reopening of the strait is assumed. Watch for a US reply to Iran’s conditions and any Vance meeting.

AI
Mixed

DeepMind for genes and robots, Meta goes small

What happened

Google DeepMind announced AlphaGenome 2, which predicts how DNA variants affect gene activity in specific cell types and states, adding DNA methylation, a chemical tag that switches genes on and off. It also released Gemini Robotics 2.5, a model that turns vision and language into robot actions, which it says packed car parts with a 96 percent success rate in tests at Mercedes-Benz. Meta released Muse Ember, its smallest model, priced for repetitive agent tasks such as tool calls and document parsing.

Context (what it sits on)

DeepMind says AlphaGenome 2 guides research but is not for clinical or diagnostic use. Robotics performance figures are company claims from proof-of-concept tests; rollout is through partners including Apptronik, Agile Robots and Boston Dynamics. These are company announcements, not independent evaluations.

Forward (months–years)

Watch independent tests and uptake by drug-discovery labs.

Science and technology
Fact

First black-hole image survives a recheck

What happened

An independent team led by researchers at Australia’s national science agency CSIRO reanalysed Event Horizon Telescope data on the black hole at the centre of galaxy M87 with a different method and recovered the same bright ring around a dark centre, published in Nature on 11 November.

Context (what it sits on)

Since 2022 a Japanese group led by Junichiro Makino had argued the ring was an artefact of image processing. The new result largely backs the 2019 image, which matched Einstein’s general relativity. Nature also published work on a waist-based obesity definition that predicts health better than body mass index.

Forward (months–years)

Watch for a response from the Makino group.
