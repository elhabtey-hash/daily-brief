AI spending
Fact

Amazon grows fast, shares fall as AI costs mount

What happened

Amazon reported third-quarter sales of 201.9 billion dollars, up 12 percent, and record operating income of 28.1 billion dollars (Amazon release, Thursday 29 October). Its cloud arm, Amazon Web Services (AWS), grew 39 percent to 46.0 billion dollars, its fastest in nearly five years. On the call it kept about 220 billion dollars of 2026 spending and said 2027 will be 'materially higher', and it will sell its own Trainium AI chips outside AWS from late 2027 (call, via tracker note). Shares fell about 7 percent after hours on a cautious fourth-quarter outlook (AP, Reuters). Intel fell 18 to 21 percent after hours on a weak fourth-quarter outlook for PCs. On Thursday Alphabet fell 12.6 percent, its worst day in years, after Wednesday's results showed negative free cash flow.

Context (what it sits on)

Demand for AI computing is still strong at every large cloud, but investors now punish the spending needed to meet it. Both Google and Amazon are selling their own chips to outsiders, which over time widens competition with Nvidia.

Forward (months–years)

Apple and Palantir results Monday 2 November, AMD Tuesday 3 November.

Central banks
Fact

ECB and Bank of Japan hold, both lean towards rises

What happened

The European Central Bank (ECB) kept its deposit rate at 2.50 percent on Thursday 29 October, after raising it in September (ECB). Lagarde said the council debated a rise but chose unanimously to wait. Eurozone inflation is 3.8 percent, its highest since 2023, and money markets put the chance of a December rise at 67 percent (CNBC, LSEG). Lagarde also announced she will step down on 7 February 2027, and the European Council will pick her successor (ECB). The Bank of Japan held at 1.25 percent today by 8 votes to 1, declared for the first time since 2013 that underlying inflation meets its 2 percent target, raised its 2027 inflation forecast to 2.6 percent and will sell more of its stock funds (Reuters, CNBC). Most economists expect a Japanese rise to 1.5 percent in December.

Context (what it sits on)

The oil shock from the Iran war has pushed the main central banks back towards raising rates, which makes borrowing costlier for governments, households and companies. Lagarde's early exit opens a race for the most powerful job in EU economic policy.

Forward (months–years)

Fed Wednesday 9 December, ECB Thursday 17 December, Bank of Japan December meeting.

US economy
Fact

Strong growth, sticky inflation, stocks slip

What happened

US gross domestic product (GDP) grew at a 3.4 percent annual rate in the third quarter, above forecasts (BEA). September inflation on the Fed's preferred PCE measure was 3.6 percent, with the core measure at 3.1 percent (CNBC). The 10-year Treasury yield ended around 4.98 percent, slightly lower (Newsquawk). On Thursday the S&P 500 fell 0.30 percent to 7,720.39, the Nasdaq 1.38 percent to 26,184.72 and the Dow 0.25 percent to 52,342.56 (CNBC).

Context (what it sits on)

The economy is strong enough to absorb higher rates, which keeps a Fed rise in December likely. Technology shares are pulling the market lower as AI spending worries return.

Forward (months–years)

US jobs report Friday 6 November, Fed Wednesday 9 December.

Iran and the Gulf
Claim

US letter offers talks while strike warnings stand

What happened

Vice President JD Vance sent Iran's Supreme National Security Council chief Mohsen Rezaei a letter, delivered by Qatar, proposing a framework for talks that would deal with Iran's nuclear programme from the start (Al Jazeera, Guardian). Iran has received it but not replied publicly, and says sanctions, frozen assets, the naval blockade and hostilities must end first. AP reported on Wednesday that US officials told Gulf allies to prepare for possible strikes after the 3 November midterms, and President Trump has not decided. Iran told Saudi, Pakistani and Turkish security chiefs it cannot halt Houthi attacks and proposed a two-week regional truce tied to a gradual reopening of the Strait of Hormuz (The National, regional press).

Context (what it sits on)

This is the clearest diplomatic opening since the strike pause began in September, but the timing alongside strike warnings suggests a last offer before escalation. A deal would lower oil prices sharply, while new strikes would put Gulf exports at risk.

Forward (months–years)

Iran's reply, US midterms Tuesday 3 November.

Israel
Fact

Final count: no majority for either side

What happened

The Central Elections Committee published final results on Thursday evening after counting absentee ballots: Likud 27 seats, Yashar 21, Together 12, Joint List 11, Democrats 8, Ra'am 8, Yisrael Beiteinu 7, Otzma Yehudit 7, United Torah Judaism 7, Shas 6 and Religious Zionism 6 (Times of Israel, Ynet, Haaretz). That gives Netanyahu's bloc 53, the Jewish-led opposition 48 and Arab-led parties 19. Turnout was 77.9 percent. Results go to President Herzog on Wednesday 4 November and the Knesset is sworn in on Tuesday 10 November. Opposition figure Yair Lapid said forming a government will be difficult.

Context (what it sits on)

A government needs 61 seats. Netanyahu is 8 short, and the opposition can only reach a majority with Arab parties, which most Jewish-led parties refuse. Long talks or another election are possible while Israel is at war with Iran.

Forward (months–years)

Results to president Wednesday 4 November, Knesset sworn in Tuesday 10 November.

SpaceX
Fact

FCC proposes more satellite-to-phone spectrum

What happened

The Federal Communications Commission (FCC) on Thursday 29 October unanimously proposed rules adding up to 482 MHz for supplemental coverage from space, meaning satellites linking directly to phones, and a separate proposal to auction mid-band spectrum (FCC). It is a proposal only, with nothing assigned to SpaceX, and its request for 902 to 928 MHz was not acted on (Broadband Breakfast). Ship 42 has completed a full-duration static fire, so both Flight 15 stages have been test-fired. Musk says Flight 15 is in November, with press pointing to about 8 November and no FAA licence yet. SpaceX shares (SPCX) closed at 143.89 dollars, down 0.92 percent, on Thursday. The Tesla combination remains under study, with nothing agreed.

Context (what it sits on)

More spectrum would let Starlink's phone service compete with mobile networks, but final rules take months.

Forward (months–years)

Starlink 15-18 Saturday 31 October 04:48 Paris, USSF-432 Tuesday 3 November, Starship Flight 15 about Sunday 8 November.
