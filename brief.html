Mixed

East-West still shut; repair timeline clash; Brent ~$107

What happened

Saudi Arabia’s East-West crude pipeline (Petroline — the main Hormuz bypass, capacity up to ~7 million barrels a day) remains shut after the 10–11 September drone attacks Riyadh blamed on Iran-backed militias in Iraq. Associated Press, citing two regional officials, says repairs could take three to five weeks, including a major pumping facility; one official said partial flows during repairs are possible. US Energy Secretary Chris Wright told Bloomberg the line would be “back running very soon,” with more clarity possible Tuesday 15 September — a Claim conflict with the AP weeks timeline. Overnight Red Sea: Houthis seized Greater and Lesser Hanish islands (~160 km north of Bab el-Mandeb) after earlier takes of Mocha and Mayun/Perim; Monday they claimed strikes on Khamis Mushait airbase; the Saudi-led coalition reported 13 civilians wounded. Oman-hosted Iran–Gulf talks on reopening Hormuz remain postponed with no new date. Oil: ICE November Brent settled Monday at $105.68 (+$1.07); Tuesday Asia/Europe open about $106.9–$107.3. NYMEX October West Texas Intermediate Monday settle $101.39; Tuesday open about $102.7–$103.2. AAA US diesel national average hit a new record ~$6.23 a gallon Monday (prior weekend ~$6.20).

Context (what it sits on)

US–Iran war since late February 2026. Dual choke (Hormuz + Bab el-Mandeb) after the Saudi bypass knock-out. Wright also said Hormuz transits average ~10 mb/d over seven days versus ~20 mb/d pre-war. Yanbu stocks may cover only ~5–7 days of exports if the outage persists (Reuters buyers).

Forward (months–years)

Multi-week pipeline outage plus Houthi island chain keeps a structural Red Sea risk premium into the Federal Reserve meeting and midterms. Watch Wright/Saudi clarity today, Yanbu stock draw, and any new Hormuz mediation date.
