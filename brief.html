East-West still shut; Yanbu halt; Wright “days” vs weeks; Brent $108.75

What happened

Saudi Arabia’s East-West crude pipeline (Petroline — the main Strait of Hormuz bypass, capacity up to ~7 million barrels a day) remains shut after the 10–11 September drone attacks. Reuters shipping sources said crude loadings at Yanbu were suspended and that Riyadh informed European customers some late-September cargoes were cancelled — converting the outage into a physical export cut. US Energy Secretary Chris Wright told CNBC Tuesday the restart would be “measured in days”; Reuters sources said damage could take five to six weeks to repair (one said partial pumping during repairs is possible); Goldman Sachs (via Reuters) framed the range from “very soon” to eight weeks and raised odds of Brent above $120 if a lasting Gulf shortfall sticks. No Saudi official restart date published. Hormuz commodity-vessel traffic remained thin: Reuters preliminary tracking cited four commodity transits Tuesday (2 in / 2 out), well below the recent 10-day average (~18); no very large crude carriers or liquefied natural gas tankers in that count. Saudi-led coalition claimed it destroyed a Houthi drone south of Mecca Tuesday (Houthi denial via SABA). Oil: ICE November Brent settled Tuesday at $108.75 (+$3.07); NYMEX October West Texas Intermediate $105.83 (+$4.44) — highest closes since 19 May. AAA US diesel national average hit a new record ~$6.27 a gallon Tuesday ($6.2694). Wednesday Asia/Europe open softened: Brent about $107.8–$108 / WTI about $104.6–$105 after a reported American Petroleum Institute crude build (~+7.1 million barrels).

Context (what it sits on)

US–Iran war since late February 2026. Dual choke (Hormuz constrained + Bab el-Mandeb/Houthi coast) after the Saudi Red Sea bypass knock-out. Prior brief: Associated Press 3–5 weeks vs Wright “very soon.” Yanbu stocks may cover only days of exports if the outage persists.

Forward (months–years)

Science
Fact

Webb: brown dwarfs ~2 Jupiter masses

What happened

NASA/ESA James Webb Space Telescope announcement Tuesday: in the star-forming cluster IC 348 (~1,000 light-years away), NIRCam/NIRSpec observations identified brown dwarfs as light as about twice Jupiter’s mass (0.19% of the Sun) — below model expectations and the least massive known. One shows signs of a disk, implying potential planet formation around a planetary-mass object. An unidentified hydrocarbon feature may support a new spectral class for the coolest young brown dwarfs. Observational result, not a commercial catalyst.

Context (what it sits on)

Challenges the lower bound in star-formation models for how light an object can form like a star.

Forward (months–years)

Watch follow-on Webb surveys of the coolest free-floating objects and any refined formation models.
