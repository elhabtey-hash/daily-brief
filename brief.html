Oil near $109; Hormuz and Red Sea both tight

What happened

Early Friday, Brent crude (the global oil benchmark) traded about $108.50–$109 a barrel and West Texas Intermediate (the US gauge) about $103–$103.50, after both rose more than 6% on Thursday. Both benchmarks are on track to end a week above $100 for the first time since mid-May. Shipping remains squeezed through the Strait of Hormuz — the narrow Gulf lane that in peacetime carries about one-fifth of seaborne oil — with Kpler-cited daily vessel counts in single digits Thursday. Iran-aligned Houthi forces seized Yemen’s Red Sea port of Mocha (Mokha) Thursday, adding Bab el-Mandeb risk. US–Iran tanker strikes earlier in the week (five Iranian carriers destroyed Tuesday; Iranian missiles toward Jordan) set the premium. US diesel’s AAA national average recently printed near $5.94 a gallon (record territory).

Context (what it sits on)

War since 28 February 2026. OPEC+ held October quotas flat. Iran’s “prohibited zone” talk still lacks published coordinates. EIA this week raised its full-year 2026 Brent average forecast toward about $91 — a forecast cut off before this week’s surge to $108+.

Forward (months–years)

Watch whether $100+ holds into the Federal Reserve’s 15–16 September meeting after today’s Consumer Price Index, and whether Red Sea plus Hormuz becomes a double choke into winter. Cross-beat: Europe’s fresh ECB hike and French borrowing costs.

Europe
Fact

ECB deposit rate to 2.50%

What happened

The European Central Bank (the euro-zone’s central bank) on Thursday raised its three key rates by 0.25 percentage points. The deposit facility rate — what banks earn for parking overnight cash at the ECB — is now 2.50%. President Christine Lagarde, speaking in Berlin, said the Middle East conflict keeps generating inflation pressure and that inflation will stay well above the 2% target for an extended period. Staff raised 2026 growth and inflation projections (headline inflation seen averaging about 3.0% this year in reporting). Lagarde kept a data-dependent, meeting-by-meeting stance and said the ECB is not pre-committing to a rate path; she called Thursday’s move a “no-brainer” in press coverage.

Context (what it sits on)

Second hike of 2026 after Iran-war energy shocks. Gas (TTF) had spiked toward ~€79/MWh earlier this week. Markets had fully priced the 25 bp move; the fight is over whether October/December bring more.

Forward (months–years)

Watch Lagarde’s vigilance language versus energy second-round effects into winter. Cross-beat: French OAT yields and the 30 September budget.

US
Fact

PPI up on diesel; mail-rule stay still blocked below SCOTUS

What happened

The Bureau of Labor Statistics said Thursday that the Producer Price Index (PPI — wholesale prices producers charge) rose 0.4% in August and 5.4% over the year. Energy jumped 4.2% for the month; diesel alone surged about 24% month-on-month in the detail. Core PPI (ex food and energy) rose 0.2%. Consumer Price Index (CPI — shoppers’ inflation) is due today 8:30 a.m. Eastern (14:30 Paris) — after this morning’s cutoff. Separately, the US Court of Appeals for the First Circuit on Thursday denied the government’s bid to stay Judge Indira Talwani’s preliminary injunction blocking mandatory parts of the US Postal Service mail-ballot rule for the 3 November midterms. The Supreme Court application (26A305) remains pending after Wednesday’s state oppositions; no overnight high-court order.

Context (what it sits on)

August jobs were already strong (+162,000). About one-third of Americans typically vote by mail; North Carolina and Alabama are already mailing ballots.

Forward (months–years)

CPI today then Federal Reserve 15–16 September. If SCOTUS stays the injunction mid-mailing wave, election admin chaos risk rises; First Circuit denial leaves the block intact for now.

France
Fact

OAT near 4.32–4.34% after ECB

What happened

France’s 10-year government bond yield (OAT) printed about 4.32% on Banque de France’s 10 September bond-index row and traded as high as about 4.34% in market reports — levels last seen in the 2008 crisis era. The jump followed the ECB hike and the oil spike. The 2027 budget bill is still aimed at the Council of Ministers on 30 September; Senate partial elections are 27 September. Farm emergency aid above €1 billion (announced 4 September) remains the delivery test. Nuclear: Gravelines-5 still out toward 25 January 2027; Flamanville-3 long outage from 26 September.

Context (what it sits on)

Hung parliament and high debt make every rate tick a political cost. Lecornu marked one year as prime minister on 9 September.

Forward (months–years)

Watch budget negotiations under hotter yields and whether farm cash reaches autumn sowing.
