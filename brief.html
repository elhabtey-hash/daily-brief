US midterms
Fact

Democrats win both chambers, AP calls

What happened

Called races (AP): the AP called Senate control for the Democrats late on Tuesday 3 November (US time) after Josh Turek won in Iowa, and called the House early on Wednesday 4 November after Democrats reached the 218 seats needed. AP reports Democrats won at least seven Senate seats in Republican-held or swing states: Kansas, Iowa, Texas (James Talarico, the first Democrat to win statewide there in more than 30 years), Ohio, Maine, North Carolina and South Carolina, and flipped House districts that Trump won by up to 18 points. Governors called by AP include Democrats Keisha Lance Bottoms (Georgia), Amy Acton (Ohio), Rod Sand (Iowa) and Gina Hinojosa (Texas), who campaigned on a pause for new data centres. Not confirmed: the final Senate and House seat totals and the number of races still uncalled. The tracker's '51 Senate seats' (AP via Nikkei) could not be matched to an AP or Reuters total in this pass, so no seat count is written here. Many House races remain uncalled.

Context (what it sits on)

From January, Democrats can block or reshape the President's agenda: tax and spending bills, money for the Iran war and for Ukraine, and his use of tariffs (Congress can try to limit emergency tariff powers, though the President keeps a veto). They gain the power to investigate the administration. Senate control gives them a say over judges and cabinet nominations. On technology, Democrats are seen as more open to AI regulation, chip-export curbs and limits on power-hungry data centres, and new state governors can act on data-centre permits directly. Divided government usually means less new legislation, and a shutdown fight over the budget becomes more likely.

Forward (months–years)

Final tallies through the week, Alaska ranked-choice count later. New Congress sits 3 January 2027.

Markets
Mixed

Blue sweep hits the AI trade

What happened

On Tuesday 3 November, before results, the S&P 500 rose 0.36 percent to 7,568.92, the Nasdaq 0.83 percent to 25,372.01, and the Dow fell 0.38 percent to 51,786.72 (CNBC closing data), as chip shares rebounded. Overnight, as Democratic wins mounted, Nasdaq-100 futures fell as much as about 2.2 to 2.5 percent before cutting losses to around 0.8 to 1 percent, and S&P 500 futures were down 0.6 to 1.3 percent at different times (Reuters, NBC, Fox Business, intraday, not closes). The 10-year Treasury yield fell about 6 basis points to around 4.91 percent and the dollar index fell 0.37 percent to 101.34, with the euro at 1.1249 dollars (Reuters). European futures were about 1.4 percent lower. Cryptocurrencies fell on expectations of tighter rules. CoreWeave was about 7 percent lower and Oracle about 4 percent before the open (tracker).

Context (what it sits on)

Markets had expected a Democratic House but not a sweep this broad. The fear is that a Democratic Congress and states like Texas slow the data-centre construction that has driven AI share prices. History says markets usually rise in the months after midterms, but this year starts from high valuations.

Forward (months–years)

US open 15:30 Paris. Arm and Qualcomm after the close. US October jobs report Friday 6 November 14:30 Paris.

AI companies
Mixed

AMD's big quarter, Arm and Qualcomm tonight

What happened

AMD reported third-quarter revenue of 13.4 billion dollars, up 45 percent, with data-centre revenue of 8.5 billion, up 97 percent, and adjusted earnings of 2.09 dollars a share against 1.95 expected (AMD press release, 3 November). It forecast about 17 billion dollars for the fourth quarter, plus or minus 3 percent. Its MI455X AI chip is shipping in volume, with Anthropic, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI and Oracle Cloud running its Helios server racks. Chief executive Lisa Su said 2027 revenue should grow 80 percent or more (Reuters, from the call, Claim). AMD is buying chip start-up Taalas and World Labs, whose founder Fei-Fei Li becomes AMD's chief scientist, and its MI430X was chosen for Europe's LUMI-AI supercomputer. Arm and Qualcomm report after the US close tonight (about 22:05 Paris). Anthropic is reported to plan its stock-market listing after the midterms (Claim).

Context (what it sits on)

AMD's numbers show demand for AI chips is still growing fast, which eases fears of a collapse in spending. They also show buyers are spreading orders beyond Nvidia, which matters for prices and profits across the industry.

Forward (months–years)

Arm and Qualcomm tonight, CoreWeave Tuesday 10 November, Nvidia Tuesday 17 November.

Iran and the Gulf
Mixed

Hormuz talks stuck, US strikes confirmed

What happened

Iran said the US response to its seven-point plan for reopening the Strait of Hormuz, which asks for sanctions relief, an end to the US naval blockade and release of frozen funds, was 'unconvincing', and President Trump confirmed recent US strikes, including on Qeshm Island (Al Jazeera). Qatar's foreign ministry says both sides remain engaged and Qatar is working towards an interim deal (Qatar MOFA, Middle East Eye). Attacks on tankers continue.

Context (what it sits on)

A deal would reopen a route for about a fifth of the world's oil and pull down fuel prices and inflation. Democrats campaigned on the cost of the Iran war, so Congress may press harder on war funding from January.

Forward (months–years)

Qatari mediation this week.

Tesla
Fact

China sales dip, Cybercab answers kept private

What happened

Tesla's China-made wholesale sales (domestic plus exports) were 67,829 in October, up 10.3 percent from 61,497 a year earlier but down 28.9 percent from 95,366 in September and the lowest since February (China Passenger Car Association, CPCA, preliminary). January to October totals 810,889, up 21.4 percent. China's purchase incentives ended on 31 October. A US National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) record shows Tesla answered request AQ26002 and asked to keep all documents on how the Cybercab was certified to federal safety standards confidential, and the inquiry stays open with no finding. In Europe the Dutch regulator RDW approved an update that caps the speed margin at plus 10 percent and renames the system 'Tesla Assisted Driving', now recognised in 10 countries. The EU vehicle committee (TCMV) has never voted on it, and a vote on Tuesday 1 December is a claim only. A free-trade-zone subzone was approved for Megapack batteries at Brookshire, Texas, which defers duties but not China tariffs. Tesla and SpaceX have not agreed any merger.

Context (what it sits on)

China is Tesla's second-biggest market and the end of incentives may weigh on November. Keeping the Cybercab answers private delays any public view of whether the car, which has no steering wheel, meets safety rules.

Forward (months–years)

Germany KBA and UK SMMT October registrations Wednesday 4 to Thursday 5 November, China retail and export split about 10 to 12 November, annual meeting Thursday 3 December 22:00 Paris.

France
Fact

Budget revenue vote nears, rejection expected

What happened

The National Assembly resumes the revenue half of the 2027 budget at 14:00 and 21:30 today with 369 amendments left (Assembly agenda), and a vote could come Wednesday night or Thursday morning (Ouest-France, citing finance committee chair Eric Coquerel). Deputies have struck out a 5 billion euro surtax on big companies and added a 10 percent tax on share buybacks and a restored exit tax. A rejection is widely expected, in which case the government's original text goes to the Senate. The overall first-reading deadline stays Tuesday 17 November.

Context (what it sits on)

A rejection would wipe out the deputies' changes and push France closer to forcing the budget through, raising political risk for the minority government and its borrowing costs.

Forward (months–years)

Revenue vote Wednesday night or Thursday 5 November, full first-reading deadline Tuesday 17 November.

Ukraine
Fact

What happened

President Zelensky said 33 people were killed in the strike on a school used as an aid centre in Komyshuvakha, Zaporizhzhia region, with more than 50 possibly trapped (Guardian). US-mediated working-level talks between Ukraine and Russia in Abu Dhabi remain planned for 10 and 11 November (RFE/RL).

Context (what it sits on)

Winter strikes on the grid raise pressure on Ukraine before talks. A Democratic Congress from January is likely to be more supportive of aid to Ukraine.

Forward (months–years)

Abu Dhabi talks Tuesday 10 and Wednesday 11 November.
