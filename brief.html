Brazil
Fact

Flávio Bolsonaro 47%, Lula 45%. Runoff Sun 25 Oct. Abstention ~21%

What happened

Sunday 4 October first round. Near-final tallies carried by G1 with about 99.98 percent of ballot boxes counted put Flávio Bolsonaro of the Liberal Party at 56,100,550 votes, or 47.05 percent of the valid vote, and President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva of the Workers' Party at 53,857,857 votes, or 45.15 percent. The gap is about 2.24 million votes, or 1.90 percentage points. Neither reached 50 percent of valid votes, so a runoff is set for Sunday 25 October. Band and HubPolítico feeds near 99.7 to 99.9 percent showed about 47.1 and 45.1 percent. Folha, citing the electoral court near 99.9 percent, put abstention at about 21.08 percent, or about 33.4 million people, a first-round record in the modern series, with blank votes about 1.84 percent and nulls about 2.93 percent. The BBC said Bolsonaro's Liberal Party was leading early tallies for governors, senators and federal deputies. Lula called the result unexpected and said the race was not over. Flávio said he was very happy and spoke of a modern government in three weeks. Thursday's Datafolha had Lula at 42 and Flávio at 38, so Flávio overperformed the last big poll.

Context (what it sits on)

Brazil is Latin America's largest electorate. A runoff between the incumbent left and the son of the former right-wing president will decide the presidency for the next four years. Congressional and gubernatorial results shape whether the winner can govern. Friday morning's brief had only the Datafolha poll. The count is the new fact.

Forward (months–years)

Watch the runoff campaign through Sunday 25 October, and how Liberal Party strength in Congress and statehouses hardens or softens. Treat any new poll as a poll, not a forecast of the second round.

Energy
Fact

Fri settle: Brent Dec $102.25 / WTI Nov $91.11. OPEC+ holds Nov quotas. G7 100mb release. Mon Asia lower

What happened

Friday 2 October settlements. Brent for December delivery, the front month, settled at 102.25 dollars a barrel, down 6 cents or 0.06 percent from Thursday's 102.31 (Reuters via London Stock Exchange and WAM, EnergyNow). West Texas Intermediate for November delivery settled at 91.11, down 1.76 dollars or 1.90 percent from Thursday's 92.87. Reuters put Brent up 0.11 percent for the week on a like-for-like front-month basis and West Texas Intermediate down about 1.6 percent. The same Friday, Group of Seven leaders agreed to a coordinated International Energy Agency release of 100 million barrels of diesel and crude from emergency reserves after a French proposal for about 50 million barrels of European diesel and 50 million barrels of International Energy Agency crude, with a substantial diesel share front-loaded (Élysée, Consilium, Reuters). President Trump said Europe would release diesel and that the United States would not ban diesel exports. On Sunday 4 October the seven OPEC Plus countries that hold voluntary cuts, Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Kazakhstan, Algeria and Oman, decided to keep September required production for November unchanged, with the next review on 1 November (OPEC.org). Early Monday Asia, Newsquawk labelled West Texas Intermediate November 2026 down about 1.1 percent and Brent December 2026 down about 0.7 percent. Reuters at about 01:15 GMT had Brent at 101.90, down 35 cents, and West Texas Intermediate at 90.49, down 62 cents, without naming the month in that wire. Those levels match the Friday December and November settles as the reference. A later Economic Times print had Brent near 101.60 and West Texas Intermediate near 90.15. Monday Asia figures are intraday, not settles. Saudi Aramco on Monday cut November Arab Light official selling prices to Asia by 3 dollars, to 5 dollars below the Oman and Dubai average, the widest discount since June 2020, cut heavier grades by 5 dollars, raised northwest Europe by 3 dollars, and left United States prices unchanged (Reuters).

Context (what it sits on)

Thursday's jump sat on a reported Chinese halt to October fuel exports beyond Hong Kong and Macau and on newspaper reports of more American forces for the Gulf. Friday's drop sat on the stock release. The United States–Iran war since 28 February still constrains Hormuz. Product tightness remains the sharper story than crude availability as Middle East crude loadings recover. Quote Brent December and West Texas Intermediate November.

Forward (months–years)

Watch how fast the 100 million barrels reach the physical market, whether Beijing confirms the fuel-export halt after the mainland holiday ends Wednesday 7 October, and Monday's Aramco Asia discount as a market-share move against high freight. Next OPEC Plus review is Sunday 1 November.

US economy
Fact

Sep payrolls +29,000. Unemployment 4.2%. August revised to +133,000. Dow 51,176.96

What happened

The Bureau of Labor Statistics said on Friday that nonfarm payrolls rose by 29,000 in September. Unemployment rose to 4.2 percent from 4.1 percent. August was revised to a gain of 133,000 from the previously published 162,000, and the prior two months together were revised down by about 60,000. Private payrolls rose 46,000 and government fell 17,000. Labour-force participation rose to 61.8 percent. Average hourly earnings were up 3.0 percent on the year. Consensus surveys before the print had looked for roughly 84,000 to 95,000 jobs. After the report, write-ups citing the CME FedWatch tool put the chance of a rate rise at the late-October Federal Reserve meeting around 13 to 23 percent, down from about 28 percent on Thursday and from the mid-to-high 60s a week earlier. Those odds were not opened on the tool this morning. Friday's close, from market recaps, was Dow 51,176.96, up 0.49 percent, S&P 500 at 7,722.72, up 0.73 percent, and Nasdaq Composite at 27,190.86, up 1.19 percent.

Context (what it sits on)

The Federal Reserve under Chair Kevin Warsh raised rates on 16 September to 3.75 to 4.00 percent because the war lifted energy costs. Soft hiring with still-elevated fuel prices leaves the October meeting open. The midterm election is Tuesday 3 November.

Forward (months–years)

Next dated labour prints and the Federal Open Market Committee meeting of 27 to 28 October. No new government-funding deadline was re-read from the statute this morning. The stopgap previously briefed still points at 11 December.

Iran
Mixed

US reply under review, unpublished. Qalibaf: Hormuz shut until seven conditions. Third carrier still unconfirmed

What happened

Iranian officials said over the weekend that Tehran is reviewing the American response to its seven-day Hormuz proposal. Parliament speaker Mohammad Baqer Qalibaf said on Sunday that the Strait of Hormuz will not reopen until seven conditions from the Islamabad memorandum are met. Foreign ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei characterised the United States counterproposal as still centred on nuclear issues and said Iran's focus is the strait, including an end to disruptions of Iranian shipping and sanctions relief. The reply text itself has not been published by either side. Newspaper and official-anonymous accounts still describe a third United States aircraft carrier strike group, the Theodore Roosevelt, and an amphibious group adding roughly 9,000 to 10,000 troops toward the region by late November. No Pentagon release confirming those force numbers was found this morning, so the deployment stays a Claim inside this row. The United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations continued to report vessel incidents on the Gulf side of the waterway.

Context (what it sits on)

The war since 28 February still constrains shipping. Trump last week rejected Iran's seven-day plan and said Iran would either sign a fair deal or not exist. Thursday's oil jump sat partly on the force reports. Friday's stock-release plan sat on product tightness.

Forward (months–years)

Watch whether either side publishes the reply, and whether a named ship movement confirms the third carrier. A credible reopening path would hit December Brent (Speculation). The United States midterm election is Tuesday 3 November.

Ukraine
Fact

Night into Mon: 205 drones, 49 jet. 172 down by 08:00 Kyiv. Northern Bridge hit again Sun

What happened

Ukraine's air force said that from 18:00 on Sunday Russia launched 205 Shahed-type attack drones, 49 of them jet-powered, plus Gerbera, Banderol and Parodiya decoys. By 08:00 Monday Kyiv time, air defence had destroyed or jammed 172. Hits were recorded at 13 locations and debris at three. About ten drones were still airborne, so the count is not final (Ukrinform). On Sunday the Northern Bridge in Kyiv was struck again, injuring two people, in a multi-day campaign against the capital's Dnipro crossings after earlier hits on the Southern Bridge. The overnight count into Sunday was 135 drones with 52 jet-powered and about 123 to 125 destroyed or jammed. President Zelensky said that in the week Russia had used more than 1,900 attack drones, more than 800 of them jet-powered, and 25 missiles. Germany's chancellor made a surprise visit to Kyiv over the weekend (BBC).

Context (what it sits on)

Jet-powered drones are harder to stop than propeller models. Repeated bridge hits force closures and raise the risk that key crossings become unusable into winter. Friday morning's brief had a separate night of 108 drones. Do not add nights together as one raid.

Forward (months–years)

Watch the final interception and casualty count later today, and whether pontoon or diversion plans are activated in Kyiv. European interceptors that can reach jet drones remain the equipment question.

SpaceX
Fact

NROL-97 success. Falcon year count 114. SDA Tranche 1 targeted 10:17 Paris today, not flown

What happened

Falcon Heavy NROL-97 is a confirmed success. SpaceX writes the flight in the past tense, liftoff at 11:54 p.m. Eastern on Thursday 1 October, which is 03:54 UTC and 05:54 Paris on Friday 2 October, from Kennedy Space Center pad 39A, with side boosters landing at Landing Zones 1 and 2. The National Reconnaissance Office launch page says the mission was successfully launched. That takes the Falcon orbital year count from 113 to 114. A Falcon Heavy counts as one launch. Starship flights are not in that count. Tracker notes for Saturday and Monday found no further Falcon family success over the weekend, so the year-to-date stays 114. The Space Development Agency Tranche 1 Transport Layer A Falcon 9 from Vandenberg is still only a target. SpaceX is targeting liftoff at 1:17 a.m. Pacific time on Monday 5 October, which is 08:17 UTC and 10:17 Paris, with a backup on Tuesday 6 October at the same clock time, also 10:17 Paris. The live SpaceX page is still future tense. It had not flown as of the Monday tracker note around 08:25 Paris. Do not write an outcome. SpaceX says the booster is on its sixth flight, will land at Landing Zone 4, and will deliver 21 data-transport satellites. KeepTrack remains flat at 12,988 launched, 11,156 in orbit and 11,142 working. The Google graphics-chip lease at the Colossus site is still not confirmed as operating.

Context (what it sits on)

Friday morning's brief held NROL-97 as not a confirmed success and the Falcon count at 113. Both conditions are gone. Crew-13 and Transporter-18 remain settled inside that path to 114.

Forward (months–years)

If today's Space Development Agency attempt succeeds, the Falcon year count would move to 115. A scrub leaves 114. Starlink Group 15-25 still shows a window opening at 01:00 Paris on Sunday 11 October. Cargo Dragon CRS-35 shows Go on Launch Library 2 for Tuesday 13 October at 12:33 Paris, while NASA's page still says no earlier than fall 2026, so that clock is Mixed. Starship Flight 15 is still only October with no day from SpaceX.

Ethiopia
Mixed

Eritrea severs ties after embassy closure and diplomat expulsions. Addis blasts still unconfirmed as strikes

What happened

After Ethiopia announced on Thursday that it would close its embassy in Asmara and ordered ten Eritrean diplomats to leave Addis Ababa within 48 hours, Eritrea said it was severing all diplomatic ties in response. Ethiopia also declared an Egyptian diplomat persona non grata. Explosions were reported in the capital overnight into Thursday near defence sites after a drone-flight ban. A source linked to the opposition alliance told Reuters the group had hit military sites. That claim is not independently confirmed. Weekend coverage largely confirmed the diplomatic break rather than adding a verified new battle tally. No confirmed closure of the Djibouti road corridor was found.

Context (what it sits on)

Friday morning's brief already had the embassy closure and the reported blasts. What is settled over the weekend is Eritrea's formal break and the mutual expulsion dynamic with Egypt. The Djibouti corridor remains Ethiopia's main sea access.

Forward (months–years)

Watch whether the ten diplomats actually left, whether a second source confirms strikes in Addis Ababa, and whether the road to Djibouti is interrupted. A wider war with Eritrea would sit next to the Hormuz shock as a second trade-route risk (Speculation until a corridor closure or a cross-border clash is confirmed).
