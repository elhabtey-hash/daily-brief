Iran
Mixed

Trump: no strikes before the midterms, talks 'productive'. Pentagon three-day plan reported. Tanker attacks spread beyond Hormuz

What happened

President Donald Trump posted on Thursday 8 October that the United States would not attack Iran before the midterm elections on Tuesday 3 November, that Washington is having productive discussions with Tehran, and that the US naval blockade stays in force (Truth Social, reported by Politico, CNBC, AP and The Wall Street Journal). The New York Times, citing US officials, reported that the Pentagon has drawn up plans for a short, intense campaign of about three days against Iran's rebuilt missile and drone arsenal, energy sites and Revolutionary Guard command posts, that Trump has rejected five earlier proposals for major operations against Iran or the Houthis, and that three US aircraft carriers will soon be in the region. At sea, the UK Maritime Trade Operations centre said several projectiles hit the Antigua and Barbuda-flagged tanker Acers 51 nautical miles north of Qatar's northern tip on Wednesday, with casualties, and a second tanker caught fire off Fujairah in the Gulf of Oman (Euronews). Outlets close to Iran's Revolutionary Guard suggested Tehran has extended its operations against ships beyond the strait, and Iran said it would close a route through Hormuz being used to get around its own blockade (The Guardian).

Context (what it sits on)

The war that began on 28 February has left Hormuz largely shut by rival Iranian and US blockades. A British Navy-run monitor counts at least 13 vessels attacked since September. Trump's statement reverses the risk flagged in Thursday's book, but it is a political pledge, not a ceasefire.

Forward (months–years)

Watch whether tanker attacks keep spreading to Qatar and Gulf of Oman waters, any formal Iranian reply through Qatar, and the carrier build-up. A US strike after 3 November remains possible (Speculation).

Energy
Fact

Thu settle: Brent Dec $104.28 (+4.08), WTI Nov $91.49 (+3.21). Fri Asia Brent Dec ~$103. Isaias shuts in 63% of Gulf oil, landfall tonight or early Sat

What happened

Thursday 8 October settlements. Brent for December delivery, the front month, settled at 104.28 dollars a barrel, up 4.08 dollars or 4.1 percent from Wednesday's December settle of 100.20. West Texas Intermediate for November delivery, the front month, settled at 91.49 dollars, up 3.21 dollars or 3.6 percent from Wednesday's November settle of 88.28, after touching 93.20 during the session (Reuters via Yahoo Finance, DTN). Both changes reconcile exactly with Wednesday's December and November settles. December Brent touched its highest since 29 September. Drivers were renewed tanker attacks, the Iran strike reports and the hurricane. Friday Asia, intraday and not settles: December Brent traded at about 102.93 to 103.53 dollars, down about 1 percent, after Trump ruled out strikes before the midterms (The Hindu BusinessLine, Zee Business). Hurricane Isaias: the National Hurricane Center's intermediate advisory 10A at 06:00 UTC (08:00 Paris) put the centre about 230 miles (375 kilometres) south of the mouth of the Mississippi, moving north-north-east at 14 miles an hour, with winds of 105 miles an hour (165 kilometres an hour), a Category 2. It says Isaias could briefly become a major hurricane today and will make landfall within the warning area, from Ocean Springs, Mississippi, to the Bay and Gulf county line in Florida, tonight or early on Saturday 10 October. Storm surge of 5 to 7 feet is possible from Dauphin Island, Alabama, to Indian Pass, Florida, and rainfall up to 15 inches locally. Landfall has not happened at filing. The Marine Minerals Administration, the new US offshore regulator that merged the old BSEE and BOEM, estimated on Thursday that 1,282,879 barrels a day of Gulf oil, 62.89 percent, and 57.35 percent of gas output were shut in, with 121 of 371 manned platforms evacuated, up from about 25 percent on Wednesday.

Context (what it sits on)

Crude had dipped below 100 dollars on easing tensions earlier this month. The hurricane takes out a large share of US offshore supply for several days, and Energy Aspects estimates about 0.5 million barrels a day of refining capacity, including Pascagoula, sits in the storm's path (via Oil Authority, secondary). US distillate stocks are more than 15 percent below a year ago (DTN).

Forward (months–years)

Watch landfall and refinery damage tonight and Saturday, the regulator's daily 13:00 Central (20:00 Paris) shut-in update, and restart speed next week. West Texas Intermediate November expires on Tuesday 20 October, Brent December on Friday 30 October. OPEC Plus meets on Sunday 1 November.

Ukraine
Fact

Glide bomb on two buses in Kramatorsk kills 33. Kyiv emergency blackouts. Ukraine hits Salavat refinery. Kremlin sees 'no clear path' to peace

What happened

A Russian guided glide bomb struck next to two city buses at a stop in Kramatorsk, in the eastern Donetsk region, on Thursday morning 8 October. Regional authorities and prosecutors put the toll at 33 dead and 18 wounded (RFE/RL), and AP and Al Jazeera reported at least 30. President Volodymyr Zelensky said Ukraine would respond. Later a drone killed two more people in Kramatorsk. The toll from Wednesday's missile strike on an apartment block in Pryluky stands at 22 dead, including five children, with at least 50 wounded (AP, CBS). A Russian strike on an energy facility important to Kyiv led grid operator Ukrenergo to order emergency power cuts in the capital, disrupting the metro and water supply, with all-day restrictions in some regions on Friday (Kyiv Independent, AP). Ukraine said it hit the Gazprom Neftekhim Salavat refinery and petrochemical complex in Bashkortostan, and Russia's Yandex said one of its five largest data centres, in the Ryazan region, was attacked (RFE/RL, not independently verified). Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said he saw no clear path to a peaceful resolution. In a side row, South Korea said it will recall its ambassador to Kyiv after Ukraine publicly disclosed the transfer of two North Korean prisoners of war to Seoul (Yonhap).

Context (what it sits on)

Kramatorsk is one of the main Donetsk cities Russia has not captured. Russia is stepping up air attacks on cities and the power grid before winter, while Ukraine hits Russian refineries deep inside the country. Talks proposed by the United States in the United Arab Emirates still have no date from Moscow.

Forward (months–years)

Watch Kyiv's power restoration, the next mass strike, and whether Western allies speed up air-defence deliveries. Casualty figures may still rise.

Nobel Prize
Fact

Literature to Anne Carson (Canada). Peace today 11:00 Paris, after filing. Economics Mon 12 Oct

What happened

The Swedish Academy announced on Thursday 8 October that the 2026 Nobel Prize in Literature is awarded to the Canadian author Anne Carson "for her bold and inventive oeuvre that, in playful dialogue with the classical tradition, has created new forms for contemporary literature" (nobelprize.org press release). Carson, 76, is a poet, essayist and translator of ancient Greek who has taught classics for more than 30 years. Her best-known books include Autobiography of Red, a novel in verse, The Glass Essay, Nox and The Beauty of the Husband. Permanent secretary Ingrid Carlberg said Carson was in Iceland, listening to the wind, when she was told (BBC). Committee chair Anders Olsson said she has broken away from lyric poetry tied to the narrating self. The prize is worth 12 million Swedish kronor.

Context (what it sits on)

Alice Munro, in 2013, was the last Canadian to win the Literature prize. This week's earlier prizes went to Deisseroth, Hegemann and Nagel (Medicine, optogenetics), Francis Halzen (Physics, IceCube) and Henri Kagan and Kenso Soai (Chemistry).

Forward (months–years)

The Peace Prize is announced in Oslo today, Friday 9 October, at 11:00 Paris, after this filing. No winner is written here. Economics follows on Monday 12 October at 11:45 Paris.

Ethiopia
Mixed

Monitor: Ethiopian drones hit Eritrean troops near Adigrat as they pull back. Addis demands withdrawal

What happened

The US-based conflict monitor ACLED said Eritrean soldiers crossed into Ethiopia on Wednesday, advanced dozens of kilometres into Tigray, then withdrew under heavy drone strikes believed to be carried out by Ethiopia's military near Adigrat on Wednesday and Thursday (Reuters via The Straits Times). A diplomat and a conflict expert said Eritreans were moving back north, but it is unclear whether they are leaving Ethiopia or redeploying. A senior Ethiopian official, speaking anonymously, claimed Eritrea has one mechanised and three infantry divisions in Tigray of about 1,500 soldiers each. Foreign Minister Gedion Timothewos told UN Deputy Secretary-General Amina Mohammed that Eritrean forces must withdraw immediately and that Ethiopia has the right to defend itself. Reuters could not verify the accounts.

Context (what it sits on)

This is the biggest escalation since the 2020 to 2022 Tigray war. Eritrea accuses Ethiopia of plotting to seize a Red Sea port, and the two cut diplomatic ties last week. The two fought a border war in 1998 to 2000.

Forward (months–years)

Watch whether Eritrean forces leave Tigray, African Union or UN mediation, and any direct Ethiopian strike inside Eritrea, which would mark interstate war (Speculation).

Ebola
Fact

Kenya traces 57 contacts after its first imported case died. Congo outbreak still spreading

What happened

Kenya is tracing 57 contacts of its first imported Bundibugyo Ebola case, including flight passengers, crew, and airport and hospital staff, and 10 are in quarantine (Kenyan health ministry via APO, NBC). The patient, a Kenyan living in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, travelled through Uganda, arrived in Nairobi on 3 October and died days later. Kenya has stepped up airport and border screening. The World Health Organization's latest count, given on Wednesday, was 8,665 confirmed cases and 4,178 confirmed deaths in Congo.

Context (what it sits on)

This is Congo's deadliest Ebola outbreak, declared on 15 May, and there is no approved vaccine or treatment for the Bundibugyo strain.

Forward (months–years)

Watch Kenya's contacts through the 21-day incubation window, any case in Uganda along the travel route, and the vaccine trial due later in October.

SpaceX
Fact

Deal for Grain's nationwide 800 MHz spectrum to make Starlink Mobile a US carrier. FCC licence for 15,000 direct-to-phone satellites. Crew-12 home

What happened

SpaceX signed a definitive agreement on Thursday 8 October to buy all of Grain Management's nationwide 800 megahertz spectrum, up to 14 megahertz of paired low-band airwaves, for an undisclosed price, subject to Federal Communications Commission approval (Grain press release). SpaceX says it would let Starlink Mobile become a US mobile carrier using both satellite and ground spectrum. Grain bought the spectrum from T-Mobile for 2.9 billion dollars plus other licences. On Tuesday 6 October the FCC authorised, in part, a 15,000-satellite Starlink Mobile direct-to-device constellation in nine shells at 326 to 335 kilometres, half to be deployed by October 2032 (Via Satellite, Light Reading, press readings of the order). After hours on Thursday AT&T fell 7.9 percent, Verizon 7.2 percent and T-Mobile 7.5 percent, and SpaceX stock rose 1.1 percent to 162.49 dollars (MarketWatch, not closing prices). NASA's Crew-12 Dragon splashed down off Los Angeles at 17:34 Paris on Thursday (NASA). No Falcon flew, so the 2026 count stays at 114, and there is no new Securities and Exchange Commission filing.

Context (what it sits on)

Owning low-band ground spectrum would let SpaceX compete directly with the big three US carriers rather than only partner with them. The newer direct-to-device satellites need Starship and would deploy from 2027 (SpaceNews).
