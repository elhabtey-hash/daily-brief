Morocco: PAM 97 seats; turnout 38.02%

What happened

Morocco's Interior Minister Abdelouafi Laftit released official preliminary results on Thursday 24 September from Wednesday's legislative election for all 395 Chambre des représentants seats. The Authenticity and Modernity Party (PAM) finished first with 97 seats; the National Rally of Independents (RNI), which headed the outgoing coalition, took 66; the Istiqlal Party 65; and the Islamist Justice and Development Party (PJD) rebounded to 54 from only 13 in 2021 (Reuters / Interior briefing). Turnout was 38.02 percent — down from 50 percent in 2021 and the lowest since about 2007 — on roughly 15.8 million registered voters out of about 27 million people of voting age. No party reached the 198-seat majority. Under the 2011 constitution King Mohammed VI appoints the head of government from the plurality party; PAM had ruled out renewing a coalition with RNI if PAM came first, so coalition arithmetic is now the near political fight. Coverage notes PAM leader Fatima Ezzahra El Mansouri and budget minister / football-federation chief Fouzi Lekjaa as possible government tracks — who the King names remains Claim until announced.

Context (what it sits on)

Wednesday's ballot closed a competitive three-way race among the outgoing RNI–PAM–Istiqlal coalition partners. Purchasing power, youth disengagement, jobs pledges, and a roughly 20-billion-dollar infrastructure push toward the 2030 World Cup co-host with Spain and Portugal sat on the campaign surface. The King retains sweeping powers over defence and foreign policy regardless of coalition shape.

Forward (months–years)

Watch the royal appointment, coalition partners beyond RNI, and whether low turnout becomes a legitimacy story into World Cup delivery years. Morocco's government formation is the near North Africa political hinge for this book.
