Mixed

Board reverses itself: Huang reinstated as chief executive after 'input from senior levels of the US government'

What happened

In an 8-K filing (Item 5.02) and press release on Mon 23 Nov, filed just before the US open, Nvidia said its board voted unanimously on Sun 22 Nov to reinstate Jensen Huang as President, Chief Executive Officer and a director, effective immediately (Fact, company statement). The board said it was 'satisfied with respect to Mr. Huang's candor and ability to lead NVIDIA' after meetings with him, and that 'the Board received input from senior levels of the United States government, and based on that input, the Board is confident that Mr. Huang's reinstatement poses no national security risk.' Chair Harvey Jones stays chair; Colette Kress returns to being Chief Financial Officer only after 11 days as interim chief executive. The independent directors' investigation 'will proceed to conclusion', a security-focused oversight committee is hiring national-security counsel, and Nvidia has begun a search for a new Chief Compliance Officer. The shares closed Monday at $165.05, up 31.48 percent on about 779 million shares, their biggest one-day gain since 2003 (Nvidia investor site; CNBC), restoring close to $1 trillion of market value (Reuters).

Context (what it sits on)

The reversal comes six days after Jones said on the 17 Nov earnings call that the board did not intend to bring Huang back; he had resigned per the 8-K of 12 Nov, effective 11 November. President Trump told CNBC he pressed the board and assured it Huang posed no risk, and Reuters writes that the move came under his pressure (Claim on the influence; the 8-K speaks only of 'senior levels' of government). Lawmakers of both parties objected: Senator Elizabeth Warren, who chairs the Senate Banking Committee from January, called it 'reckless', Senate Intelligence chair Tom Cotton urged passage of the Chip Security Act, which would make chipmakers track where advanced chips end up, and Republican Senator John Curtis asked 'what changed' (Reuters, NBC, Axios). Analysts upgraded in a cluster: Melius, Bernstein, StoneX and Oppenheimer moved to Buy or Outperform, and Citi raised its target to $240 (CNBC, TipRanks; Claim as opinions). General counsel Tim Teter's Form 4 confirms a pre-planned sale of 30,000 shares on 19 Nov for $4.54 million (Fact, SEC). Still open: no 8-K on California Attorney General Rob Bonta's investigative subpoena of 19 Nov or on the reported SEC accounting inquiry, and the quarterly 10-Q is not filed.

Forward (months–years)

Dates: replies to Senator Michael Bennet's letter on chip exports are due today (none public as of 06:47 CET); Treasury Secretary Bessent's informal ask for answers runs to about Wed 25 Nov. Huang's closed-door House interview stands for Tue 1 Dec, now as sitting chief executive, and Teter's for Wed 2 Dec. GTC Washington runs Mon 30 Nov to Thu 3 Dec, with a keynote on Tue 1 Dec at 20:00 CET (speaker not reconfirmed) and a UBS conference talk at 15:35 CET. Banking Committee chair Tim Scott said on Monday that Huang will testify in public on Thu 3 Dec (Reuters), but no official hearing notice was found, so the date remains a claim. The 10-Q is due by about Fri 4 Dec. Watch whether Monday's jump holds and whether Congress moves chip-tracking rules into the annual defence bill, as incoming Speaker Hakeem Jeffries urged. Links: Markets, AI companies, US.

AI
Mixed

Riemann proof: very likely correct, not yet understood; OpenAI ships a chat version of GPT-6.2

What happened

Since Friday's claim by Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge that he proved the Riemann hypothesis working with Claude, the evidence has firmed but not settled. The proof exists only as more than 17 million lines of code in Lean, a language whose checker verifies every logical step; Alpöge says an AI-written English version ran past 250,000 pages (The Conversation, 22 Nov). Mathematician Daniel Litt summed up the state of play: the Lean-formalised proof is 'exceedingly likely to be correct' and 'no human understands it yet.' The Lean Focused Research Organization and the Mathlib community have said they believe it is correct. No statement from the Clay Mathematics Institute, which offers a 1 million dollar prize, was found.

Context (what it sits on)

The Riemann hypothesis, posed in 1859, predicts a hidden regularity in how prime numbers are spread out. Alpöge's code actually claims the broader generalised Riemann hypothesis. A separate, entirely human-written 940-page proof by Stanford's Alessandro Masullo, released the same day by American Mathematical Society president Ravi Vakil, had potential problems flagged within hours (The Conversation). Separately, OpenAI released on Mon 23 Nov a version of GPT-6.2 tuned for ChatGPT conversations for Plus and Pro subscribers, with a published safety system card (OpenAI).

Forward (months–years)

A Lean check alone does not satisfy the Clay prize, which requires publication and two years of acceptance, so the community verdict will take months. Watch for a human-readable write-up, which Alpöge has promised, and expert checks of the Masullo draft. Links: AI companies (Anthropic).

Science
Fact

Deepest explosive eruption ever found, in the Mariana Trench

What happened

A study published on Mon 23 Nov in Frontiers in Earth Science reports a pyroclastic deposit, the remains of an avalanche of hot rock, ash and gas, at about 6,150 metres (20,170 feet) on the wall of the Challenger Deep, found by the Woods Hole robotic submersible Jason/Medea (National Geographic). The previous record was about half that depth.

Context (what it sits on)

Water pressure at that depth should suppress the gas expansion that drives explosive eruptions, so the find challenges models of underwater volcanism; co-author Robert Stern called it 'completely unexpected'. Separately, NASA released new James Webb Space Telescope images of the Cassiopeia A supernova remnant.

Forward (months–years)

Watch for follow-up dives; few submersibles can reach these depths.
