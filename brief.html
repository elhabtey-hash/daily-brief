Taiwan, Korea and Japan (lead)
Fact

Chip stocks slump across Asia

What happened

Official Monday closes: Taiwan’s main index (Taiex) fell 2,635.04 points, or 6.03 percent, to 41,040.08 (Central News Agency), with chipmaker TSMC down 5.74 percent; South Korea’s Kospi fell 190.03 points, or 3.59 percent, to 5,100.50 (Yonhap); Japan’s Nikkei 225 fell 1,480.33 points, or 2.42 percent, to 59,662.02 (Nikkei). Hong Kong’s Hang Seng traded higher.

Context (what it sits on)

These markets are heavy with AI hardware makers, such as TSMC, SK Hynix and Samsung, that supply Nvidia. Investors are reassessing the AI spending boom after Huang’s resignation and before Nvidia’s results. Friday’s closes in our last brief (Taiwan 43,675.12, Nikkei 61,142.35, Kospi 5,290.53) are confirmed by today’s point changes.

Forward (months–years)

Watch whether European chip stocks follow at the open and whether Nvidia’s results on Tuesday night steady the sector. Watch for any official market-stabilisation steps in Taipei (none announced at filing).

Taiwan, Korea and Japan (lead)
Fact

Japan’s economy shrinks slightly in the summer quarter

What happened

The Cabinet Office says real gross domestic product (GDP) fell 0.02 percent in July to September from the previous quarter, an annualised fall of 0.1 percent and the first contraction in four quarters. Economists had expected growth of about 0.2 to 0.3 percent annualised. Household spending fell 0.3 percent; business investment rose 1.6 percent, helped by AI-related servers and chip equipment.

Context (what it sits on)

Imports of replacement oil after the Hormuz disruption rose 2.5 percent, and net exports cut 0.6 points from growth. Household energy subsidies ended in September. Underlying domestic demand (final sales) fell 2.1 percent annualised, which State Street calls a loss of momentum.

Forward (months–years)

The Bank of Japan meets in December; LSEG data put the chance of a rate rise at about 76 percent (via CNBC). A second GDP estimate follows in early December.

US air travel
Fact

Nationwide ground stop and weekend disruption

What happened

At about 21:30 CET on Friday the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) halted departures at all US airports after telecommunications problems disrupted communications and radar at several regional air traffic control centres. Officials said new equipment overloaded older parts of the system. Reuters says it was the longest nationwide stop since 11 September 2001.

Context (what it sits on)

Airlines cancelled more than 6,400 flights on Saturday and about 2,300 on Sunday, with 7,700 delays (FlightAware via Reuters). The outage adds to long-running concerns about outdated air traffic systems. Airline shares fell 1.4 percent in Friday’s final minutes.

Forward (months–years)

Transportation Secretary Duffy expects normal operations on Monday. Watch for a formal FAA account of the cause and any congressional hearings.

Energy
Fact

Oil settles flat as the market tips into surplus

What happened

Friday 13 November settlements: January Brent rose 2 cents to $95.84 a barrel; December West Texas Intermediate (WTI) fell 18 cents to $88.56 (Xinhua, PFL). Both fell about 2.5 percent on the week, a second weekly loss. December WTI is the front month until it expires on Fri 20 Nov.

Context (what it sits on)

The International Energy Agency’s monthly report said the market has moved into surplus for the first time since the Iran war began, of around 2 million barrels a day this quarter, and cut its 2026 demand estimate by 90,000 barrels a day. Gulf exports have recovered faster than demand. In Asian hours on Monday January Brent traded about 1 percent lower near $94.91 (AP, intraday, not a settlement).

Forward (months–years)

Watch the Gulf: mine and drone incidents near Hormuz are still pushing up risk. December WTI expires Fri 20 Nov, after which January WTI becomes the front month.

Iran and the Gulf
Mixed

Iranian drones at Bahrain; tankers hit in Hormuz

What happened

Iran’s Revolutionary Guards (IRGC) said they launched six drones at a US radar at Bahrain’s Riffa Air Base on Sunday, after claiming on Friday to have destroyed a US radar there. US Central Command said it shot down two drones on Friday with no damage; Bahrain says it intercepted several attacks on Sunday. The IRGC says two tankers on an ‘unauthorised’ route hit mines on Saturday and one supertanker with over 2 million barrels sank.

Iran’s chief negotiator Ghalibaf says Tehran sent seven conditions via Qatar and wants to move beyond ‘no war, no peace’.

Forward (months–years)

SpaceX
Fact

Flight 15: first ship catch, booster lost

What happened

Starship Flight 15 lifted off from Starbase, Texas, at about 23:48 CET on Fri 13 Nov and deployed 26 Starlink satellites. After 22.5 hours in orbit, the ship returned and was caught by the launch tower’s arms at about 22:21 CET on Sat 14 Nov, the first catch of a Starship upper stage (SpaceX mission page and posts). The Super Heavy Booster 22 cut its return burn short on low fuel, aborted automatically, diverted offshore, and its flight termination system fired after landing, so it was lost.

Context (what it sits on)

SpaceX says the root cause of the booster problem is under investigation; Musk blames slight engine underperformance and says the timeline is unchanged. Musk says Ship 42 could be reflown after a few weeks of refurbishment (claim). The FAA has issued no statement or mishap ruling; it ruled the Flight 12 booster loss a mishap in May. The FAA licence document for this flight is still not posted on its site.

Forward (months–years)

Watch for an FAA decision on Booster 22. Flight 16 is reported for no earlier than 10 December from Florida (secondary, not announced by SpaceX).

Science and technology
Fact

Vulcan rocket fails; Voyager nears one light-day

What happened

United Launch Alliance (ULA), owned by Boeing and Lockheed Martin, said its Vulcan rocket failed about 10 minutes after lift-off from Cape Canaveral on Sunday, destroying 40 Amazon Leo internet satellites. It was the rocket’s return to flight after a nine-month stand-down over its solid rocket boosters, and ULA’s first total mission failure. The cause is not yet known.

Context (what it sits on)

The loss sets back Amazon’s effort to build a rival to Starlink and leaves the US with fewer working heavy rockets. Separately, NASA says Voyager 1 will become the first human-made object one light-day from Earth on Wed 18 Nov at 11:16 CET, 49 years after launch.

Forward (months–years)

Watch for a ULA failure investigation, the effect on Amazon’s launch schedule, and US national security launches assigned to Vulcan.

Mixed

Talks split over the 1.5 degree goal

What happened

At the halfway point of the UN climate summit in Antalya, some countries want the final deal to say 1.5 degrees of warming is still ‘the right goal’, while the Arab Group and a bloc of developing countries including Saudi Arabia and India see room to drop it, observers told AFP. Forward (months–years)

The second week brings ministers; watch the draft text on 1.5 degrees and finance.

Context (what it sits on)

Forward (months–years)

Watch weekly case counts and any spread to neighbouring countries.
