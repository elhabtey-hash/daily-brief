US midterms
Speculation

Election day: what is at stake, all pending

What happened

Voting takes place today, Tuesday 3 November, for all 435 seats in the House of Representatives, 35 of the 100 Senate seats and 36 governors. Final forecasts give Democrats about a 95 percent chance of winning the House and about 80 percent for the Senate (Axios round-up of models and markets), and the Princeton Election Consortium's central Senate estimate is 52 Democrats to 48 Republicans, with a range of 49 to 56. The closest Senate races are North Carolina (Roy Cooper against Michael Whatley, Democrats' best pick-up chance), Ohio, Maine (Susan Collins seeking a sixth term), Iowa, Kansas, Nebraska, Texas and Alaska, while Democrats defend Michigan and Minnesota (NPR, MS NOW). Missouri votes on a Republican-drawn congressional map and an abortion measure. Polls close in Paris time from 00:00 (parts of Kentucky and Indiana), 01:00 (Georgia, Virginia), 01:30 (North Carolina, Ohio), 02:00 (16 states including Pennsylvania, Florida, Maine), 03:00 (Texas, Michigan, Iowa, Kansas, Arizona), 04:00, 05:00 (California) and 07:00 Wednesday (Alaska). No results are in.

Context (what it sits on)

If Democrats win the House, the President loses single-party control of Congress from January, which can slow tax, tariff and spending plans and opens the way to investigations. The Senate matters for judges and appointments. Close Senate races and Alaska's ranked-choice count could leave control unclear for days. Markets also watch for a US decision on Iran after the vote.

Forward (months–years)

Results overnight Paris time from 00:00 Wednesday 4 November. Forecasts are probabilities, not results.

Palantir
Fact

Revenue nearly doubles, outlook raised, signings slow

What happened

After the US close on Monday 2 November Palantir reported third-quarter revenue of 2.313 billion dollars, up 96 percent on a year earlier and well above its own guide of 2.160 to 2.164 billion and Street forecasts of about 2.17 billion. US commercial revenue, 984 million dollars (up 148 percent), passed US government revenue, 953 million (up 96 percent), for the first time. Adjusted operating income was 1.427 billion dollars, a 62 percent margin, and adjusted earnings per share were 0.44 dollars against about 0.41 expected. It guided fourth-quarter revenue of 2.649 to 2.653 billion dollars (Street about 2.46 billion) and raised full-year 2026 revenue to about 8.53 billion from about 8.15 billion. Soft spots: total contract value signed (TCV) was 2.918 billion dollars, up only 6 percent, deals over 10 million dollars fell to 64 from 73 in the second quarter, and remaining performance obligations (contracted future revenue) were flat on the quarter at 4.88 billion. The finance chief said some Sovereign AI deals are shorter. The shares closed at 178.44 dollars on Monday, down 3.08 percent, and were about 3.8 percent lower after hours (tracker, Palantir).

Context (what it sits on)

Palantir is the cleanest test of whether companies are actually paying for AI software, and the answer this quarter is strongly yes. The worry is the future pipeline: slower signings could mean growth peaks, which matters for a share price that already assumes years of rapid growth.

Forward (months–years)

Tuesday 3 November first full trading-day reaction (unknown at writing). 10-Q filing by mid-November.

Iran and the Gulf
Mixed

Deal hopes, no deal, a small US strike

What happened

Iranian negotiators left three days of talks in Doha with no breakthrough, but a draft proposal is circulating and Qatari mediators travelled to Tehran (Al-Monitor, Arab News). Iran's plan would have the US end its port blockade and ease oil and sanctions restrictions before Iran reopens the Strait of Hormuz. President Trump said 'No' when asked about striking Iran after the election and that a deal 'could happen'. Late on Monday the US hit a missile launcher on Iran's Qeshm Island after, US officials say, a missile was fired at a US aircraft (Wall Street Journal and Axios via Newsquawk). Saudi Arabia's defence minister discussed continued Houthi attacks with the US defence secretary.

Context (what it sits on)

A deal that reopens Hormuz, which carries about a fifth of the world's oil, would pull down oil prices and inflation worldwide. Until it is signed, each incident can reverse Monday's oil fall.

Forward (months–years)

Qatari shuttle diplomacy this week, any US move after the vote.

Australia
Fact

Rates at an 18-year high

What happened

The Reserve Bank of Australia (RBA) raised its cash rate by 0.25 points to 4.85 percent on Tuesday 3 November, its third rise this year, by six votes to three, with three members wanting a hold (RBA statement). It now expects inflation to stay above its 2 to 3 percent target through much of 2027, citing fuel prices from the Middle East war, tight capacity and AI investment driving up technology prices. Governor Bullock gave no guidance on the next move.

Context (what it sits on)

Australia is an early example of central banks raising rates again because of the oil shock, the same pressure facing the US Federal Reserve before its December meeting.

Forward (months–years)

Next RBA decision Tuesday 8 December.

AI companies
Mixed

AI spending doubts deepen, Apple beats

What happened

On Monday the S&P 500 fell 0.51 percent to 7,541.40 and the Nasdaq 1.07 percent to 25,164.32, while the Dow rose 0.41 percent to 51,983.98 (CNBC, MarketWatch closing data). Oracle fell 8 percent after announcing 10 billion dollars of new shares and 10 billion of bonds to fund AI data centres. Marvell agreed to buy optical-parts maker Lumentum in an all-stock deal valued at about 115 billion dollars, and Marvell fell about 13 percent. Apple reported July to September revenue of 114.48 billion dollars, up 12 percent, with iPhone revenue of 57.58 billion and earnings of 2.10 dollars a share against about 1.98 expected, in the first results under chief executive John Ternus. Greater China revenue of 17.54 billion missed forecasts and Apple guided a 45 to 46 percent gross margin, squeezed by memory and AI costs, and its shares fell about 2.7 percent after hours (Apple, Reuters, Newsquawk). Anthropic's amended IPO filing shows third-quarter revenue of 16.96 billion dollars and an operating loss of 567 million (tracker, Claim until checked in the filing). European futures point to a lower open.

Context (what it sits on)

Investors are now asking who pays for the AI build-out. Companies are raising money through share sales and debt, which is a sign the boom is getting more expensive to fund. Rising memory prices are also starting to hit consumer devices like iPhones.

Forward (months–years)

AMD tonight after the US close, Arm and Qualcomm Wednesday 4 November, CoreWeave Tuesday 10 November.

Nvidia
Mixed

Rubin specs quietly lowered, AMD tonight

What happened

Nvidia's website now lists Vera Rubin per-chip memory bandwidth (HBM4) at 19.2 terabytes a second instead of the 22 first announced, and the NVLink chip-to-chip connection at 3 terabytes a second instead of 3.6, while memory size (288 gigabytes) and computing power are unchanged. The cause, possibly slower memory chips, is speculation. Reports say Nvidia is discussing a credit guarantee of more than 5 billion dollars for Nscale data centres (The Information, Bloomberg, Reuters, unsigned). Nvidia fell 1.26 percent to 202.06 dollars on Monday and the chip index (SOX) 3.37 percent (tracker).

Context (what it sits on)

Lower Rubin specs could narrow Nvidia's lead over rivals' chips slightly, and acting as a lender to customers adds risk if the AI build-out slows.

Forward (months–years)

AMD results tonight, call 23:00 Paris. Nvidia third-quarter results Tuesday 17 November. GTC Washington 30 November to 3 December.

Tesla
Mixed

Driverless rides in Fort Worth, Europe sales up

What happened

Tesla opened its robotaxi service in Fort Worth, Texas, on Monday 2 November, its seventh city, with no safety monitor, using Model Y cars over about 143 square miles (Tesla fan news sites). Texas registrations show 573 Cybercabs and 1,244 robotaxis in total, unchanged. October registrations rose strongly: France 2,410 (up 35.2 percent), Spain 1,459 (up 271 percent), Sweden 463 (up 248 percent), Norway 1,548, a seven-market total of 6,742 against 3,985 a year earlier (up 69 percent), though the Netherlands roughly halved (secondary sources). Tesla's sworn answers to US safety regulators (NHTSA) on the Cybercab, request AQ26002, are still not public. Tesla and SpaceX are studying a combination, which has not been agreed.

Context (what it sits on)

After a weak 2025 in Europe, a rebound would ease worries about its car business, while driverless expansion supports the robotaxi story behind its valuation.

Forward (months–years)

China October sales Wednesday 4 November or so (last year 61,497), Germany KBA Wednesday 4 to Thursday 5 November, UK Thursday 5 November, annual meeting Thursday 3 December 22:00 Paris.

France
Fact

Budget revenue vote postponed

What happened

The formal National Assembly vote on the revenue half of the 2027 budget, scheduled for today at 15:00, will not take place because 758 amendments were still pending when debate stopped on Monday night (LCP). Debate continues with an end expected Wednesday evening, and the vote comes only after all amendments. If the text is rejected, the government's original version goes to the Senate.

Context (what it sits on)

France needs a budget that reduces its deficit, and a drawn-out fight keeps pressure on its borrowing costs and on the minority government.

Forward (months–years)

Vote date to be set once amendments are done, possibly late this week.

Israel
Fact

Herzog's consultations start Wednesday

What happened

President Isaac Herzog starts consultations with parties on Wednesday 4 November at 17:00, beginning with the smallest, and ends with Likud on Thursday 5 November before choosing who gets the first chance to form a government (Jerusalem Post, i24). The final count gives Netanyahu's bloc 53 of 120 seats, the opposition 48 and Arab-led parties 19. Herzog is urging compromise to avoid another election.

Context (what it sits on)

No obvious majority means either a unity government or a sixth election in a few years, with Israel's Iran policy hanging on the result.

Forward (months–years)

Ukraine
Fact

Deadly strike after truce ends, talks set

What happened

After the six-day Kherson truce ended, a Russian strike on a school used as an aid centre in Zaporizhzhia killed at least 26 people (DW, Al Jazeera), and strikes cut power in 10 regions. US envoy Witkoff said the US will mediate working-level Ukraine-Russia talks in Abu Dhabi on 10 and 11 November, with US meetings in Brussels this week with France, Germany, the UK, the EU and NATO.

Context (what it sits on)

The talks are the first structured negotiations in months, but the strikes show neither side is easing pressure.

Forward (months–years)

Abu Dhabi talks Tuesday 10 and Wednesday 11 November.
