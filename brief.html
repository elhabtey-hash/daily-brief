Five more tankers hit; Iran strikes toward Jordan; oil ~$99

What happened

US Central Command (CENTCOM) said Tuesday it destroyed five Iranian crude carriers — M/T Kaviz, Charminar, Horizon 1 and Riesco in the Gulf of Oman, plus M/T Derya near Kharg Island — after Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) twice tried to hit a US Navy warship with ballistic missiles. Crews were ordered off before the ships were disabled; no US personnel were harmed, CENTCOM said. This follows Saturday’s strike on three Iranian tankers. Iran retaliated by firing ballistic missiles toward Jordan; Jordan’s armed forces said they engaged 20 missiles, intercepted 18, and that two fell in empty areas with no casualties. Iran claimed heavy damage at a US-used base (Al Azraq / Muwaffaq Salti) — treat that damage claim as unverified against Jordan’s “no casualties” line. Early Wednesday, Brent crude (the global oil benchmark) traded about $99.30–$99.50 a barrel, up more than $1, heading toward $100. US diesel’s AAA national average hit a new nominal record about $5.90 a gallon as of Monday’s print. Iran still says it will announce a Hormuz exclusion zone and an Oman-managed corridor “in coming days”; an International Maritime Organization filing is claimed, not independently confirmed as complete.

Context (what it sits on)

War since 28 February 2026. The Strait of Hormuz normally carries about one-fifth of seaborne oil. Independent trackers still show traffic far below pre-war levels. OPEC+ kept October quotas flat on Sunday. Goldman Sachs researchers have raised the odds of a path where Brent exceeds $120 if shipping attacks keep broadening — that is a bank scenario, not a forecast everyone shares.

Forward (months–years)

Watch whether Jordan becomes a regular IRGC target, whether more Gulf hosts of US forces are threatened, and whether $100 oil sticks into US midterms (3 November) and the Federal Reserve meeting 15–16 September. CPI Friday is the next hard inflation print. Cross-beat: European gas and French nuclear outages.

World
Fact

Kyiv pounded after capital pause ended

What happened

Early Tuesday Russia hit Kyiv with ballistic missiles and jet-powered drones after the three-day capital-only pause around US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner ended. Mayor Vitali Klitschko and other Ukrainian officials reported deaths (wire counts 2–3) and multiple injuries; fires and damage across several districts including residential blocks, a school and a clinic. The weekend Moscow–Kyiv shuttle produced talk of later three-way talks but no written peace deal. Fighting outside the capitals never stopped during the pause.

Context (what it sits on)

Russia’s full-scale invasion began February 2022. US diplomatic bandwidth is split with the Iran war. Germany still plans to close Russia’s Bonn consulate by 18 September.

Forward (months–years)

A pause that ends in a capital barrage is a winter-attrition signal, not a ceasefire path. Watch interceptor-missile appeals to allies and whether any trilateral date is actually set.

Forward (months–years)

Science & tech
Fact

Chimeric mRNAs still the biology lead

What happened

No larger overnight science overturn. The Nature paper (2 September, Harvard Medical School team led by Ruaidhrí Jackson) remains the week’s biology lead: genes can combine into chimeric messenger RNAs that make working proteins, with more than 30,000 examples catalogued and immune-trade-off evidence in mice. The LUX-ZEPLIN dark-matter single-event result (~2.6σ global) is still not a discovery claim.

Context (what it sits on)

Long-read RNA sequencing opened the catalogue. This is a research map for future drugs, not a medicine on shelves.

Forward (months–years)

Cancer, inflammation and neurodegeneration labs will test which fusions matter in people over months and years. EU AI Act chatbot transparency (live since 2 August; further marking backstop 2 December) remains the tech-compliance clock.
