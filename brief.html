AI companies
Fact

Anthropic's IPO filing and Meta's giant share sale

What happened

Anthropic filed its S-1 registration with the US Securities and Exchange Commission on Monday 26 October for an initial public offering (IPO). It shows revenue of 16.29 billion dollars in the six months to 30 June 2026, against 1.16 billion a year earlier, and 4.59 billion for all of 2025 (SEC filing, WSJ). Meta priced 25 billion dollars of Class A shares at 625 dollars, upsized from 22.5 billion and sold through forward agreements settling by 19 November 2027, plus 12.5 billion dollars of 6.50 percent mandatory convertible preferred shares (Meta release). It also plans an at-the-market programme of about 60 billion dollars. Nvidia said this morning it will invest 3 billion dollars in Meta's offering, and Meta intends to expand its use of Nvidia Vera Rubin chips, Vera processors and networking (Nvidia release).

Context (what it sits on)

Anthropic's numbers are the first audited look at how fast an AI model maker is growing, and they eased last week's fear that AI spending cannot be paid for. Meta's raise is one of the largest equity sales ever and shows even the richest tech firms now need outside money for data centres. Nvidia putting cash into a customer that then buys its chips repeats a pattern seen with OpenAI and xAI, which critics call circular.

Forward (months–years)

Microsoft and Alphabet results Wednesday 28 October, Amazon Thursday 29 October, Anthropic IPO pricing date not set.

Global markets
Fact

Wall Street rebounds on the AI trade

What happened

On Monday 26 October the S&P 500 rose 0.70 percent to 7,676.77, the Nasdaq Composite 0.82 percent to 26,169.38 and the Dow 0.28 percent to 52,361.58 (Reuters, CNBC). The Philadelphia chip index rose 2.8 percent but is still down about 12 percent this month, and Nvidia closed 2.4 percent higher at 216.00 dollars. The US 10-year yield was near 4.99 percent. European shares also rose, with the CAC 40 up 0.83 percent.

Context (what it sits on)

After two losing weeks the market is betting AI demand is real, but this week's results from Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon and Meta's financing will test that. A 10-year yield near 5 percent keeps borrowing costs high for data-centre builders.

Forward (months–years)

Fed Wednesday 28 October, big tech results Wednesday and Thursday.

Brazil
Mixed

Markets slump as Bolsonaro backs off annulment

What happened

Brazil's Bovespa index was down about 13.9 percent in late trade on Monday 26 October after President Lula's re-election (PrimerIQ market data, claim pending official close). Flavio Bolsonaro first said he would seek to annul the runoff, then dropped that plan and is instead filing complaints of abuse of power against Lula's campaign (CNN Brasil). His Liberal Party said it recognises the result (Reuters). Trump said the vote looked like another rigged election, without evidence. Fewer than 35,000 votes were cast in the two extra hours of voting (Insper analysis via Reuters).

Context (what it sits on)

Investors fear a looser budget in Lula's fourth term, so the real and Brazilian bonds are under pressure. Bolsonaro's retreat lowers the risk of a contested transfer of power, but Trump's words may sour US relations and tariff talks.

Forward (months–years)

Lula's economic team choices in coming weeks, inauguration 1 January 2027.

Iran and the Gulf
Mixed

Tehran talks with Saudi pact states as Houthi fire continues

What happened

Saudi Arabia's defence minister Prince Khalid bin Salman, Pakistan's army chief Asim Munir and Turkey's intelligence chief Ibrahim Kalin met President Pezeshkian in Tehran on Monday, the first high-level contact since the war spread to Saudi Arabia, and agreed to continue talks (Al Jazeera, Reuters). Overnight the Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted three Houthi ballistic missiles aimed at the Eastern Province and four drones, with no casualties (Arab News). Iran's armed forces chief said the Strait of Hormuz is completely closed to vessels that do not comply with Iranian rules (Tasnim via Gulf News, claim).

Context (what it sits on)

The Tehran meeting opens a regional channel alongside the Pakistan and Qatar mediation with Washington. Hormuz remains restricted, and Houthi strikes on the East-West pipeline threaten Saudi Arabia's route around it, so the oil supply risk stays high. Reports of US and Israeli strike plans after the midterms remain unconfirmed.

Forward (months–years)

US midterms Tuesday 3 November, Israeli election result.

Egypt
Fact

Parliament authorises military support for Saudi Arabia

What happened

Egypt's House of Representatives on Monday 26 October authorised President Sisi to take necessary measures to support Saudi Arabia in defending its territory, sovereignty and holy sites, by a two-thirds majority in a closed session (parliament statement via Reuters, AP). No tally was released. The Social Democratic Party's 11 to 12 members and four other opposition deputies abstained, calling it a blank cheque (Mada Masr, AFP). Sisi says support would be defensive and not include attacks on Yemen.

Context (what it sits on)

The Arab world's largest army is now cleared to join Saudi defence, which raises the stakes for the Houthis and for Red Sea shipping through the Suez Canal, a key source of Egypt's foreign currency. The size, form and timing of any deployment are unknown.

Forward (months–years)

Any Saudi request and Egyptian deployment.

Energy
Fact

Oil eases as regional talks start

What happened

December Brent settled at 115.81 dollars on Monday 26 October, down 0.91 from 116.72, and December WTI at 101.76 dollars, down 1.51 from 103.27 (exchange settles via eOption, Reuters). Some data feeds showed December Brent near 110 dollars late in the day, which is not a settlement price. Gold rose to about 4,038 dollars an ounce.

December WTI is the front month. December Brent expires on Friday 30 October, after which January becomes the Brent benchmark. The gap between Brent and WTI, about 14 dollars, shows the risk is in Gulf seaborne oil. Prices fell on diplomacy, not on any reopening of supply.

Forward (months–years)

December Brent expiry Friday 30 October, OPEC+ decisions early November.

Israel
Fact

Election day, result pending

What happened

Israel votes today, Tuesday 27 October, with about 7.33 million eligible voters at some 12,000 polling stations (Central Elections Committee via press). Final polls showed neither Netanyahu's bloc nor the opposition led by former army chief Gadi Eisenkot reaching the 61 seats needed. SpaceX
Mixed

Filing shows chip lease committed, not closed

What happened

An 8-K filed on 26 October shows a binding commitment letter, dated 24 October, for a master finance lease of about 40 billion dollars with an Apollo-managed lessor that will buy Nvidia Vera Rubin chip systems and lease them to SpaceX over about five years (SEC filing). Closing is expected before the end of 2026, with leases starting in the first half of 2027 as chips arrive. Musk's statement that the deal had closed is a claim the filing does not support. Goldman Sachs structured it, with BlackRock and KKR-advised accounts among anchors. Starlink 15-16 was set for 09:25 Paris today and was still awaiting liftoff at filing, and the Falcon count for 2026 stays at 120.

Context (what it sits on)

This is the largest GPU financing yet and moves a big AI cost off SpaceX's balance sheet. A board committee is still studying a possible combination with Tesla, and nothing is agreed.

Forward (months–years)

Starlink 15-16 Tuesday 27 October 09:25 Paris (pending), FCC meeting on satellite-to-phone spectrum Thursday 29 October, USSF-432 Monday 2 November, Starship Flight 15 about Sunday 8 November (press only).

Nvidia
Fact

3 billion dollar Meta stake and a chip rebound

What happened

Nvidia said on 27 October it will invest 3 billion dollars in Meta's equity offering and that Meta intends to expand deployment of Vera Rubin, Vera processors, Spectrum-X networking and BlueField-4 (Nvidia release). Shares closed Monday up 2.39 percent at 216.00 dollars. Nvidia is not named as financier or guarantor in SpaceX's 40 billion dollar lease, although the chips are Nvidia's.

Context (what it sits on)

The stake is small against Nvidia's cash of about 57 billion dollars, but it extends its habit of funding customers. Memory makers SK hynix and Samsung report this week, a read on supply of the high-bandwidth memory its chips need.

Forward (months–years)

SK hynix Wednesday 28 October, Samsung Thursday 29 October, AMD Tuesday 3 November, Nvidia results about Tuesday 17 November (claim until announced).

Tesla
Mixed

Tenth EU country approves supervised self-driving

What happened

Tesla said Latvia approved Full Self-Driving (Supervised) on 26 October, the tenth EU country after Slovakia, recognising the Dutch approval of April (Reuters, claim from Tesla). Rollout begins soon, with no date. Car-sharing site Turo will flag Teslas with the software and give hosts a free trial (TechCrunch). Barclays upgraded the shares to overweight. There are still no merger terms, and Democrats signalled scrutiny before the midterms.

Context (what it sits on)

Country-by-country approvals build pressure before an EU-wide decision. The EU vehicle committee has never voted on it, and a December vote is only a claim. Answers on the Cybercab to the US road-safety regulator NHTSA are due Friday, which decides whether driverless Dallas rides can start in November as Tesla claims.

Forward (months–years)

NHTSA answers Friday 30 October, China incentives end Saturday 31 October, China October wholesale about 2 to 4 November, annual meeting Thursday 3 December 22:00 Paris.

Ukraine
Mixed

Oleshky ceasefire begins as Kyiv is struck

What happened

A US-brokered local ceasefire took effect today in Russian-occupied Oleshky in the Kherson region, to allow aid deliveries and possible evacuations, and is expected to last at least until Saturday (AP, Al Jazeera). Hours earlier Russian missiles struck Kyiv, killing at least one person and damaging the Southern Bridge and medical sites (Guardian, Euronews).

The truce is narrow but a rare practical agreement before proposed three-way talks around 10 November, which remain a claim. Strikes elsewhere continue.

Forward (months–years)

France
Mixed

Budget debate resumes after social security vote

What happened

The National Assembly resumes the tax-and-revenue part of the 2027 state budget today after a break for the social security budget, whose formal vote was scheduled today (Assembly timetable, franceinfo). Without a majority the government may need the special procedure known as 49.3, which risks a no-confidence vote. The deficit path matters for France's borrowing costs.

Forward (months–years)

Revenue-side vote reported for Tuesday 3 November (claim).

Morocco
Claim

UN vote on Western Sahara approaches

What happened

The UN Security Council is negotiating a US draft on Western Sahara ahead of a vote expected around Friday 30 or Saturday 31 October, when the peacekeeping mission's mandate ends (Hespress). Later drafts reportedly restore self-determination language and call Morocco's autonomy plan the most viable solution rather than the only one (claim). Foreign Minister Bourita spoke with Russia's Lavrov before the vote.

Context (what it sits on)

The wording decides how far the UN tilts towards Morocco's autonomy plan, which the US backs. Russia's possible veto or abstention is the swing factor.

Forward (months–years)

Security Council vote about 30 to 31 October.
