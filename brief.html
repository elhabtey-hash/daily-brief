Fact

Wed settle: Brent Nov expiry $103.53 / WTI Nov $90.42. Thu Asia: Brent Dec $96.98 / WTI Nov $89.16. Diesel Oct expiry $4.9569

What happened

Wednesday 30 September settlements, Dow Jones Market Data, which names the month: Brent for November delivery, the contract that expired that day, rose 94 cents, or 0.92 percent, to 103.53 dollars a barrel. That reconciles to Tuesday's November settlement of 102.59. West Texas Intermediate for November delivery, still the front month, rose 1.04 dollars, or 1.16 percent, to 90.42, which reconciles to Tuesday's 89.38. Ultra-low-sulfur diesel for October delivery, which also expired Wednesday, rose 5.90 cents to 4.9569 dollars a gallon, from Tuesday's 4.8979. Do not read 103.53 as Thursday's Brent price. December is the Brent front month from today, and November diesel is the new diesel front month. No official December Brent settlement for Wednesday was taken from the exchange. In Asian trading, Reuters at 00:45 GMT (02:45 Paris) had Brent futures at 98.15 dollars, up 12 cents, and West Texas Intermediate at 90.35, down 7 cents, without naming the month. The Brent level sits about five dollars under the November expiry, so it is the December contract. A later report, Hindu Business Line at 10:01 India time (about 06:31 Paris), names the months: December Brent 96.98 dollars, down 1.07 percent, and November West Texas Intermediate 89.16 dollars, down 1.39 percent. That West Texas Intermediate print is 1.39 percent under Wednesday's 90.42 November settlement. The Energy Information Administration said commercial crude stocks for the week ended 25 September were 427.3 million barrels. Reuters put the rise at 922,000 barrels, against a poll that had looked for a draw of 264,000. Oil and Gas Journal rounded the same release to a rise of 900,000 barrels, 2 percent above the five-year average, and said distillate stocks fell 2.3 million barrels and stand 14 percent below that average.

Context (what it sits on)

The United States–Iran war since 28 February still constrains the Strait of Hormuz. Wednesday's bounce in the expiring November Brent contract came with stalled diplomacy and tight fuel stocks, even as more crude moves out through the Red Sea. Households feel diesel, and October diesel has now expired at 4.9569 dollars a gallon. A headline that compares Thursday's December Brent with Wednesday's November expiry will look like a crash of about six dollars. It is the roll.

Forward (months–years)

OPEC+ meets on Sunday 4 October. Two people told Reuters they expect November quotas to be left unchanged (Speculation). The Brent price quoted that day should be December. Official United States jobs figures are Friday 2 October at 14:30 Paris. The ISM manufacturing survey is today at 16:00 Paris and was not out at filing. Goldman Sachs, in a note Reuters cited, estimates a broader Gulf flow including ships with transponders off at 23.3 million barrels a day over the last week (Claim, not an official tally). Market reports again put September West Asia loadings near 16.3 million barrels a day. That series was already in dispute on Wednesday and was not reconciled here.

Iran
Mixed

Tehran says the formal US reply arrived and was shown to the president. The text is not public

What happened

Iranian government spokesperson Fatemeh Mohajerani told the state news agency IRNA that Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi presented a United States response to President Masoud Pezeshkian at Wednesday's cabinet meeting (Associated Press copy via the Manila Times, Fortune, Reuters copies). Officials did not say what the response contained, or whether it was a rejection. A separate account, not an Iranian statement, says the document reached Araghchi on Tuesday evening in Doha via Qatari mediators. President Trump had already rejected the seven-day plan in public and, on Tuesday, denied a report that he had offered sanctions relief. Those remarks are not a published text of the reply Mohajerani described. No United States document was found.

Context (what it sits on)

Iran's plan would reopen the strait on a seven-day clock if Washington lifts the blockade on Iranian ports, releases frozen funds and waives sanctions on oil sales. Washington has said the nuclear programme has to move first. A public rejection and a paper handed over in Tehran can both be true, which is why the contents matter more than the fact of delivery.

Forward (months–years)

Watch whether either side publishes the text, and whether Qatar or Oman is given a shipping role. A credible reopening path would hit December Brent, now the front month (Speculation). The United States midterm election is Tuesday 3 November.

Iran
Fact

UKMTO issued three late attack warnings for tankers in the Strait of Hormuz

What happened

gCaptain reported on Wednesday that United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations issued warnings 144-26, 145-26 and 146-26. They cover three incidents reported for Monday 29 September and published only on Wednesday. All three were classed as attacks from verified sources: a crude tanker struck on the port side by an unknown projectile, a tanker hit while inbound, and a third tanker struck with even less detail. The centre did not name the ships, did not give damage, casualties or pollution, and listed the time of each incident as still to be confirmed. The warnings do not say who fired. They also do not confirm Iranian claims of a larger number of hits. A separate warning for 28 September, first classed as suspicious activity, involved a vessel struck by a suspected projectile, a fire, and a crew reported safe.

Context (what it sits on)

The public record of attacks in the strait is running late. Shipowners and insurers are judging risk off notices that can appear a day after the shot. That lag sits under Wednesday's price bounce and under Tehran's statement that a reply has arrived.

Forward (months–years)

Watch the next UKMTO notices and whether any of the three ships is later named with a damage report. Do not treat unnamed-ship tallies from Iranian statements as the same count.

Iraq
Fact

CENTCOM: departure from Erbil Air Base completed Wednesday, ending the Iraq counter-Islamic State mission

What happened

United States Central Command said on Wednesday 30 September that it had completed an orderly departure of American forces and equipment from Erbil Air Base, and that this ends the Operation Inherent Resolve mission in Iraq. The task force remains operational and is now headquartered in Jordan. American forces continue the counter-Islamic State mission in Syria with coalition partners and the Syrian government. Admiral Brad Cooper said Iraqi forces, including the Peshmerga, can now manage threats at home, and that forces in the region stay ready to respond. The base had been the hub for most of about 1,500 American and coalition personnel on that mission. The release describes the end of the mission and a shift to a bilateral defence relationship. It is not a headcount proving that no American in uniform remains anywhere in Iraq under some other arrangement.

Context (what it sits on)

Wednesday morning's brief had Reuters reporting that the last bases were being left that day, and said the last aircraft was not confirmed. This release is that confirmation for Erbil and for the end of the named mission. Troops first left federal Iraq last October under a 2024 timetable. The war with Iran is a separate campaign and is not ended by this exit.

Forward (months–years)

Watch militia moves, any Islamic State claim in Iraq after the intelligence partnership changes, and how the bilateral relationship is actually staffed. The Jordan headquarters means the Syria fight is the piece that continues.

US economy
Fact

August PCE 3.4% / core 3.0%. Q2 growth revised to 2.2%. October hike odds about 37%. Dow 50,906

What happened

The Bureau of Economic Analysis said personal-consumption-expenditures prices rose 0.3 percent in August and 3.4 percent on the year. Excluding food and energy, prices rose 0.2 percent and 3.0 percent on the year. Personal income rose 0.2 percent. Spending rose 0.9 percent, or 190.8 billion dollars. The saving rate was 4.1 percent. A Dow Jones survey cited by CNBC had looked for 3.7 percent headline and 3.3 percent core on the year. CNBC noted that the bureau changed how it prices software, portfolio-management fees and legal services, so part of the gap versus the forecast may be the method. The same morning, the third estimate of second-quarter gross domestic product put real growth at 2.2 percent annualised, revised up 0.7 point from 1.5 percent, on investment, consumer spending and government. First-quarter growth was revised to 2.5 percent. The bureau said the investment revision included commercial structures, among them data centers. On Wednesday the Dow fell 443.87 points, or 0.86 percent, to 50,906.05. The S&P 500 fell 19.30 points, or 0.25 percent, to 7,651.54. The Nasdaq Composite rose 63.52 points, or 0.24 percent, to 26,861.06 (Reuters). A London Stock Exchange close note put the 10-year Treasury yield at about 5.298 percent, up roughly four basis points, and the 30-year at about 5.642 percent. Reuters said CME FedWatch put the chance of at least a quarter-point rise in October at about 37 percent, from about 51 percent a day earlier. Intraday reads differed, and this was not checked on the tool itself. Tokyo's Nikkei 225 morning close on Thursday, not the full close, was 68,355.81, up 1,602.09, with 77 members up and 146 down (Fisco via Zaikei). Mainland Chinese markets are shut through Wednesday 7 October for the national holiday. Hong Kong is shut today.

Context (what it sits on)

The Federal Reserve, under Chair Kevin Warsh, raised rates on 16 September to 3.75–4.00 percent because the war lifted energy costs. August inflation is still well above the 2 percent target, but it came in under the forecast, which is why hike odds fell even as long-term yields stayed near highs last seen in 2007. Stronger spring growth, including data-center building, is the other half of that argument.

Forward (months–years)

Today at 14:30 Paris: weekly jobless claims, not out at filing. Today at 16:00 Paris: the ISM manufacturing survey, not out at filing. Friday 2 October at 14:30 Paris: the September jobs report, the last payrolls before the October meeting. No new government-funding deadline turned up. Ethiopia
Mixed

BBC: medic says 52 civilians killed around Alamata. MSF: more than 700 wounded in Tigray hospitals

What happened

The BBC reported, on Wednesday afternoon and evening Paris time, that a doctor at Alamata General Hospital, who asked not to be named, said at least 52 civilians were killed in fighting around the Tigray town between Saturday and Monday, including a strike the medic put at 12 dead in the marketplace. The BBC cannot report from the region and says communications are cut, so this toll is one unnamed medical source (Claim inside this row). Médecins Sans Frontières said more than 700 wounded people had been admitted to hospitals it supports in Tigray. The BBC's reading of clips and local accounts is that federal troops have taken Alamata and mean to push toward Mekelle. Banking and internet in Tigray are disrupted. The government and the Tigray People's Liberation Front disagree on who fired first. In Amhara, tourists have been stuck in Lalibela since the airport closed to commercial flights. A British tourist told the BBC that 14 Israelis were lifted out by military helicopter on Monday and that the airport might reopen on Thursday. The government accuses Eritrea of backing the armed groups. Eritrea denies it.

Context (what it sits on)

Fighting restarted in late September and broke the 2022 ceasefire that ended a two-year war. Federal control of Alamata was already the claim on Wednesday morning. The casualty figures, the hospital load and the stranded tourists are the new account. The Djibouti corridor, Ethiopia's sea access, runs through Afar, where Tigrayan fighters are also reported to be engaged.

Forward (months–years)

Watch whether Lalibela's airport actually reopens, any named Eritrean movement, and whether a second source confirms the Alamata death toll. A wider war that closes the Djibouti road would sit next to the Hormuz shock as a trade-route risk (Speculation until a corridor closure is confirmed).

Ukraine
Fact

Night of 30 Sep–1 Oct: 107 drones, 63 jet-powered. 87 destroyed or jammed by 08:00. Hits at 11 places

What happened

Ukraine's air force said Russia launched 107 drones on the night of 30 September to 1 October, a mix of Shahed attack drones of which 63 were jet-powered, Gerbera drones and Parodiya decoys (Ukrainska Pravda, 08:11, citing the air force). By 08:00, air defence had destroyed or jammed 87. Hits were recorded at 11 locations and debris at four. Aircraft, missiles, electronic warfare, drone units and mobile groups took part. Several Russian drones were still airborne, so the count is not final. This is a new night. It is not Wednesday morning's raid of 188 drones.

Context (what it sits on)

Russia has been hitting economic and communications targets as the heating season starts. Jet-powered drones are harder to stop than the propeller models. Wednesday's raid is a separate set of figures and should not be added to this one.

Forward (months–years)

Watch the final interception and casualty count later today, and whether power and telecom sites were among the 11 hit locations. European launchers that can reach jet drones remain the equipment question, not a new pledge this morning.

France
Fact

2027 budget is on today's cabinet agenda. Senate presidency vote at 15:00, Larcher still the only declared candidate

What happened

Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu's 2027 budget, set out on 17 September, is before the Council of Ministers today, Thursday 1 October. The plan aims at 54 billion euros of savings to bring the deficit to 5 percent of gross domestic product. It relies on spending restraint, including a freeze of the civil-service pay index, rather than a broad tax rise. No wire available at filing reported that the cabinet had adopted a rewritten text, so the figures above are the plan going in, not a new communiqué. The same afternoon, at 15:00, the Senate elects its president by secret ballot. Gérard Larcher, in the post since 2014, is the only declared candidate. That vote had not been held at filing. Group lists are due by Monday 5 October. The right and centre kept their majority on Sunday. The National Rally has the numbers, with allies, for a first Senate group once the lists are filed.

Context (what it sits on)

Lecornu leads a minority government seven months before the April 2027 presidential election. Tuesday's strike (Interior Ministry 206,000, largest union 300,000) did not, overnight, force a published rewrite. The Senate's right-centre majority is a necessary partner on the budget.

Forward (months–years)

Watch the text that leaves the cabinet, the 15:00 presidency result, and group registrations by Monday 5 October. National Assembly examination is still scheduled from Tuesday 13 October. The government may again need special procedures after that.

Morocco
Fact

First coalition round held. No partner has signed. Talks resume today, including with the Islamist party

What happened

A Moroccan political dispatch reported on Wednesday that Fatima Zahra El Mansouri, named head of government earlier in the week, received the Popular Movement, the Socialist Union of Popular Forces and the Party of Progress and Socialism in Rabat, after earlier meetings with the National Rally of Independents and the Istiqlal party. No one announced a coalition. Istiqlal's Nizar Baraka said the party's national council will use Saturday's session to decide whether to join. The Popular Movement's Mohamed Ouzzine left the door open. The socialist leader Driss Lachgar would not describe the substance. The Party of Progress and Socialism's Nabil Benabdallah said his party's choice depends on whom El Mansouri allies with. The paper's seat counts: Authenticity and Modernity 97, National Rally of Independents 66, Istiqlal 65, Justice and Development 54, Popular Movement 29, Socialist Union 26, Party of Progress and Socialism 19, in a house of 395. A majority is 198. Talks resume Thursday at party headquarters with the Constitutional Union, then the Democratic and Social Movement, then the Justice and Development Party. Abdelilah Benkirane still says that Islamist party will stay in opposition, but he agreed to the meeting after a call from El Mansouri on Tuesday evening.

Context (what it sits on)

The king names the head of government from the party that came first. The appointment answered Tuesday's open question. The government is not formed until partners sign and parliament gives confidence. Most power remains with the palace. The 2030 football tournament build sits under whatever coalition emerges.

Forward (months–years)

Watch who actually signs, whether Benkirane holds the opposition line after today's meeting, and the first programme taken to parliament. Adding the two next-largest parties on paper would clear 198 seats. That arithmetic is not an agreement.

Fact

FTC confirms an investigation of OpenAI, Anthropic and other AI firms over product dangers

What happened

CNBC reported on Wednesday, first published at 11:27 a.m. US Eastern time (17:27 Paris), that a Federal Trade Commission spokesperson confirmed the agency has opened an investigation into OpenAI, Anthropic and other artificial-intelligence companies over dangers their products may pose. The spokesperson would not name the other companies. The New York Post was first with the probe. Representatives for OpenAI and Anthropic had not answered CNBC when the story was published. The same piece says President Trump gathered executives on Tuesday, including those two firms, and that the group signed a short voluntary, nonbinding accord saying each company is responsible for developing its technology safely. The text of that accord was not opened here. This investigation is new relative to Wednesday morning's brief, which covered OpenAI's cheaper Sol model and a reported Anthropic listing document.

Context (what it sits on)

Regulators are now looking at the labs at the same time as the labs are shipping faster models and, in Anthropic's case, preparing a possible listing. A spokesperson confirming a probe is not a finding, a fine or a list of charges. The voluntary Tuesday accord does not bind anyone.

Forward (months–years)

Watch whether formal demands are issued, whether further company names surface, and whether the probe slows the model releases or the listing timetables. The first United States–China artificial-intelligence dialogue exchange is still described as due in November. That date was not re-confirmed this morning.
