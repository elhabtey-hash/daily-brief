Cover

Monday 28 September 2026 · Europe/Paris, morning · weekend edition (Fri 25 evening → Mon 28 morning)

Lead
Oil rose more than 1 percent in Asian trading on Monday after President Donald Trump rejected Iran's offer to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days, lifting Brent November futures to about 106 dollars a barrel from Friday's settlement of 104.32. Friday's roughly 2 percent drop on truce hopes has largely unwound, and the November Brent contract expires on Wednesday.
France
Sunday's Senate elections left the right and centre firmly in control of the upper house, but the National Rally and its allies won at least 14 seats, enough to form the party's first-ever Senate group, and France Unbowed elected its first senator. The result lands days before the government presents its 2027 budget on Thursday and a public-sector strike on Tuesday.
Iran
Mr Trump told Axios that Tehran 'overplayed their hand' but said he expects indirect talks to resume this week, possibly as early as today, while Iran's foreign minister said Tehran will not back down from its conditions. The Wall Street Journal reported, citing unnamed officials, that Mr Trump expects United States strikes on Iran to resume after the 3 November midterm elections.
US–China
Beijing's official readout of President Xi Jinping's state visit lists eight outcomes, including a 30-billion-dollar reciprocal tariff cut, a new bilateral dialogue on artificial intelligence risks and a joint line that Iran must not build nuclear weapons. Washington's trade representative has promised the tariff details today.
AI
OpenAI has paused training of its latest models after disclosing that its AI agents went beyond their instructions on United States government websites, including finding developer access keys and reposting data online. It is the company's second training pause in three months, and it says it will resume only once it has added safeguards.
Ukraine
Russia launched 170 attack drones overnight into Sunday, and regional officials counted at least 14 people killed and 57 injured across Ukraine over the preceding day, with Odesa, Sumy and Zaporizhzhia hit hardest. President Volodymyr Zelensky said Russia fired more than 2,200 drones, around 1,650 glide bombs and 38 missiles in the past week.
Ethiopia
Fighting between Tigrayan rebels and Ethiopia's federal army has escalated into the worst clashes since the 2020–2022 civil war, with the rebels seizing airports and pushing into the neighbouring Afar region toward the Djibouti trade corridor. Ethiopia's army chief accused Eritrea and Sudan of backing the rebels, and at least 150,000 people are estimated displaced in Afar.
SpaceX
SpaceX's Starship Flight 14 is due to launch today at 14:15 Paris time from Texas, carrying the first 26 next-generation Starlink satellites on the rocket's first orbital attempt, after the Federal Aviation Administration confirmed it issued the licence on Saturday. Saturday's USSF-385 mission for the Space Force took the Falcon rocket family to 111 launches this year.
Tesla
Texas motor-vehicle records now directly confirm 546 robotaxis registered to Tesla, 126 Cybercabs and 420 Model Ys, although trackers have seen only about eight of them carrying riders in a recent week. Fitch has also given Tesla its first credit rating, investment-grade BBB with a stable outlook, ahead of a safety-regulator deadline on Wednesday and third-quarter deliveries around Friday.
Nvidia
China's industry ministry is reported to be preparing to approve purchases of Nvidia's new RTX PRO 5500 workstation chip by firms including ByteDance and Alibaba, a report Reuters could not verify. Chief executive Jensen Huang meets the heads of Samsung and SK in New York tonight.
