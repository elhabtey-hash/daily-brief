Friday 23 October 2026 · Europe/Paris, morning · window Thursday 22 morning to Friday 23 morning

AI chip sell-off (lead)
Chip stocks had their worst day in months on Thursday, with the Philadelphia semiconductor index down 6.72 percent and the Nasdaq down 2.60 percent, its biggest fall since 5 June. Investors are questioning how the huge AI building programme will be paid for after Meta's 7 percent fall and a remark by Nvidia's chief about missing working capital.
Iran and the Gulf
The New York Times reported that US and Israeli forces have drawn up plans for renewed strikes on Iran after the US midterm elections, and President Trump said something will happen fairly soon. Washington ordered non-emergency diplomats and their families to leave Saudi Arabia as Houthi attacks on Saudi airports and oil sites escalated.
Energy
December Brent jumped 4 percent to settle at 113.17 dollars, its highest close since early May, and December WTI rose to 99.47 dollars. The rise reflects fear that the Gulf war is widening to Saudi oil infrastructure.
Asia markets
The AI sell-off spread to Asia, where the Kospi fell 3.52 percent and the Nikkei 2.65 percent on Thursday, and both fell again this morning. Strong Japanese inflation data added pressure in Tokyo.
Nvidia
Nvidia shares fell 4.40 percent to 212.21 dollars, about 11 percent below their 6 October close. The company said a Kazakh firm has a US export licence for 45,000 Grace Blackwell chips, and its 64 gigabyte DGX Spark desktop computer goes on sale today from 4,999 dollars.
SpaceX
SpaceX shares fell about 9 percent on the first big lockup day, and its quarterly filing confirms a board committee is studying a combination with Tesla with no agreement reached. Another 328.4 million shares are freed on Saturday, and the Starlink 15-15 launch is now targeted for 23:30 Paris tonight after a third scrub.
Tesla
Tesla shares fell about 2 percent as investors focused on lower capital spending of about 20 billion dollars this year. Its claim of a Dallas Cybercab launch next month depends on answers due to the US road-safety regulator on 30 October.
Brazil
Sunday's presidential runoff is a dead heat, with Datafolha's final poll giving 50 percent of valid votes each to Flavio Bolsonaro and President Lula. The result will set Brazil's economic course and its relations with Washington.
Ukraine
Russia fired 21 ballistic missiles at Kyiv hours after agreeing in principle to new three-way talks, killing at least four people. Zelensky said the strikes show no change in Moscow's position.
