Cover

Friday 20 November 2026 · Europe/Paris, morning · window Thursday 19 November morning to Friday 20 November morning

Markets (lead)
Asian stock markets crashed on Friday as the sell-off in artificial intelligence shares spread, with Taiwan's main index down 6.2 percent and both Japan's Nikkei 225 and South Korea's Kospi down more than 5 percent. European shares are expected to open lower after Wall Street's worst day in months.
US markets
The Dow Jones index lost 1,101 points on Thursday and closed below 50,000 for the first time since June, while the S&P 500 fell 2 percent for a sixth straight loss. Walmart's cautious holiday outlook and a sharp drop in a Philadelphia factory survey added to the gloom.
AI companies
Anthropic's shares opened 34 percent above their 83 dollar offer price on their first day of trading, then gave back half the gain to close at 97.17 dollars, up 17 percent. The sale formally closes today.
Nvidia
Nvidia fell 3.3 percent to 145.98 dollars on Thursday, a new 52-week low, as a new US rule requiring export licences for advanced chips to five more countries took effect.
Energy
Oil ended Thursday almost unchanged, with January Brent settling at 91.98 dollars, as Iranian claims that two supertankers hit mines in the Strait of Hormuz offset signs of ample supply. December West Texas Intermediate expires today.
Iran and the Gulf
Iran's Revolutionary Guards said two supertankers loaded with more than 4 million barrels of crude hit naval mines and caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz, a claim not independently confirmed. Saudi authorities said a Houthi missile attack hit a tower in Riyadh.
Shenzhen summit
Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin signed a short peace declaration after the first-ever three-way meeting of their countries' leaders, saying a nuclear war must never be fought. It set out no arms-control talks and no Ukraine plan.
Ukraine and Russia
At a Berlin meeting of Ukraine's allies, US envoy Jared Kushner said he believes Russia is comfortable with NATO-like security guarantees for postwar Ukraine. President Volodymyr Zelensky questioned whether both sides mean the same thing.
Myanmar
The International Court of Justice ruled by nine votes to six that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya in 2017 and ordered it to prosecute those responsible and pay compensation. The ruling is final, but the court cannot enforce it.
India
Cyclone Akvan began crossing the Odisha coast on Friday morning after weakening, and officials report no major damage so far. More than 300,000 people were evacuated.
COP31
The UN climate summit in Antalya reaches its scheduled last day with a draft deal that drops all mention of fossil fuels and a 120 billion dollar adaptation finance target. Talks may run into the weekend.
Europe
The European Commission confirmed it was hit last month by a cyberattack that used a publicly available AI tool, with some systems still offline. EU lawmakers and governments also agreed to ease methane rules on gas imports to protect supply.
Ethiopia and Eritrea
Drone strikes and heavy artillery were reported overnight around the border town of Badme as both armies build up forces, raising fears of a full-scale war.
France
The Senate starts debating the 2027 budget today after the National Assembly rejected it, and the government is keeping open the option of forcing it through without a vote.
AI
A study reported in Nature finds that many bad habits of AI chatbots, including invented academic references, fade within about 16 months of a model generation's release.
Tesla
Tesla opened public rides in its steering-wheel-free Cybercab in Dallas, the second city after Austin, but gave no fleet size or fares. Elon Musk also made his first visit to the Shanghai factory since 2023.
SpaceX
Anthropic's final prospectus confirms its computing contract with SpaceX's AI unit, worth up to 120.5 billion dollars, unchanged. Mexico's environment agency and SpaceX dispute whether debris from the last Starship flight reached a Mexican beach.
Palantir
Senators Thom Tillis and Ron Wyden said they will place holds on Palantir security chief Michael LoSapio's nomination to run the US cyber defence agency after he would not say who won the 2020 election.
