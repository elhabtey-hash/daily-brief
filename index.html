Thursday 8 October 2026 · Europe/Paris, morning · window Wednesday 7 morning to Thursday 8 morning

Ukraine (lead)
Russia's missile and drone barrage on Wednesday killed at least 32 people, according to Reuters, including 22 in Pryluky in the Chernihiv region, where a missile destroyed a five-storey apartment block, and four in Kyiv. President Volodymyr Zelensky called it one of Russia's most vile strikes, and it also hit industrial sites and energy infrastructure ahead of winter.
Iran
The Atlantic and NBC News report that the White House has asked the Pentagon for strike options against Iran that could be used before the US midterm elections on Tuesday 3 November, although no decision has been made. A tanker was also hit by several projectiles off the northern tip of Qatar, with casualties, the first reported attack deep inside the Gulf in about a month.
Yemen
Saudi Arabia says Houthi attacks on Riyadh's King Khalid airport and on Abha airport killed three people and wounded 36, and the Saudi-led coalition says it struck more than 80 Houthi sites in reply. Reuters reports that the Yemeni government's claim to have reached Mocha was premature, with the fighting now centred on the Dhubab district above the Bab el-Mandeb Strait.
Energy
Brent crude for December settled on Wednesday at 100.20 dollars a barrel, down 38 cents, and West Texas Intermediate for November fell 1.16 dollars to 88.28 dollars after the International Energy Agency agreed to speed up its release of emergency stocks. Both rose more than 1 percent in Asia this morning on the Iran strike reports and Hurricane Isaias, which has already shut in about a quarter of US Gulf of Mexico oil output.
Nobel Prize
The 2026 Nobel Prize in Chemistry went to Henri Kagan of France and Kenso Soai of Japan for showing how chemical reactions can amplify one mirror-image form of a molecule, which helps explain why life uses only one form and how drug makers build the right one. The Literature prize is announced at 13:00 Paris today, after this filing, with Peace on Friday and Economics on Monday.
Federal Reserve
Minutes of the Federal Reserve's September meeting show that most officials think another interest-rate rise will likely be needed by the end of the year, with several saying rates are barely restraining an economy pushed by energy prices and AI investment. The ten-year Treasury yield touched 5.365 percent on Wednesday, its highest since 2002, and the S&P 500 slipped 0.22 percent from its record.
Ethiopia
Witnesses told Reuters that dozens of Eritrean soldiers drove through Adigrat, 25 kilometres inside Ethiopia's Tigray region, on Wednesday, the first reported sighting of Eritrean troops in Ethiopia since the war resumed on 23 September. Eritrea did not deny it but accused Ethiopia of inventing a pretext for a war of aggression, days after the two countries cut diplomatic ties.
AI
OpenAI began rolling out GPT-6 to ChatGPT's paying users on Wednesday and extends it to free users from today, adding what it calls Intelligent UI, which lets answers come back as interactive charts, maps and small tools rather than plain text. OpenAI says more than 1.2 billion people use ChatGPT each week.
Tesla
A Reuters special report says Tesla pressed European regulators to soften safety reviews of its supervised Full Self-Driving software and mobilised fans on X, and seven independent researchers told Reuters that Tesla's own studies do not show the system prevents fatal crashes. An EU-wide decision is expected in December, and Tesla reports third-quarter results on Wednesday 21 October.
Nvidia
Nvidia and Microsoft launched the first Windows laptops built on Nvidia's RTX Spark chips, led by the Surface Laptop Ultra, with pre-orders open and shipping on Friday 16 October. Its chipmaker TSMC reported September sales up 54.6 percent on a year earlier, which by our own arithmetic puts its third quarter above guidance ahead of results on Thursday 15 October.
