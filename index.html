Cover

Monday 26 October 2026 · Europe/Paris, morning · window Friday 23 morning to Monday 26 morning, including the weekend

Brazil (lead)
President Lula won Sunday's runoff with 51.79 percent of valid votes against 48.21 percent for Flavio Bolsonaro, the first comeback between rounds in Brazilian history. Bolsonaro has not conceded, so the coming days test how smoothly Brazil accepts the result.
Iran and the Gulf
Iran says messages to Washington still pass through Pakistan and Qatar, and its envoy offered to reopen the Strait of Hormuz if the US lifts its naval blockade, which a hardline Iranian outlet denied. The Houthis fired at least 12 missiles at Saudi Arabia over the weekend and say they hit oil pumping stations, which Riyadh has not confirmed.
Energy
December Brent settled at 116.72 dollars on Friday, its highest close since late April, and December WTI at 103.27 dollars, a weekly gain of about 6 percent for Brent. Prices eased this morning as Qatari mediators visited Tehran.
Global markets
Wall Street fell on Friday as oil rose, with the Dow down 1.23 percent while chip stocks steadied after Thursday's rout. This week brings the Fed, the European Central Bank and the Bank of Japan, plus results from Microsoft, Alphabet and Amazon.
Asia markets
Seoul and Tokyo fell again on Monday, with the Kospi closing down 1.46 percent and the Nikkei down 0.80 percent. Friday's closes were better than the intraday figures in Friday's brief, and this brief corrects them.
SpaceX
Elon Musk says SpaceX has closed 40 billion dollars of debt to buy AI chips arriving in the first half of 2027, a claim with no company filing yet. Another 328 million previously locked-up shares can be traded from today, and Starlink 15-15 did launch early on Saturday Paris time.
Nvidia
Nvidia ended the week at 210.96 dollars, and its chief Jensen Huang said its agent software costs customers about 2,000 dollars per chip per year. This week's results from SK hynix, Samsung and the big US cloud companies will test demand for its chips.
Israel
Israel votes on Tuesday, and the final polls show neither Prime Minister Netanyahu's bloc nor Gadi Eisenkot's opposition reaching the 61 seats needed for a majority. A long coalition fight is likely while the Iran war continues.
Egypt
Egypt's parliament votes today on whether to authorise military support for Saudi Arabia against the Houthis, which needs a two-thirds majority. A yes vote would draw the Arab world's largest army into the Yemen war.
