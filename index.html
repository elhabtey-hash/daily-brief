Cover

Monday 9 November 2026 · Europe/Paris, morning · window Friday 6 November morning to Monday 9 November morning

Nvidia (lead)
Nvidia’s board has opened an independent investigation into reports that chief executive Jensen Huang was targeted by a foreign intelligence effort, and Huang has apologised to staff in a personal message. The board says it has no reason to believe national security or company secrets were compromised, and the shares were indicated about 3.4 percent lower outside regular trading.
Japan, Korea and Taiwan
Taiwan’s main index closed up 4.94 percent and Tokyo’s Nikkei up 2.33 percent on Monday as chip stocks recovered from last week’s sell-off. Seoul’s Kospi closed down 0.81 percent after a volatile session, with analysts citing the Nvidia investigation.
US economy
The US added 165,000 jobs in October, far above forecasts, and unemployment fell to 4.1 percent. Traders raised the odds of a second Federal Reserve rate rise in December to about 80 percent, with October consumer prices due on Tuesday.
Energy
January Brent settled almost unchanged at 98.32 dollars a barrel on Friday after its steepest weekly fall in years. December West Texas Intermediate settled at 90.83 dollars as traders weighed US-Iran talks against continuing attacks on shipping.
Iran and the Gulf
President Trump said new strikes on Iran were highly unlikely if talks produce a strong agreement, while both sides reported ships hit in or near the Strait of Hormuz. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said he did not see a breakthrough as imminent.
Israel
President Isaac Herzog formally asked Gadi Eisenkot on Sunday to try to form Israel’s next government. Eisenkot has 28 days, extendable by 14, and has invited all parties including Likud to join a national emergency government.
Ukraine and the G7
G7 foreign ministers meet in Paris today, the first test of whether the US midterm result changes Washington’s approach to Ukraine and the Middle East. US-mediated talks between Ukraine and Russia are due in Abu Dhabi from Tuesday.
Poland
A gunman killed six people in Kraków’s main square on Saturday, and prosecutors are treating it as terrorism. Police also found three of his relatives dead at his home, and officials say he held far-right views.
Paraguay
Paraguay’s Senate removed President Santiago Peña from office on Friday over the collapse of the lender Ueno, and Vice President Pedro Alliana was sworn in. Prosecutors ordered Peña held under house arrest, and he has said he will cooperate with the investigation.
China
Chinese factory-gate prices rose 5.7 percent in October from a year earlier, the fastest in about four and a half years, as energy costs feed through. Consumer inflation eased to 0.7 percent, pointing to weak household demand.
France
The 2027 budget is now before the Senate after the National Assembly rejected it, and Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu still lacks a majority. Security concerns after weeks of protests led France to move the G7 meeting from Marseille to Paris.
SpaceX
NASA picked SpaceX to use a Starship to deliver communications satellites towards the Moon in 2028 and named it one of eight companies eligible for lunar delivery work. The next military Falcon 9 launch is targeted for early Tuesday in Paris, and Starship Flight 15 still awaits regulatory approval.
Tesla
Texas now lists 1,282 vehicles registered to Tesla’s robotaxi company, up 38 over the weekend, though registrations are not the same as active rides. China’s October breakdown of domestic and export sales is expected this week.
