Cover

Tuesday 24 November 2026 · Europe/Paris, morning · window Monday 23 November morning to Tuesday 24 November morning

Nvidia (lead)
Nvidia's board unanimously reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive on Sunday, six days after its chair said he would not return, citing input from senior levels of the US government that he poses no national security risk. The shares jumped 31.5 percent on Monday to 165.05 dollars, while lawmakers of both parties attacked the decision and said Huang must still answer to Congress.
Markets
Wall Street had its best day since April 2025 on Monday, with the S&P 500 up 4.7 percent and the Nasdaq up 6.5 percent, as Huang's return revived the AI trade. Asia followed this morning: Taiwan rose 9 percent in its biggest points gain on record, South Korea's Kospi climbed 6.3 percent back above 5,000 and Tokyo's Nikkei gained 3.9 percent.
Fed
New York Fed President John Williams said on Monday that inflation is too high and that there is still work to do, and he did not rule out rate rises at consecutive meetings. Futures now treat a quarter-point rise on Wed 9 Dec as a near certainty, which would be the second rise since September, and October inflation data comes out on Wednesday.
AI companies
The rebound lifted the whole AI supply chain, with Dell up 20 percent and Micron up 19 percent, and Hewlett Packard Enterprise beat forecasts after the close but fell in late trading. Dell reports after the US close tonight, the next test of whether AI server demand is holding up.
AI
Three days after the claimed proof of the Riemann hypothesis with Anthropic's Claude, mathematicians say the machine-checked proof is very likely correct but that no human yet understands it. The rival human-written proof released the same day has already run into possible problems.
Energy
Oil fell to a three-month low on Monday, with January Brent settling at 89.37 dollars a barrel, down 3.5 percent, as a Libyan pipeline reopened and forecasts of a supply glut outweighed tanker attacks near Hormuz. January West Texas Intermediate settled at 80.17 dollars, its lowest since August.
Iran and the Gulf
Iran's Revolutionary Guards navy said three oil tankers struck mines in the Strait of Hormuz on Monday, and Tehran vowed to use all means against the US naval blockade. In Washington, Vice President Vance and Secretary of State Rubio met Qatar's prime minister, the main mediator, as the US added new sanctions on Iranian shadow banking.
NATO eastern flank
Polish prosecutors say all three objects that fell in Poland on Friday were Russian Kh-101 cruise missiles, and NATO is sending an early-warning surveillance aircraft in response. Estonia closes its road border with Russia on Tue 1 Dec, and Moscow says Tallinn is on the verge of triggering open war.
Russia-Ukraine war
A Russian strike on an apartment block in Dymer, north of Kyiv, killed at least 11 people, part of attacks that killed at least 15 across the Kyiv region. President Zelensky confirmed that Ukrainian sea drones sank the Russian corvette Merkury in Libya and warned that Russia is preparing new strikes.
Europe
The European Commission proposed its 22nd package of sanctions on Russia, cutting about 60 more Russian banks off the SWIFT payments system and banning Russian teams from sporting events in the EU. The package needs the agreement of all 27 members, and EU leaders meet on Thu 26 and Fri 27 Nov to discuss a new 45 billion euro loan for Ukraine.
Spain
The last polls before Sunday's general election, published on Monday, still put the People's Party first and show it and the far-right Vox winning a joint majority. The state pollster CIS is the outlier, putting the Socialists well ahead.
France
Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu wrote to MPs on Monday offering a budget compromise on five themes, from farming to housing, provided the deficit target of 5 percent holds. The Senate began debating the budget line by line, and the government will probably have to choose between forcing it through without a vote and an untested budget decree.
Morocco
The Court of Arbitration for Sport rules this afternoon on Senegal's appeal against the decision that awarded Morocco the 2025 Africa Cup of Nations title. Spanish newspaper Marca reports that the appeal has been rejected, but the court says its ruling will be published only on its own website.
US
A federal appeals court on Monday revived President Trump's bid to move his hush money case to federal court, where he could seek to have the conviction thrown out on immunity grounds. Reuters reports that a Senate committee is investigating whether Vice President Vance played a role in a Venezuelan oil contract awarded to the trader Vitol.
Mexico
Mexico will try the captured drug lord Fausto Isidro Meza Flores at home even though the US asked for his extradition, the attorney general said. President Sheinbaum confirmed that Mexico asked for and authorised US government drones to gather intelligence on cartels.
Tesla
Tesla shares rose 11.5 percent on Monday to 385.46 dollars, their biggest gain since April 2025, as the market rebounded and the new self-driving software rolled out to early users. The Financial Times reports that chair Robyn Denholm sits on the special committee weighing a possible merger with SpaceX, nine days before a contested vote on her re-election.
SpaceX
Virginia and Oregon sued SpaceX, which now runs X and xAI, alleging that its Grok AI tool generated sexual images of real people, including children, without their consent while the company promoted its safety. X said such content has no place on its services, and SpaceX has a US military launch scheduled for 16:55 CET today.
Science
Scientists using a robotic submersible have found the remains of an explosive volcanic eruption about 6,150 metres down in the Mariana Trench's Challenger Deep, far deeper than researchers thought such eruptions were possible.
