Cover

Thursday 15 October 2026 · Europe/Paris, morning · window Wednesday 14 morning to Thursday 15 morning

Asia markets (lead)
Asian technology stocks plunged on Thursday, with South Korea's Kospi down about 8 percent in the afternoon after circuit breakers halted trading and Japan's Nikkei 225 closing down more than 4 percent. Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix each lost around 10 percent as hotter US inflation, fears of Federal Reserve rate rises and doubts about financing AI data centres hit chip stocks.
US inflation
US consumer prices rose 0.6 percent in September and 3.7 percent from a year earlier, just above the 3.6 percent forecast, while core prices excluding food and energy rose 2.6 percent against 2.5 percent expected. The S&P 500 fell 1.11 percent to 7,718.94 and the Nasdaq fell 1.88 percent as traders raised bets on a Fed rate rise, with the next decision due on Wednesday 28 October.
Energy
December Brent settled on Wednesday at 106.06 dollars a barrel, up 37 cents from Tuesday's 105.69, and November West Texas Intermediate settled at 95.09 dollars, up 95 cents from 94.14 and its highest close in three weeks. Gulf of Mexico oil shut in after Hurricane Isaias fell to 9.92 percent of output, and the US regulator says production has recovered to nearly 90 percent of normal.
Iran
The Singapore-registered container ship GFS Pride sank in the Strait of Hormuz on Wednesday after being hit by a projectile, and all 21 crew were rescued, while a second Singapore-registered ship was damaged. Iran's Revolutionary Guard says a mine sank it, but a maritime security firm says there is no independent evidence of that.
Saudi Arabia
After a missile hit an Aramco office building in Dhahran, the Houthis vowed more attacks on Saudi airports and oil facilities, and the Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted another missile aimed at Riyadh on Wednesday. The coalition has also struck Houthi fuel sites near Sanaa.
Ukraine
A Russian missile and drone attack overnight into Wednesday damaged 26 power facilities and four oil and gas sites, leaving about 600,000 people without power in the Kyiv and Kharkiv regions, according to President Zelensky, despite Trump's announced energy ceasefire. Russia denies breaking the deal and accuses Ukraine of hitting two refineries, which Kyiv calls fake.
European Union
EU leaders meet in Brussels from 10:00 today and tomorrow, with energy prices, the 2028 to 2034 EU budget, Ukraine and the Middle East on the agenda. No conclusions have been adopted yet, and the draft text reaffirms support for Ukraine and asks the European Commission for targeted, temporary measures on energy costs.
AI companies
TSMC, the world's largest contract chipmaker, reported record third-quarter revenue of 1.49 trillion Taiwan dollars, about 46 billion US dollars and up 50 percent from a year earlier, and net profit of 763.1 billion Taiwan dollars, up 68.7 percent. Its operating margin of 58.7 percent beat the top of its own forecast range, and its fourth-quarter guidance and spending plan were due on its call at 08:00 Paris.
AI financing
CoreWeave's 2032 bonds yielded about 15 percent and its shares fell around 15 percent on Wednesday, deepening worries about how debt-heavy AI infrastructure firms will pay for their buildout as rates rise. Anthropic told investors its third-quarter revenue rose 45 percent to 16.8 billion dollars ahead of a planned stock market listing, according to the BBC.
Nvidia
Nvidia shares fell 3.16 percent to 221.30 dollars on Wednesday and slipped about 1.5 percent more after hours, as hot inflation, a 30-year Treasury yield that touched 5.63 percent and AI-credit worries hit chip stocks. The company also announced a 250 million dollar, five-year compute donation for UK AI safety research, and its GTC Berlin conference starts on Tuesday 20 October.
Japan
Japan's agriculture minister Kazuo Yana resigned on Wednesday over scandals including alleged election-law problems, the first departure from Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi's cabinet. She said his replacement would be named on Thursday.
North Korea
North Korea said it test-fired two types of submarine-launched strategic cruise missiles into the East Sea, but its state media did not say whether they were fired from an actual submarine. The claim has not been independently verified.
Ebola
Congo had confirmed 9,144 cases and 4,410 deaths in its Bundibugyo-strain Ebola outbreak as of 13 October, with 79 new cases reported for 12 October and Ituri province the worst hit. No vaccine is approved for this strain, and a pivotal trial is expected to start in the coming weeks.
Spain and Morocco
King Felipe VI visited the Spanish cities of Ceuta and Melilla on Wednesday and said relations with Morocco must rest on trust and good faith, after July's mass migration crossing into Ceuta. Opposition leader Alberto Núñez Feijóo demanded that Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez explain alleged Moroccan naval incursions into Spanish waters.
Brazil
Quaest's poll put Flávio Bolsonaro on 46 percent and President Lula on 44 percent for the runoff on Sunday 25 October, a statistical tie within its 2-point margin of error. Datafolha's new runoff poll is due today.
Israel
Polls show neither Benjamin Netanyahu's bloc nor Gadi Eisenkot's bloc reaching the 61 seats needed for a majority in the election on Tuesday 27 October. Netanyahu says he is not seeking to attack Iran before the vote and warns that an opposition win would mean a Palestinian state.
France
The National Assembly began debating the 2027 budget and rejected a Socialist motion to halt the debate by 236 votes to 149. The government wants a public deficit of 5 percent backed by about 43 billion euros of cuts and other measures, and a compromise looks further away after a heated start.
Ethiopia
Ethiopian federal forces have reached Zalambessa, a Tigray town near the Eritrean border, and Ethiopia accuses Eritrea of putting troops up to 60 kilometres inside its territory. Eritrea denies this and has appealed to the United Nations, and no Ethiopian troops have been reported crossing into Eritrea.
China
China's consumer prices rose 0.9 percent in September from a year earlier, below forecasts, while factory-gate prices rose 5.0 percent on higher fuel and commodity costs. The yuan strengthened to 6.7249 per dollar.
Tesla
Texas now lists 349 Cybercabs, up 30, among 769 driverless-authorized Tesla vehicles, which counts permits rather than rides, and Tesla will livestream its new Roadster at 02:55 Paris time on Friday 16 October. The US auto safety regulator's school-zone speeding petition on Tesla's robotaxis has a decision deadline of 21 January 2027, according to a trade outlet's calculation.
SpaceX
A Falcon 9 is targeted to launch the USSF-481 mission from California at 01:44 Paris on Friday 16 October, and the CRS-35 cargo Dragon is targeted to reach the International Space Station at about 17:00 Paris today. Ukraine says speed limits on Starshield military terminals have disrupted its forces, and Musk says the US government controls those limits.
Palantir
Palantir's investor page now lists third-quarter results for Monday 2 November, with the company guiding to revenue of 2.160 to 2.164 billion dollars. It also settled its lawsuit with London's policing office over the blocked 50 million pound Met Police contract, which will be re-run and which Palantir may bid for.
