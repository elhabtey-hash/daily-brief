Cover

Tuesday 13 October 2026 · Europe/Paris, morning · window Monday 12 morning to Tuesday 13 morning

Ukraine (lead)
The Kremlin would not confirm Trump's announced energy ceasefire on Monday, saying only that contacts are continuing, while Zelensky said Russia had already violated it and Ukraine would respond if strikes went on. The EU separately adopted its largest-ever sanctions listing, almost 1,600 Russian arms-industry companies and executives.
Iran
Iran's Revolutionary Guard said it set a third supertanker on fire in the Strait of Hormuz in two days, after six merchant ships were reported hit around the strait on Sunday alone. The British maritime security agency rates the threat to shipping as severe.
Energy
December Brent settled on Monday at 106.65 dollars a barrel, up 1.8 percent, and November West Texas Intermediate at 93.74 dollars, up 2.1 percent, as doubts about the energy truce and the Gulf shipping attacks outweighed the Russian diesel deal. Gulf of Mexico oil still shut in after Hurricane Isaias fell to about 30 percent of output on Monday from 57.5 percent on Sunday.
Nobel Prize
The Economics prize went to Robert Hall of Stanford and Michael Woodford of Columbia for the microeconomic foundations of macroeconomic stabilisation, the models central banks use to steer inflation and output. It closes a Nobel week that also honoured Navi Pillay, Anne Carson and the optogenetics pioneers.
Saudi Arabia
The Houthis targeted Riyadh for a third day running on Sunday, and Saudi Arabia is investigating how its air defences failed to stop Saturday's deadly airport strike. Washington is giving the Saudis intelligence and refuelling support but has not joined strikes in Yemen, and the Houthis threaten a painful response if it does.
US markets
The S&P 500 ended Monday flat at 7,807.24 as higher oil and a 3.9 percent drop in Micron weighed on technology shares, while the Dow rose 0.38 percent. JPMorgan opens bank earnings today at 13:00 Paris and September consumer prices follow on Wednesday at 14:30 Paris.
AI
Meta released Muse Wonder, which it calls its most capable model, claiming state-of-the-art results for personal and work agents and coding, and made it available through its developer interface. It is the first big release from Meta's rebuilt AI effort trained at its gigawatt-scale Prometheus data centre.
Apple
Apple's Welcome home event in New York starts today at 15:00 Paris, after this filing. Reports expect a first smart-home hub with a small screen plus new HomePod mini and Apple TV models, but Apple has confirmed only the event.
Tesla
The US road safety regulator has opened a review of a petition alleging Tesla's driverless robotaxis speed through active school zones, and will decide whether to launch a formal investigation. Tesla's September retail sales in China fell 9 percent from a year earlier to 64,837, the fourth monthly decline in a row.
Nvidia
Microsoft said its own Maia chips will become the main system for its internal AI inference at multi-gigawatt scale from next year, a competitive blow as Nvidia shares fell a fifth straight day to 226.32 dollars. Nvidia used the Open Compute summit to open up its Vera Rubin rack designs and unveil faster optics with Cisco.
SpaceX
A Falcon 9 launched 27 Starlink satellites from California early this morning, the 116th Falcon flight of 2026. The CRS-35 cargo mission to the space station targets 12:33 Paris today, after this filing.
