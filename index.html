Cover

Thursday 5 November 2026 · Europe/Paris, morning · window Wednesday 4 November morning to Thursday 5 November morning

Energy (lead)
January Brent plunged 8.7 percent on Wednesday to 94.62 dollars a barrel, and December West Texas Intermediate fell 8.3 percent to 88.12 dollars, their lowest closes since late August. Markets bet that a Democratic Congress will push President Trump toward a deal that reopens the Strait of Hormuz.
Markets
US stocks reversed an overnight futures sell-off and closed sharply higher after the midterm results, with the Dow up 3.03 percent, the S&P 500 up 1.58 percent and the Nasdaq up 1.44 percent. AI chip and data-centre shares were left behind as investors rotated into consumer and healthcare names.
Asia markets
Seoul's Kospi closed down 5.28 percent at 5,383.80 after a trading curb, led by Samsung and SK hynix, while Tokyo's Nikkei fell 1.79 percent to 61,077.90. Broader Asian indexes with less chip exposure rose, including Hong Kong's Hang Seng, up about 2.8 percent.
US midterms
Reuters puts the Democrats' Senate majority at least at 54 to 44, with Alaska and Mississippi still uncalled, and says Democrats gained at least 30 House seats. President Trump accepted the losses and Vice President Vance blamed the Iran war.
AI companies
Arm reported record second-quarter revenue of 1.44 billion dollars, up 27 percent, but guided only in line, while Qualcomm missed on handset chips and guided soft, sending both shares lower after the close. China blocked SoftBank's 5.4 billion dollar purchase of ABB's robotics unit, according to the Financial Times.
Nvidia
Nvidia closed down 3.17 percent at 200.13 dollars on Wednesday, even as the S&P 500 rose, and is now about 16 percent below its early October close. The Financial Times reports Nvidia has told cloud customers to sell at least 51 percent of new-contract capacity to non-Chinese clients.
France
The National Assembly unanimously rejected the revenue half of the 2027 budget on Wednesday night, with 458 votes against and none for, so the whole bill falls at first reading. The government's original text now goes to the Senate.
Bank of England
The Bank of England decides at 13:00 Paris today and is widely expected to raise Bank Rate from 3.75 percent to 4 percent, its first increase in more than three years. The move would answer inflation driven by energy costs from the Iran war.
Iran and the Gulf
Oil's plunge reflects hopes that Democratic pressure and Vance's public blame of the war will force a deal, while Qatar keeps mediating an interim arrangement. No agreement has been reached and attacks on tankers continue.
Tesla
Germany registered 2,932 Teslas in October, nearly four times a year earlier, though BYD overtook Tesla year to date. UK October figures are due around 10:00 Paris today, and the Cybercab Europe tour opens at Auto Zurich.
SpaceX
SpaceX chose Qualcomm's Dragonwing platforms for Starlink Mobile's terrestrial 5G network, with rollout from 2027 and no terms disclosed. Shares fell 3.81 percent to 135.92 dollars on Wednesday, and the Falcon year count is corrected to 121.
Israel
President Herzog is finishing consultations today and is widely expected to give the first mandate to form a government. No bloc has the 61 seats needed for a majority.
Morocco
King Mohammed VI ordered security and development measures for the southern provinces after the UN MINURSO mission's mandate ended on 31 October. Morocco says it will keep observing the ceasefire while the Interior Ministry manages the mission's withdrawal.
