Cover

Friday 16 October 2026 · Europe/Paris, morning · window Thursday 15 morning to Friday 16 morning

Global markets (lead)
Asian stocks rebounded on Friday after Thursday's chip-stock rout, with South Korea's Kospi up about 3 percent at 5,738 and Japan's Nikkei 225 up 1.75 percent to about 65,550, while mainland Chinese shares fell. On Wall Street on Thursday the S&P 500 recovered from an early loss to close up 0.33 percent at 7,744.06 and the Dow gained 1.08 percent to 52,177.69.
US inflation and rates
US producer prices jumped 1.3 percent in September, the largest monthly rise in more than four years, yet investors bought government bonds as a refuge and the 10-year Treasury yield fell about 10 basis points to 5.09 percent, its biggest one-day drop in over a year. Federal Reserve futures put roughly a one-in-three chance on a rate rise at the 27 to 28 October meeting and a very high chance of at least one by December.
Energy
December Brent settled on Thursday at 105.92 dollars a barrel, down 14 cents from Wednesday's 106.06, while November West Texas Intermediate settled at 97.97 dollars, up 2.88 dollars from 95.09 after a large US crude stock draw. The International Energy Agency agreed to speed up the release of the rest of its emergency stocks, including more than 20 million barrels of diesel.
Iran
Two cargo ships have now sunk near the Strait of Hormuz, the Cape Dao and the GFS Pride, and a second tanker caught fire in the Gulf of Oman on Thursday, according to Iranian state media. Trump has said the US will not attack Iran before the 3 November midterm elections, and indirect talks through Pakistan and Qatar are continuing.
Saudi Arabia and Yemen
The Houthis said they fired another ballistic missile at Riyadh's King Khalid airport on Thursday, and Saudi forces intercepted a missile over the capital earlier in the day, with falling shrapnel injuring one resident. The Saudi-led coalition says the airport has been targeted 20 times since 1 October, with 17 people killed and more than 330 injured in those attacks.
Ukraine
Ukraine ended its three-day pause on strikes against Russian energy sites and hit a refinery in Russia's Ryazan region on Thursday, after Russia fired more than 850 drones overnight and kept attacking Ukrainian power facilities despite Trump's energy ceasefire call. President Zelensky said he feels betrayed by Trump, and Russian drones hit Kyiv's power network and other regions again overnight into Friday.
European Union
EU leaders failed to agree on the 2028 to 2034 EU budget at Thursday's summit in Brussels, and the European Council President will now take over the talks ahead of a special summit on 26 November. The leaders adopted conclusions on competitiveness, energy costs and enlargement on Thursday, and today's second day covers Ukraine, defence, the Middle East and migration.
AI financing
Credit stress around CoreWeave, the debt-funded AI cloud company, pushed the cost of insuring its debt to a record, and its shares closed up 5.9 percent at 69.69 dollars on Thursday after a 15.5 percent fall the day before. Anthropic is reportedly preparing to file publicly for a stock market listing around 26 October, and investors are said to be trimming their valuation hopes.
AI companies
TSMC, the world's largest contract chipmaker, reported third-quarter revenue of 46.72 billion dollars, above its own forecast range, and guided fourth-quarter revenue to 52.3 to 54.3 billion dollars. Its chief executive said AI demand shows no sign of slowing, and the company kept its 2026 spending plan at 60 to 64 billion dollars.
Nvidia
Nvidia shares fell 1.84 percent to 217.23 dollars on Thursday as the credit stress at its customer CoreWeave weighed on AI stocks, although Chief Executive Jensen Huang said no financial backstop for CoreWeave is needed. The company's RTX Spark laptops go on sale today, and its GTC Berlin conference opens on Tuesday 20 October with Huang's keynote on Wednesday 21 October.
AI models
Meta released Muse Spark Open, an open-weight model with 816 billion parameters whose weights anyone can download, and Apple said developers can run it locally on pairs of its high-end Mac Studio computers. Meta says it is not its most capable model, which remains the proprietary Muse Wonder.
Tesla
Tesla showed its new Roadster in Texas on Thursday evening, with a thruster-assisted demonstration, and Tesla says production will start in 2028, with no price given. The company reports third-quarter earnings on Wednesday 21 October after the US close.
SpaceX
A Falcon 9 launched the USSF-481 national security mission from California at 02:01 Paris time on Friday and the booster landed on a drone ship, taking SpaceX to 118 Falcon launches this year. The CRS-35 cargo Dragon docked with the International Space Station on Thursday afternoon and stays until mid-November.
Palantir
Spain's Palantir defence contract will reportedly expire at the end of 2026 and not be renewed, according to a single report in El País, and the contract value has not been disclosed. The report adds to political pressure on Palantir in Britain, and its third-quarter results are due on Monday 2 November.
Indonesia
Indonesia's Jakarta Composite index plunged about 11 percent on Friday after MSCI, the index provider, opened a consultation on moving Indonesia from emerging-market to standalone-market status over ownership transparency and suspected coordinated trading. MSCI expects a final decision by 30 June 2027.
Brazil
Datafolha's new runoff poll has Flávio Bolsonaro on 47 percent and President Lula on 45 percent, or 51 to 49 among valid votes, which is a statistical tie within the 2-point margin of error. The runoff is on Sunday 25 October.
Israel and Lebanon
Polls for Israel's election on Tuesday 27 October still show neither Benjamin Netanyahu's bloc nor Gadi Eisenkot's bloc reaching the 61 seats needed, with Eisenkot's party ahead of Likud in most surveys. Separately, US Secretary of State Marco Rubio pressed Lebanon not to return seized explosives to Hezbollah as Israel and Lebanon discuss a US-backed plan for the Lebanese army to take over parts of the south.
