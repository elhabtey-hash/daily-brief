Cover

Wednesday 14 October 2026 · Europe/Paris, morning · window Tuesday 13 morning to Wednesday 14 morning

Saudi Arabia (lead)
A Houthi ballistic missile hit an Aramco administrative building in Dhahran on Tuesday, causing a limited fire, and falling debris over Riyadh wounded four people, after the Saudi-led coalition struck Houthi fuel depots near Sanaa. The strike moves the Yemen war from airports towards Saudi oil infrastructure.
Iran
Two more ships, a tanker and a container ship, were hit by unknown projectiles north of Oman's Musandam peninsula on Tuesday, and Iranian officials told Reuters they have changed tactics to hit more ships before the US midterms. At least 12 tankers have been attacked around the Strait of Hormuz in the past week.
Energy
December Brent settled on Tuesday at 105.69 dollars a barrel, down 96 cents, as a US-Russia diesel deal eased supply fears, while November West Texas Intermediate rose 40 cents to 94.14 dollars, its highest close since 24 September. Gulf of Mexico oil still shut in after Hurricane Isaias fell to about 16 percent of output.
Ukraine
Russia hit no Ukrainian energy sites overnight into Tuesday for the first time since Trump announced an energy ceasefire, but it pounded cities and rail links, killing at least four in Kharkiv. The Kremlin still says only that contacts are continuing, and Zelensky says Ukraine has paused refinery strikes for two days.
China
China's exports jumped 36.3 percent in September from a year earlier and imports rose 31 percent, leaving a monthly surplus of 133.5 billion dollars. The surplus for the first nine months reached 938 billion dollars, putting a trillion-dollar year in sight and adding to pressure on Beijing from trading partners.
US markets
Big banks opened earnings season with mixed results, as JPMorgan's profit of 5.57 dollars a share missed forecasts while Goldman Sachs and Citigroup beat, and the S&P 500 closed flat at 7,805.45. September consumer prices are due today at 14:30 Paris, with forecasters expecting energy to lift yearly inflation to about 3.6 percent.
Apple
Apple launched HomeView, its first smart-home display, at 299 dollars with deliveries from Friday 13 November, plus a 149-dollar HomePod mini 2 arriving Friday 30 October and a new Apple TV 4K. HomeView will not be sold in the European Union because Apple's new Siri AI is not available there.
AI
Google made its Gemini 4 Argon model available to everyone on Tuesday, through its app, Search and developer interface, after limiting it to vetted cyber defenders since 30 September. It arrives a day after Meta's Muse Wonder, so the two biggest consumer AI platforms launched new top models in the same week.
AI companies
ASML, the only maker of the most advanced chipmaking machines, reported record third-quarter sales of 11.66 billion euros and guided fourth-quarter sales to 14.2 to 15.2 billion euros. That points to strong orders for AI and memory chip factories, with TSMC reporting on Thursday at 08:00 Paris.
Nvidia
Jensen Huang said in Paris that Nvidia will announce about 2 billion dollars of new European investments within a week, according to Bloomberg, likely around its GTC Berlin conference from Tuesday 20 October. Nvidia shares rose 0.97 percent to 228.52 dollars, ending a four-day slide.
Ethiopia
Ethiopian federal troops reached Zalambessa on the Eritrean border, stoking fears of a war between the two countries, and the UN Security Council discussed the tensions at the request of the United States. Eritrea denies its troops are inside Ethiopia.
Brazil
New polls put Flávio Bolsonaro narrowly ahead of President Lula for the runoff on Sunday 25 October, at 52 to 48 percent of valid votes in BTG/Nexus and 51 to 49 in PoderData. Both leads are within the margin of error.
Tesla
Tesla will livestream its long-delayed new Roadster from near Waco at 02:55 Paris on Friday 16 October, with no price or production date known. It also paused Canadian orders for its cheapest Model 3 until about March 2027, blaming the quota on China-built electric cars.
SpaceX
A Falcon 9 launched the CRS-35 cargo Dragon to the International Space Station on Tuesday at 12:33 Paris, SpaceX's 117th Falcon flight of 2026. Docking with the station is targeted for Thursday at about 17:00 Paris.
